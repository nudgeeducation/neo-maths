NEO Mathematics: 2D and 3D Shapes

Analysing 2D and 3D Properties

Driving Question: What stays true about a shape, however we turn or draw it?

Lesson type: Property investigation — describing 2D shapes and 3D solids by what is countable and certain.

Stepping Stone: analyse the properties of 2D and 3D shapes; describe a solid by its faces, edges and vertices, and recognise the structure a family of solids shares.

🌱 Open Learner Guide 📘 Open Supporting Adult Guidance
🌿 Connection — How is a prism built, and what does its base decide?

🔤 New words: faces, edges and vertices

Every solid can be described with three words:

Face — a flat surface of the solid.
Edge — a straight line where two faces meet.
Vertex — a corner where edges meet. (More than one are called vertices.)

Keep these three words handy — you will count them for every solid in this unit.

What to do: choose a base for the prism, then predict how many faces, edges and vertices it will have before you reveal the counts.

What you are trying to notice: a prism is built from its base — change the base and the whole structure changes with it in a predictable way.

📐 Prism Family Pattern Explorer

Change the base polygon. The picture and the counts update together. Try to see the rule that links the base to the faces, edges and vertices.

Carry this forward: the base decides everything about a prism. Next you will see that same base sweeping through space to build the solid.

🚶 Movement — How does a flat shape become a solid?

What to do: use the slider to sweep the base polygon through space. Watch a flat shape turn into a solid prism.

What you are trying to notice: a prism is a base carried a certain distance — the flat cross-section is the same all the way along.

Carry this forward: because the cross-section stays the same all the way along, later we will measure a prism by its cross-section — for its surface and its volume.

🪞 Reflection — Can you become a shape detective?

What to do: read the clues one at a time. Decide which solid fits — or reveal another clue first. Each clue is a property, so you are reasoning, not guessing.

What you are trying to notice: a few well-chosen properties are enough to identify a solid exactly.

🔎 Shape Detective

Which solid is it?

Carry this forward: the properties you used — faces, edges, vertices, curved or flat — are exactly the language you will use to describe every solid in this unit.

🎨 Creativity — Can you design a solid from a set of faces?

What to do: choose a set of flat faces and see which solid they would make. Then invent your own on the Scratchpad.

What you are trying to notice: a solid is decided by which faces meet, and how many.

🌙 Rest — A pause to let the structure settle

Naming what stays the same is quiet, patient work. Before moving on, take a slow breath and let one idea settle.

Notice one thing: of everything you have looked at, which single property did you find most useful for telling two solids apart? There is no right answer — just the one that helped you.

You can jot it on the Scratchpad, say it aloud, or simply hold it in mind. You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — What is this lesson feeding?

Mode: intellectual. This lesson does not sit in a food or body context, and we will not pretend it does. What it feeds is your spatial reasoning — the ability to see structure, to describe it precisely, and to trust a property you can count.

Carry one idea forward: "A shape's properties stay true however I turn or draw it." That steadiness is what makes measurement possible later in the unit.

Which felt stronger for you today — seeing the structure, or describing it in words? Noticing that helps you choose how to show your thinking next time.

🛠️ Final Task — Evidence for today

Your task: describe three different solids by their properties, and state the rule linking a prism's base to its faces, edges and vertices.

  • For three solids of your choice, record: name, number of faces, edges and vertices, and whether any surface is curved.
  • Complete this sentence in your own words: "A prism with an n-sided base always has ___ faces, ___ edges and ___ vertices, because…"
  • Choose one solid and write three clues that would let a shape detective identify it.

Submission choices: an annotated Scratchpad image, a short written table, a labelled poster, or a spoken/video walkthrough with a labelled sketch.
