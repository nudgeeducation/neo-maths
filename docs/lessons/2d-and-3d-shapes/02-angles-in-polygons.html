Angles in Polygons

Driving Question: What decides the angles inside a polygon — and what stays the same, however many sides it has?

Lesson type: Angle investigation — discovering how a polygon's angles are decided by its number of sides.

Stepping Stone: use polygon angles — find the interior-angle sum and each interior and exterior angle of a polygon, and know that the exterior angles of any polygon add to 360°.

🌿 Connection — What decides the angles inside a polygon?

What to do: set the number of sides, then draw the triangulation lines from one corner and count the triangles. Fill in each row of the table, and look for the pattern before you write the formula.

What you are trying to notice: every polygon splits into triangles — always two fewer than its number of sides — and each triangle holds 180°. So the angle sum is fixed by the number of sides.

🚶 Movement — What happens if you walk all the way around?

What to do: choose a polygon, then step the walker around it one corner at a time. At each corner it turns by the exterior angle. Watch the total turn add up.

What you are trying to notice: however many sides a polygon has, walking once around it turns you through exactly one full turn — 360°.

🪞 Reflection — True or false, and how do you know?

What to do: decide whether each statement is true or false, then reveal the reasoning. You are being asked to justify, not just answer.

What you are trying to notice: a few secure facts — the triangle split, and the 360° full turn — let you judge any claim about polygon angles.

🎨 Creativity — Which regular polygons tessellate, and why?

What to do: drag a shape onto the centre point — or tap a tile — to place copies around the point. Find how many of each regular polygon fit, which ones close the point exactly, and record what you notice.

What you are trying to notice: a regular polygon (equal sides and equal angles) tessellates — tiles a flat surface with no gaps — only if its interior angle divides exactly into 360°.

Note: these are all regular polygons. Some irregular polygons tessellate too (every triangle and every quadrilateral does), but a regular polygon only tessellates when its angle divides exactly into 360°.

So what makes a shape tessellate? Look back at the shapes that closed the point exactly. What do their interior angles have in common? Decide first, then reveal.

Carry this forward: only the triangle, the square and the hexagon fill a flat point exactly. If we let the shapes fold up out of the flat — leaving a small gap at each point — those gaps become the corners of a 3D solid. That is exactly how the Platonic solids in Lesson 6 are built.

🌙 Rest — A pause to let the pattern settle

You have found two things that stay fixed: a polygon's interior angles are decided by its number of sides, and its exterior angles always add to a full turn. That is quiet, steadying work.

Notice one thing: which felt more surprising to you — that the interior angles are fixed by the sides, or that the exterior angles are always 360°? There is no right answer; just the one that struck you.

You can jot it on the Scratchpad, say it aloud, or simply hold it in mind. You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — Where does this show up in the real world?

Mode: contextual. This lesson reaches something you can hold: the honeycomb. Bees store their honey in cells that are almost perfectly hexagonal.

Why hexagons? As you saw in Corner-Fit, three regular hexagons meet exactly around a point (3 × 120° = 360°), so they tile with no gaps and no wasted space. Of the shapes that tile — triangle, square, hexagon — the hexagon encloses the most space for the least wall. For a bee, that means the most honey stored for the least wax made.

The angle fact you discovered today is the reason a honeycomb looks the way it does. Where else might you spot shapes chosen because their angles fit — floor tiles, paving, a football?

🛠️ Final Task — Evidence for today

Your task: work out the angles of some polygons, and explain the two facts that make it possible.

  • For three regular polygons of your choice, record: name, number of sides, the sum of the interior angles, each interior angle, and each exterior angle.
  • Complete this sentence in your own words: "The interior angles of a polygon with n sides add up to ___, because…"
  • Explain, in your own words, why the exterior angles of every polygon add up to 360°.
