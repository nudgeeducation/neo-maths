NEO Mathematics: Accuracy, Powers and Scale

Using Index Laws

Driving Question: How can patterns help us simplify powers without expanding every factor?

Lesson type: Index Law Detective, Factor Strips working builder and supported practice.

Stepping Stone: use the multiplication, division and power-of-a-power index laws and explain why they work, including the zero-index pattern.

NEO Cornerstones: Connection · Movement · Reflection · Creativity · Rest · Nutrition

45-minute shape: Connect 8 · Move 18 · Practise 10 · Reflect/Create 5 · Rest/Nourish 2 · Evidence 2

🌱 Open Learner Guide 📘 Open Supporting Adult Guidance
🌿 Connection — Index Law Detective

What to do: inspect the three claims. Choose the one that correctly simplifies the expression.

What you are trying to notice: when multiplying powers with the same base, the repeated factors join together.

Expression

23 × 24

Carry this idea forward: check the base first; then look at what happens to the repeated factors.
🚶 Movement — Factor Strips: growing the working

What to do: work through one expression at a time. Tap each step in order — expand the powers into strips of factors, act on the strips, then count what you are left with. Every step writes a line of your working, with its reason beside it.

What you are trying to notice: each index law is a short way of describing what happens to the repeated factors. The operation decides what happens; counting decides the exponent.

Every factor has cancelled. Nothing is left to multiply.

You have divided a number by itself. What is the value of 90?

My working
Carry this idea forward: an index law is a shortcut for counting factors. When you are not sure which law applies, expand and count — the count will tell you.
🪞 Reflection — Which law, and why?

What to do: choose the law needed for (x3)5, then explain what the two exponents mean.

This law makes sense because...

What you are trying to notice: choosing a law comes before performing it.

🎨 Creativity — Build an Index Puzzle

What to do: create three different expressions that all simplify to the same target power, and write the chain of working for each.

  1. Choose a target, such as q12.
  2. Create one multiplication expression.
  3. Create one division expression.
  4. Create one power-of-a-power expression.
  5. Write each one as a chain of lines, with a reason beside every line.
  6. Explain why every expression reaches the target.
🌙 Rest — What is the calm three-step route?

Look away from the screen, then retrieve this route in your own words:

same base → what happens to the factors → count what is left

If the route ever deserts you, expand and count. The count is always there.

🥣 Nutrition — What is this lesson nourishing?

This lesson uses intellectual nourishment. It nourishes pattern recognition: replacing a long string of repeated factors with a compact statement that still preserves the mathematical structure.

🌱 Structure
I check whether the bases match.
🌱 Reasoning
I connect each law to repeated factors.
🌱 Efficiency
I simplify without expanding every factor.
🌱 Precision
I change the exponent, not the base.
🏠 Final Task — Become an Index Law Designer

Design and explain an index-law pathway

What to do: create a four-step pathway that uses multiplication, division and a power of a power. For each step:

  • record the original expression;
  • identify the common base;
  • write the working as a chain of lines, each equal to the line above;
  • give a reason beside every line;
  • write the simplified power; and
  • justify one step by counting repeated factors.
