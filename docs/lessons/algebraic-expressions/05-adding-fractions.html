Adding and subtracting algebraic fractions

Driving Question: How do fractions with letters share a denominator?

🌿 Connection — Fractions you already know

🔤 The idea for this lesson

To add 2/3 and 1/4, rewrite both over a common denominator, 12: 8/12 + 3/12 = 11/12. You multiply each numerator and denominator by the same number — a form of 1 — so neither fraction changes value. Algebraic fractions work in exactly the same way; the denominators may just contain letters.

What to do: add and subtract these fractions.

What you are trying to notice: only the numerators are added. The common denominator stays.

➕ Warm up

Carry this forward: same denominator first; then add or subtract the numerators.

🚶 Movement — The common-denominator builder

What to do: Step 1: choose what to multiply each fraction's numerator and denominator by, so both denominators become the same. The cards rewrite themselves. Step 2: combine the numerators over the common denominator. Step 3: write the answer in its simplest form. Then see it checked with numbers.

What you are trying to notice: each fraction is multiplied by a form of 1, so its value never changes. When you subtract, the whole second numerator is subtracted — every sign inside it changes.

Carry this forward: common denominator → combine numerators (brackets when subtracting) → simplify → check with a number.

🪞 Reflection — Spot the slip

What to do: each calculation has been worked out line by line. Find the line with the slip — or decide that every line is correct.

What you are trying to notice: the usual slips are adding the denominators, forgetting to multiply a numerator, and a sign error when subtracting.

🔍 Where does it go wrong?

Carry this forward: check each line, not just the answer — and test the answer with a number.

🎨 Creativity — Split it apart

What to do: now run the method backwards. Find the numbers A and B so that the two fractions add up to the one on the right — for every value of x.

What you are trying to notice: combine the left side over the common denominator, then match its numerator with the right side's numerator.

🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

Two fractions that could not be added, made able to — without changing either one. Take a slow breath.

Notice one thing: multiplying by (x + 1)/(x + 1) looks like a big change. It is the same as multiplying by 1.

You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: intellectual. The mathematics is the nourishment, so we name honestly what the learning feeds.

What it feeds: today feeds transfer: taking a method you learned with numbers and trusting it with letters. Methods that are understood, not just memorised, travel well — into new topics, and into problems you have never seen.

Carry one idea forward: when algebra looks hard, ask what you would do with numbers.

🛠️ Final Task — One common denominator

Your task: show that you can add and subtract algebraic fractions, and check your answers.

  • Work out x + 23 + x − 14 and x + 52 − x − 36, in their simplest form.
  • Work out 4x + 1x − 2 and 2x + 1 − 1x − 1.
  • Work out 1x − 4 + 3x² − 16, using the lowest common denominator.
  • Check one of your answers by substituting a value of x.
  • stretch Explain why a/b + c/d = (ad + bc)/bd, and why (a + c)/(b + d) is wrong.

Submission choices: an annotated Scratchpad image, a photographed page, a short slideshow, or a spoken/video walkthrough.
