NEO Mathematics: Algebraic Expressions and Identities

Changing the subject, harder cases

Driving Question: What if the subject appears twice, or as a square?

Lesson type: Core — rearranging when one inverse operation is not enough.

Stepping Stone: change the subject of a formula where the subject appears twice — collect its terms, factorise it out, divide; change the subject where it appears as a square or under a square root; state the conditions a rearrangement creates; and check a rearrangement by substituting values.

You will need: changing the subject when it appears once, and taking out a common factor — and the optional ✏️ Scratchpad. A calculator is useful for checking, not needed for rearranging.

🌿 Connection — Once, then twice

🔤 The idea for this lesson

To make x the subject of y = 3x + 2, undo each step on both sides: x = (y − 2)/3. But in xy + 4 = 3x, the x appears twice, and no single "undo" can reach it. The new move: gather all the x terms on one side, and factorise — so x appears only once.

What to do: make the letter in brackets the subject. Type your answer like x = (y − 2)/3.

What you are trying to notice: when the subject appears once, undoing works directly.

↩️ Warm up

Carry this forward: do the same to both sides; undo in reverse order.

🚶 Movement — The line-by-line balance

What to do: rearrange in your own steps. Type each new line of working (like xy − 3x = −4) and add it. Every line is checked: if it says something different from the formula you started with, you will know straight away. Finish with a line that starts with the subject, like x = …. Use Hint whenever you want the next move.

What you are trying to notice: once the subject's terms are all on one side, factorising leaves the subject just once — and then you can divide.

stretch Why do rearrangements come with conditions? A pattern suggests — a proof convinces.

Carry this forward: collect the subject's terms → factorise → divide (or square, or square root) → check with numbers.

🪞 Reflection — Spot the faulty rearrangement

What to do: each rearrangement is set out line by line. Find the line with the slip — or decide every line is correct.

What you are trying to notice: the usual slips are dividing only part of a side, a sign error when moving a term, and halving instead of square rooting.

🎨 Creativity — Turn it inside out

What to do: choose a and b to build a formula y = (x + a)/(x + b). Make x the subject. Some of these formulas turn inside out and come back the same shape — find them all.

What you are trying to notice: a formula is self-inverse when making x the subject gives the same formula with x and y swapped.

🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

A letter tangled up in two places, gathered together and set free. Take a slow breath.

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: intellectual. The mathematics is the nourishment, so we name honestly what the learning feeds.

What it feeds: today feeds persistence with structure. When a problem resists the usual move, look at its shape: gather what belongs together, and a new move appears. That is true of formulas — and of many tangled situations.

Carry one idea forward: if something appears in two places, bring it together first.

🛠️ Final Task — Untangle the subject

Your task: show that you can rearrange harder formulas, state their conditions and check them.

  • Make x the subject of 6x − a = 2x + b, and of ax + 3 = bx. Show every line.
  • Make x the subject of y = x + 1x − 4. Which value can y never take, and why?
  • Make r the subject of V = πr²h, and explain why you take only the positive root.
  • Check one of your answers by substituting numbers.
  • stretch Make l the subject of T = 2π√(l/g).

Submission choices: an annotated Scratchpad image, a photographed page, a short slideshow, or a spoken/video walkthrough.
