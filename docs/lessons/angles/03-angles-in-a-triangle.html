NEO Mathematics: Angles Tell Us Why

Angles in a triangle

Driving Question: Does every triangle hide a straight line?

Lesson type: Core — the three angles of any triangle fit together on a straight line, so they add up to 180°.

Stepping Stone: derive and use the angle sum of a triangle, and the angle facts of isosceles, equilateral and right-angled triangles.

You will need: angles on a straight line (Lesson 2), and the optional ✏️ Scratchpad. No calculator needed.

🌿 Connection — The Turning Triangles

🔤 The idea for this lesson

Turn a copy of a triangle half a turn about the middle of one side, and two of its corners swap places. Do this about the middles of the two sides that meet at C, and angles a and b land at C, either side of angle c. The three angles fit together exactly on a straight line, so for every triangle, a + b + c = 180°. Special triangles have extra facts: an isosceles triangle has two equal sides and two equal base angles; an equilateral triangle has three equal sides and three angles of 60°.

What to do: drag the yellow corners to make any triangle (A and B slide along the base). Then press Turn the triangles and watch where angles a and b go. Change the triangle and try again.

What you are trying to notice: does every triangle, whatever its shape, hide a straight line?

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: intellectual. The mathematics is the nourishment, so we name honestly what the learning feeds.

What it feeds: today feeds certainty. Measuring a few triangles suggests 180°; the turning triangles show why it must be true for every triangle, including ones nobody has drawn yet.

Carry one idea forward: every triangle hides a straight line.

🛠️ Final Task — The triangle file

Your task: make a short file of triangle facts.

  • Show why the angles of a triangle add up to 180°: tear the corners off a paper triangle, or draw the turning triangles.
  • Draw an isosceles, an equilateral and a right-angled triangle, marking equal sides and right angles, and write each one's angle facts.
  • Make up two missing-angle puzzles and solve them, giving the reason for each step.
  • stretch Explain why a triangle can never have two obtuse angles.

Submission choices: an annotated Scratchpad image, a photographed page, a short slideshow, or a spoken or video walkthrough.
