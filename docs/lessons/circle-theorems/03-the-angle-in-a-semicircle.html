NEO Mathematics: Circle Theorems

The angle in a semicircle

Driving Question: What is special about an angle drawn on a diameter?

Lesson type: Core — the first and cleanest consequence of the keystone: the special case where the two points are ends of a diameter.

Stepping Stone: discover, and then justify, that the angle in a semicircle is 90° — every triangle drawn on a diameter has a right angle at the third vertex — and use it (with the reason) to find missing angles.

You will need: the angle at the centre theorem (Lesson 2), that a diameter passes through the centre, that angles on a straight line sum to 180°, and that angles in a triangle sum to 180°.

🌿 Connection — An angle drawn on a diameter

🔤 The idea for this lesson

Last lesson gave us the keystone: the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference on the same arc. Now try the most special arc of all. Let the two points A and B be the ends of a diameter — a chord straight through the centre O. Then the "arc" AB is a whole semicircle, and the angle at the centre, ∠AOB, is a straight line: 180°. Halve it, and the angle at the circumference ∠APB must be 90°. So every triangle you draw on a diameter has a right angle at the third corner — a fact worth its own lesson.

What to do: A and B are the ends of a diameter. Drag P around the semicircle and watch the angle ∠APB at P.

What you are trying to notice: the diameter is a straight line through O, so the "angle at the centre" is 180°. The angle at P is half of that — and it never moves off 90°.

🔦 The angle on a diameter

Carry this forward: a diameter subtends a straight angle (180°) at the centre, so it subtends a right angle (90°) at the circumference.

🚶 Movement — The right angle, and the other two

What to do: triangle APB sits on a diameter AB, so the angle at P is 90°. That leaves 90° to share between the other two angles. For each diagram, work out the marked angle and type it in (degrees), and be ready to give the reason.

What you are trying to notice: once you know ∠APB = 90° (angle in a semicircle), the two acute angles of the triangle must add to 90° — so if you know one, you subtract from 90° to get the other.

🧮 Find the missing angle

Carry this forward: angle in a semicircle = 90°; the other two angles of the triangle then add to 90°.

🪞 Reflection — The right angle that will not move keystone case

What to do: A and B are the ends of a diameter. Drag P slowly around the whole semicircle, keeping your eye on ∠APB and the small square at P. Then press Why 90°?.

What you are trying to notice: the right angle at P never changes — a tall thin triangle, a short wide one, it is always exactly 90°. And you can see why: the diameter makes a 180° angle at the centre, and the angle at the circumference is always half of it. This is the angle in a semicircle theorem.

🔬 The Circle Theorem Lab — diameter case

Carry this forward: the angle in a semicircle is a right angle (90°) — because a diameter subtends 180° at the centre, and the circumference angle is half of it.

🎨 Creativity — Why is it a right angle? stretch

What to do: press the steps to build the proof straight from last lesson's keystone. It takes just three lines.

What you are trying to notice: the whole proof is the angle-at-the-centre theorem applied to the most special arc. The diameter forces the centre angle to be a straight line, and half of a straight line is a right angle.

🧩 Build the proof

Another way to see it: join OP. Then OA = OB = OP are radii, so triangles OAP and OBP are isosceles with base angles a and b. The angles of triangle APB are a, b and ∠APB, and ∠APB = a + b. Since they sum to 180°, we get a + b + (a + b) = 180°, so a + b = 90° — the right angle again.

The useful converse: it also works backwards — if ∠APB = 90°, then P must lie on the circle with AB as diameter. That is how you can build a right angle, or find the centre of a circle through a right-angled corner.

🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

A right angle you cannot shake loose, hidden inside every diameter. Notice how much of this lesson was really last lesson, viewed from a new angle — one strong idea quietly doing more work. Take a breath before moving on.

Notice one thing: did seeing the semicircle right angle as "just the 180° case" of the keystone make it feel obvious, or surprising? Both reactions are worth having.

You can jot it on the Scratchpad, say it aloud, or simply hold it in mind. You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: intellectual. We name honestly what the learning feeds.

What it feeds: today feeds the pleasure of a special case — how a general truth, aimed at exactly the right target, produces something clean and memorable. The right angle in a semicircle (Thales' theorem, one of the oldest results in geometry) is the sort of fact that engineers and builders reach for: a way to test or make a right angle using nothing but a circle. It also feeds the habit of asking "what happens in the extreme case?" — pushing an idea to its limit is where much of mathematics is discovered.

Carry one idea forward: when you meet a general rule, try its most special case — it often hands you a new theorem for free.

🛠️ Final Task — Evidence for today

Your task: show you can state, use and justify the angle in a semicircle.

  • Draw a circle with a diameter AB, mark a point P on the circle, and join PA and PB. State that ∠APB = 90° and give the reason (angle in a semicircle).
  • Given one acute angle of a triangle drawn on a diameter, find the other, writing the reason at each step (angle in a semicircle, then angles in a triangle).
  • Explain in your own words why the angle is 90° — using the angle at the centre (a diameter gives 180° at O, halved).
  • stretch State and use the converse: if a triangle has a right angle, its hypotenuse is a diameter of the circle through all three vertices.

Submission choices: an annotated Scratchpad image, a photographed hand-drawn diagram, a triangle built and measured on a diameter in the Geometry Studio (Measure → Angle), a written proof, or a spoken/video explanation.
