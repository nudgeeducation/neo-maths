NEO Mathematics: Circle Theorems

Cyclic quadrilaterals

Driving Question: Why do opposite corners of a circle-quad always add to 180°?

Lesson type: Core — the fourth angle-in-a-circle theorem, completing the set. It marks the mid-unit checkpoint before tangents and chords.

Stepping Stone: discover, and then justify, that opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add to 180° — and use it (with the reason) to find missing angles.

You will need: the angle at the centre theorem (Lesson 2), that angles at a point add to 360°, and that the angles of a quadrilateral add to 360°. A cyclic quadrilateral has all four vertices on the circle.

🌿 Connection — Four corners on a circle

🔤 The idea for this lesson

A cyclic quadrilateral is a four-sided shape whose four corners all lie on a circle. Draw one and measure its angles, and something surprising shows up: the two pairs of opposite corners each add to exactly 180°. ∠A + ∠C = 180° and ∠B + ∠D = 180° — the opposite angles are supplementary. Like the last two theorems, it comes straight from the keystone: opposite corners look across the two arcs of a diagonal, and those two arcs make a full turn of 360° at the centre, so the two angles are half of that — 180°.

What to do: the four vertices A, B, C, D all sit on the circle. Drag any vertex around and watch the four angles — and especially the two opposite-pair sums.

What you are trying to notice: whatever shape you drag it into, ∠A + ∠C stays 180°, and ∠B + ∠D stays 180°. The individual angles change; the opposite sums do not.

Carry this forward: in a cyclic quadrilateral, opposite angles add to 180° (they are supplementary).

🚶 Movement — Find the opposite angle

What to do: ABCD is a cyclic quadrilateral. One angle is given — find the angle opposite it and type it in (degrees), with the reason.

What you are trying to notice: opposite angles add to 180°, so the missing one is simply 180° minus the given one. The skill is spotting which angle is opposite (across the shape, not next to it) and naming the theorem.

🧮 Find the opposite angle

Carry this forward: opposite angle = 180° − the given angle. Always name the reason.

🪞 Reflection — The sum that will not budge keystone case

What to do: all four vertices are draggable. Reshape the quadrilateral however you like — tall, wide, lopsided — watching the two opposite-pair sums. Then press Why 180°?.

What you are trying to notice: the four angles change wildly as you drag, but ∠A + ∠C and ∠B + ∠D are always 180°. And the reason is the keystone again: opposite angles are each half of the two central angles that fill a whole 360° turn.

Carry this forward: opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add to 180° — because each pair is half of a full 360° turn at the centre.

🎨 Creativity — Why do they add to 180°? stretch

What to do: press the steps to build the proof — the keystone one last time.

What you are trying to notice: ∠A and ∠C look across the two arcs on either side of the diagonal BD. The central angles for those two arcs together make a full turn (360°), and each corner angle is half its own central angle — so the two corner angles add to half of 360°, which is 180°.

🧩 Build the proof

Connecting back: this is the "opposite side of the chord" case teased last lesson. Same segment → equal angles; opposite segments → angles that add to 180°.

🌙 Rest — A checkpoint pause

Take a proper pause here — this is the mid-unit checkpoint. You now hold all four of the angle-in-a-circle theorems: the angle at the centre (double), the angle in a semicircle (90°), angles in the same segment (equal), and now the cyclic quadrilateral (opposite angles 180°). Remarkably, the last three all grew from the first. The unit now turns to a new family — lines that touch the circle rather than cross it.

Notice one thing: which of the four theorems feels most secure, and which would you most like to revisit? Naming that is exactly the kind of self-check a strong mathematician does at a milestone.

You can jot it on the Scratchpad, say it aloud, or simply hold it in mind. You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: intellectual. We name honestly what the learning feeds.

What it feeds: today feeds a sense of completeness — the quiet satisfaction of a set of ideas closing into a whole. Four theorems, one root; a small, complete theory you can hold in your head. That is what mathematicians chase: not a heap of facts, but a structure where each piece explains the next. Cyclic quadrilaterals also turn up wherever things must fit exactly on a circle — gear teeth, clock faces, the geometry of a camera's view — so the pattern earns its keep well beyond the exam.

Carry one idea forward: when a set of results all trace back to one root, you understand them far better than by memorising each alone.

🛠️ Final Task — Evidence for today

Your task: show you can state, use and justify the cyclic-quadrilateral rule.

  • Draw a circle with four points A, B, C, D on it, join them into a quadrilateral, and show that ∠A + ∠C = 180° and ∠B + ∠D = 180°, giving the reason.
  • Use the theorem to find missing angles in a cyclic quadrilateral, writing the reason at each step.
  • Explain in your own words why opposite angles add to 180° — using the angle at the centre and the full 360° turn.
  • stretch The exterior angle of a cyclic quadrilateral equals the opposite interior angle. Explain why, using the 180° rule and angles on a straight line.

Submission choices: an annotated Scratchpad image, a photographed hand-drawn diagram, a quadrilateral measured in the Geometry Studio (Measure → Angle), a written proof, or a spoken/video explanation.
