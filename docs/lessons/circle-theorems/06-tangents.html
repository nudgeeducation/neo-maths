NEO Mathematics: Circle Theorems

Tangents to a circle

Driving Question: How does a line that only touches a circle behave?

Lesson type: Core — the opening lesson of the second family: lines that touch the circle rather than cross it.

Stepping Stone: discover, and then justify, that a tangent meets the radius at 90° at the point of contact, and that the two tangents from an external point are equal — and use both (with reasons) to find angles and lengths.

You will need: what a tangent is (Lesson 1), that a radius joins the centre to the circle, congruent triangles (RHS), and that angles in a triangle sum to 180°.

🌿 Connection — A line that just touches

🔤 The idea for this lesson

Every line so far has crossed the circle (a chord) or missed it. A tangent is the in-between case: it touches the circle at exactly one point, the point of contact. Tangents behave with a beautiful simplicity, captured in two facts. First, a tangent always meets the radius drawn to the point of contact at exactly 90°. Second, if you stand at a point P outside the circle you can draw two tangents, and they are always equal in length — the figure OAPB they make with the two radii is a kite. Both facts come from the humblest source again: the two radii are equal.

What to do: P is a point outside the circle, with its two tangents touching at A and B, and radii OA, OB drawn. Drag P around and watch the right angles and the two tangent lengths.

What you are trying to notice: the little squares at A and B never open up — the tangent always meets the radius at 90°. And the two tangent lengths PA and PB stay exactly equal, wherever P goes.

🔦 The tangent kite

Carry this forward: a tangent meets the radius at 90°, and the two tangents from a point are equal.

🚶 Movement — Use the two tangent facts

What to do: each diagram uses one of the two facts — the 90° tangent–radius angle, or the equal tangent lengths (and the kite they make). Work out the marked value and type it in, with the reason.

What you are trying to notice: spotting which fact a diagram needs is the skill. A radius to a point of contact means a right angle is hiding there; two tangents from one point means two equal lengths — and the kite OAPB means ∠AOB and ∠APB add to 180°.

🧮 Find the marked value

Carry this forward: tangent ⟂ radius (90°); equal tangents; and in the kite, ∠AOB + ∠APB = 180°.

🪞 Reflection — Two facts that hold as you drag keystone case

What to do: the external point P is fully draggable — move it near the circle, far away, all the way round. Keep your eye on the two right-angle squares and the two length labels. Then press Why?.

What you are trying to notice: two invariants at once. The tangent–radius angle is always 90°, and the two tangent lengths are always equal. The reason for both is a single pair of congruent right-angled triangles.

🔬 The tangent-kite lab

Carry this forward: a tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact, and the two tangents from an external point are equal — two congruent right triangles make both true.

🎨 Creativity — Why are the two tangents equal? stretch

What to do: press the steps to build the proof. It rests on congruent triangles (RHS) — right angle, hypotenuse, side.

What you are trying to notice: the two triangles OAP and OBP share the line OP, have equal radii OA = OB, and each has a right angle at the point of contact. That is enough to make them identical — so their third sides, PA and PB, must be equal.

🧩 Build the proof

The other fact: the tangent–radius right angle is itself worth a reason — the radius to the point of contact is the shortest line from the centre O to the tangent, and the shortest line from a point to a line is always perpendicular to it. So the tangent meets the radius at 90°.

🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

A new kind of line — one that touches without crossing — and already two clean facts. Notice how the same humble idea (equal radii) that powered the whole first family is quietly at work again. Take a breath before moving on.

Notice one thing: the kite OAPB is symmetrical about the line OP. Where else have you relied on a line of symmetry to know two things must be equal? Spotting symmetry is a shortcut worth keeping.

You can jot it on the Scratchpad, say it aloud, or simply hold it in mind. You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: intellectual. We name honestly what the learning feeds.

What it feeds: tangents are where circle geometry meets the physical world most directly — a wheel on a road, a belt around a pulley, a planet's path grazing a gravitational curve, the line of sight that just clears a hill. The tangent–radius right angle is the reason a spinning object flung loose flies off perpendicular to the radius. Today feeds an appreciation of how a clean piece of geometry underwrites real motion and design — and, again, the quiet pleasure of two facts from one idea.

Carry one idea forward: a tangent is the direction of "just touching" — the instantaneous direction of travel around a curve.

🛠️ Final Task — Evidence for today

Your task: show you can state, use and justify the two tangent facts.

  • Draw a circle with centre O and a point P outside it. Construct the two tangents from P (touching at A and B) and the radii OA, OB. Mark the right angles and show PA = PB, giving reasons.
  • Use the facts to find missing angles and lengths in a tangent diagram, writing the reason at each step.
  • Explain in your own words why the two tangents are equal (congruent triangles) and why the tangent meets the radius at 90°.
  • stretch Show that in the kite OAPB, ∠AOB + ∠APB = 180°, and use it to find one from the other.

Submission choices: an annotated Scratchpad image, a photographed hand-drawn diagram, a construction measured in the Geometry Studio (Measure → Angle), a written proof, or a spoken/video explanation.

📐 Verify it in the Geometry Studio

Draw a circle and a point P outside it, construct the two tangents and the radii, and use Measure → Angle to check the radius–tangent angles are 90°. Compare the two tangent lengths, drag P, and confirm they stay equal. Save it with a sentence on why.
