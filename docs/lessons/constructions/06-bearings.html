NEO Mathematics: Constructions, Loci and Bearings

Bearings — describing direction

Driving Question: How do we describe a direction precisely, so anyone means the same thing?

Lesson type: Core — describing direction exactly with three-figure bearings: reading them, back bearings, and fixing a position from two bearings.

Stepping Stone: measure and state a three-figure bearing (clockwise from North), find a back bearing, and use two bearings to fix a position.

You will need: the four compass directions, the idea of measuring an angle in degrees, the locus idea from Lesson 5, and the optional ✏️ Scratchpad.

🌿 Connection — The compass, and three-figure bearings

🔤 The idea for this lesson

"It's over there" is useless to a rescue helicopter. Sailors, pilots and mountain rescuers describe a direction with a bearing: an angle measured clockwise from North, always written with three figures — so 72° becomes 072°. North is 000°, East 090°, South 180°, West 270°. Three figures, clockwise, from North: that convention makes a direction mean the same thing to everyone, everywhere.

What to do: press each compass direction and read its three-figure bearing on the dial.

What you are trying to notice: bearings grow clockwise from North (000°), and the halfway (ordinal) directions land on the odd multiples of 45° — North-East is 045°, South-East 135°, and so on.

🧭 The eight points and their bearings

Carry this forward: a bearing is measured clockwise from North and written with three figures (000°–360°).

🚶 Movement — Read and set a bearing

What to do: the dial is centred on A, with North straight up. Drag the point B and read the bearing of B from A. Then take the challenge: drag B until you hit the target bearing.

What you are trying to notice: the bearing is the clockwise angle swept from North round to the line AB — and it is written with three figures. Turning B clockwise increases the bearing; a full turn brings you back to 000°/360°.

🎯 The bearing dial

Carry this forward: to read a bearing, start at North and sweep clockwise to the direction you want; write the angle with three figures.

🪞 Reflection — There and back: back bearings

What to do: here are two places, A and B, each with its own North line. Read the bearing of B from A, and the bearing of A from B. Drag B and watch both.

What you are trying to notice: the two bearings always differ by exactly 180°. The return journey faces the opposite way, and because both North lines point the same way (they are parallel), the two bearings are a half-turn apart. So the back bearing is the forward bearing ± 180°.

🔁 Forward and back

Carry this forward: the back bearing (A from B) is the forward bearing (B from A) + 180° if it is under 180°, or − 180° if it is 180° or more.

🎨 Creativity — Fix the ship from two bearings stretch

What to do: two coastguard stations, A and B, each report the bearing of a ship in distress. Drag the ship until both reported bearings are correct — that is where it must be.

What you are trying to notice: one bearing only tells you the ship is somewhere along a line; it takes two bearings, from two places, to pin down a single point — where the two bearing lines cross. This is triangulation, and it is exactly the intersection-of-loci idea from Lesson 5.

🚢 Locate the ship

Make it your own: in the Studio, draw two bearing lines from two stations and use Construct → Intersection to mark where they cross.

📐 Fix a position from two bearings

Draw two bearing lines from two stations (check each with Measure → Bearing) and use Construct → Intersection to find the ship.

🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

Think of everyone who has ever been guided safely home by a bearing — a ship in fog, a plane at night, a walker off the path in cloud. A number and the word "North" can carry someone to safety. Take a breath and let that quiet usefulness settle.

Notice one thing: where have you relied on a direction being exact — a map app's blue cone, a wind direction, a satellite dish angle? There is no need to write anything; just let your attention wander and notice.

You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: intellectual. The mathematics is the nourishment, so we name honestly what the learning feeds.

What it feeds: today feeds an appreciation of shared convention — how a single agreed rule (clockwise, from North, three figures) lets strangers coordinate exactly, from anywhere on Earth. That is a quietly profound idea: mathematics as a common language. It also feeds a sense of real consequence — bearings are not an exercise; they keep ships off rocks and find people who are lost.

Carry one idea forward: a good convention removes ambiguity — everyone means the same thing.

🛠️ Final Task — Evidence for today

Your task: use bearings to describe direction and to fix a position.

  • State the three-figure bearing of one place from another, measured clockwise from North.
  • Find the back bearing — the bearing of the return journey — and show it differs by 180°.
  • Solve a fixing problem: given two bearings from two stations, find the single point they describe.
  • stretch Plot a short journey: sail on one bearing for a distance, then turn onto a new bearing, and describe where you end up.
