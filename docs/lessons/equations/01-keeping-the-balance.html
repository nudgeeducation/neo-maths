NEO Mathematics: Equations, Inequalities and Simultaneous Relationships

Keeping the balance

Driving Question: What value makes this true, and how do I undo my way to it?

Lesson type: Core — the opening lesson of the algebra unit, where solving becomes keeping the balance.

Stepping Stone: solve one- and two-step linear equations by applying inverse operations to both sides so the equation stays balanced, and check a solution by substituting it back.

You will need: the four operations and their inverses, working with negative numbers, and the optional ✏️ Scratchpad. No calculator needed today.

🌿 Connection — An equation is a hidden number

🔤 The idea for this lesson

An equation like 2x + 1 = 7 is a puzzle: some starting number x was doubled, then 1 was added, and the result was 7. To find x, you run the steps backwards, undoing each one with its inverse (opposite) operation, in reverse order. Solving is not moving symbols around by magic — it is careful undoing.

What to do: the forward machine shows what was done to a hidden x to reach the answer. Build the inverse machine beneath it — in reverse order, naming each operation correctly (like subtract 1, then divide by 2) — to get back to x. Type your inverse operations, then press Check.

What you are trying to notice: the inverse machine is the forward one read backwards. The last thing done is undone first, and each operation is replaced by its opposite: + becomes −, × becomes ÷.

🚶 Movement — Keep the scales balanced

What to do: the equation is shown as a pair of scales, with the bags and blocks resting on top of each pan — an x bag has an unknown mass; each small block is 1, and a red −1 tile is a block owed (it appears when the equation subtracts). The two sides balance because they are equal. Use the buttons to change both sides in the same way until a single x bag is left alone.

What you are trying to notice: the scales only stay level if you do exactly the same to both sides. If there are loose blocks, subtract the same number from both pans; if there are red owed tiles, add the same number to both pans until they cancel; then divide both pans into equal groups. When one x stands alone, the blocks facing it are its value.

⚖️ Solve by balancing

Carry this forward: whatever you do to one side of an equation you must do to the other — that is what keeps it true.

🪞 Reflection — The same moves, written down

What to do: the balancing you just did is exactly the written method. Choose the inverse operation to apply to both sides, and watch the equation rewrite itself, line by line, down to x = something. Then check by substituting back.

What you are trying to notice: each written line is one balance move. "Do the same to both sides" keeps the two sides equal, so every line is still true — which is why the method works. And the check is the proof: put your answer back and both sides must match.

✍️ Solve it symbolically, then check

Carry this forward: the written solution is a chain of true equations; the substitution check confirms the answer.

🎨 Creativity — Build your own equation stretch

What to do: if solving is undoing, then building an equation is just doing. Pick the answer you want x to be, then choose what to do to it — multiply, then add — and watch your equation appear. You have made a puzzle whose answer you already know.

What you are trying to notice: every equation is built forwards from its solution, so it can always be undone back to it. Building and solving are inverses of each other — which is why an equation always has an answer waiting to be found.

🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

There is something calming about an equation: it is simply a statement that two things are equal, and every move you make keeps that truth intact. A moment of order. Take a breath before moving on.

Notice one thing: did "do the same to both sides" feel different once you saw the scales — less a rule to obey, more an obvious way to keep things fair? There is no right answer; just notice what shifted.

You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: intellectual. The mathematics is the nourishment here, so we name honestly what the learning feeds.

What it feeds: today feeds a taste for certainty you can check — the quiet confidence that comes from an answer you can prove is right by putting it back, rather than hoping. It also feeds a sense of fairness and balance as a way of reasoning: keep both sides equal and the truth is preserved at every step. Learning that you can undo your way to a hidden value, reliably, is one of algebra's first real powers.

Carry one idea forward: when something is hidden, look for the steps that hid it, and undo them.

🛠️ Final Task — Evidence for today

Your task: solve linear equations by keeping the balance, and prove your answers.

  • Solve at least three equations — a one-step, a two-step, and one with the unknown after a subtraction (like 3x − 4 = 11) — showing the same operation on both sides at each line.
  • Check every answer by substituting it back into the original equation.
  • Explain in a sentence why doing the same to both sides keeps the equation true.
  • stretch Build your own two-step equation with a chosen answer, then solve it back to that answer.

Submission choices: an annotated Scratchpad image, a photographed hand-written solution showing each line, a short spoken walkthrough of one solve, or a poster of the method.
