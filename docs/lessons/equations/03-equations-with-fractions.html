Equations with fractions

Driving Question: How do I clear a fraction and keep the equation true?

Lesson type: Core — the opening lesson of the algebra unit, where solving becomes keeping the balance.

Stepping Stone: solve linear equations that contain fractions by multiplying every term by the denominator (or the lowest common multiple of the denominators) to clear them, then solving and checking by substitution.

You will need: the four operations and their inverses, working with negative numbers, and the optional ✏️ Scratchpad. No calculator needed today.

🌿 Connection — What a fraction of x means

🔤 The idea for this lesson

A fraction like x/3 just means "x shared into 3 equal parts, take one part". So 3 lots of x/3 rebuild the whole x. That is the secret to fractions in equations: multiply every term by the denominator and the fraction disappears — leaving an equation you already know how to solve. The denominator is the number underneath. If two different denominators appear, multiply by their lowest common multiple (the smallest number both divide into).

What to do: the bar is one whole x, cut into equal parts. See how many copies of one part rebuild the whole.

What you are trying to notice: x/n is one of n equal parts, and n of them make a whole x — so multiplying x/n by n gives x.

🍫 One part, then the whole

Carry this forward: to clear x/n, multiply by n — and to keep the equation true, multiply every term by n.

🚶 Movement — Clear the fraction, term by term

What to do: here is an equation with one fraction. Multiply every term by the denominator to clear it — watch each term change — then finish by keeping the balance.

What you are trying to notice: the fraction only clears if every term is multiplied, both sides. Miss a term and the equation is no longer true. Once the fraction is gone, it is an ordinary equation.

🧮 Multiply every term

Carry this forward: multiplying every term by the denominator clears the fraction without breaking the balance.

🪞 Reflection — The multiply-through method, written down

What to do: solve a fraction equation the written way. Choose the right move — multiply through by the denominator first, then clear the number, then divide — and watch it rewrite, line by line. Then check by substituting back.

What you are trying to notice: clearing the fraction is always the first move. Multiplying every term by the denominator keeps both sides equal — that is why it is allowed — and turns a fraction equation into a familiar one. The substitution check is the proof.

✍️ Solve by multiplying through, then check

Carry this forward: clear the fraction first by multiplying every term by the denominator; then it is a two-step equation you can already finish — and the check confirms it.

🎨 Creativity — Two denominators at once stretch

What to do: when two different denominators appear, one multiplier has to clear both. Find the lowest common multiple of the denominators, multiply every term by it, and both fractions vanish at once.

What you are trying to notice: the LCM is the smallest number both denominators divide into. Multiplying by it clears every fraction in one move — then you collect and solve as usual.

🔗 Clear both with the LCM

🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

Fractions can feel like the scary part of an equation — but notice that the whole method is just one calm move: multiply everything by the bottom number, and they are gone. What looked hard becomes something you have already met. Take a breath.

Notice one thing: did clearing the fraction feel less like a new rule and more like the same "do the same to both sides" idea — just used to tidy first? There is no right answer; just notice the connection.

You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: intellectual. The mathematics is the nourishment here, so we name honestly what the learning feeds.

What it feeds: today feeds composure with the awkward-looking — the confidence that a fraction is not a wall but a single tidy step away from an ordinary problem. Learning to make something intimidating look simple, without changing what it means, is a quietly powerful habit that reaches far beyond equations.

Carry one idea forward: when a problem looks messy, find the one move that clears the mess — then solve the familiar thing underneath.

🛠️ Final Task — Evidence for today

Your task: solve equations containing fractions by clearing them, and prove your answers.

  • Solve at least three: one like x/3 + 2 = 5, one like (x + 1)/4 = 3, and one with two denominators like x/2 + x/3 = 10.
  • Show the clearing step — multiply every term by the denominator (or the LCM) — then solve keeping the balance.
  • Check every answer by substituting it back into the original equation.
  • stretch Make your own fraction equation with a chosen whole-number answer, then solve it back to that answer.

Submission choices: an annotated Scratchpad image, a photographed hand-written solution showing each line, a short spoken walkthrough, or a poster of the method.
