NEO Mathematics: Equations, Inequalities and Simultaneous Relationships

Forming and solving a pair

Driving Question: How do I set up two equations from one situation?

Lesson type: Core — the opening lesson of the algebra unit, where solving becomes keeping the balance.

Stepping Stone: form a pair of simultaneous equations from a real situation with two unknowns, solve the pair (by substitution or elimination), and interpret the answer in context — checking it makes the situation true.

You will need: the four operations and their inverses, working with negative numbers, and the optional ✏️ Scratchpad. No calculator needed today.

NEO Cornerstones: Connection · Movement · Reflection · Creativity · Rest · Nutrition

🌱 Open Learner Guide 📘 Open Supporting Adult Guidance
🌿 Connection — Two unknowns, two facts

🔤 The idea for this lesson

To find two unknowns you need two facts. A situation like "two lunches and a drink cost £8; three lunches and two drinks cost £13" hides two prices — but it also gives two sentences, and each sentence becomes an equation. Name the unknowns (let x and y be the two prices), form the pair, solve it with the methods you know, then interpret: say what each unknown is worth, and check it fits the story.

What to do: read the two sentences and build the two equations by filling in each coefficient. Press Check, then see the pair solved and the prices read back.

What you are trying to notice: "two lunches" is 2x, "a drink" is 1y, "cost £8" is = 8. Each sentence maps straight onto an equation — the same translation as before, done twice.

🚶 Movement — Solve the pair

What to do: here the pair is already formed. Solve it by elimination — one variable already has a matching coefficient, so subtract to remove it — then find the other and read the prices.

What you are trying to notice: once a situation is a pair of equations, it is just the algebra from the last two lessons. The context does not change the method — it only tells you what the answer means.

✍️ Eliminate, solve, interpret

Carry this forward: a context pair solves exactly like any other — eliminate, solve, back-substitute, interpret.

🪞 Reflection — What does the answer let you work out?

What to do: the prices are found. Now use them to answer a new question about the same situation — the cost of a different combination. That is what a solved pair is for.

What you are trying to notice: once you know each unknown, the situation is fully understood — you can price any order, not just the two you were given. Interpreting turns the answer into something useful.

🧾 Use the prices

Carry this forward: a solved pair prices any combination — interpret it to answer new questions.

🎨 Creativity — Set up your own shop stretch

What to do: forming is just doing to prices you choose. Set two prices, and the shop writes two purchase receipts — a pair of equations whose answer is your prices. You have created a solvable puzzle.

What you are trying to notice: every "find the prices" puzzle is built forwards from the prices. Forming a pair is the exact reverse of solving one.

🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

Notice how a everyday scene — a couple of café orders — quietly contains exactly enough information to pin down two hidden prices. Two facts, two unknowns, one clean answer. The world is full of these little solvable puzzles once you know how to see them. Take a breath.

Notice one thing: did it feel satisfying to turn a vague "how much is each?" into two tidy equations and an exact answer? Just notice the moment the situation became solvable.

You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: contextual. Forming and solving a pair genuinely lives in everyday budgeting and shopping, so the context here is honest.

What it feeds: this is real, usable numeracy. Working out the price of each item from two receipts, splitting a bill fairly, or comparing two offers with two unknowns are exactly this skill. Being able to name two unknowns and pin them down with two facts gives you genuine control over money and choices — the mathematics pays for itself.

Carry one idea forward: when two things are unknown, look for two facts — together they are usually enough to find both.

🛠️ Final Task — Evidence for today

Your task: form and solve pairs of equations from situations, and interpret the answers.

  • Solve at least three situations with two unknowns. For each: name the unknowns, form both equations, solve the pair, and say what each unknown is worth.
  • Check each answer against both facts in the situation.
  • Use one solution to answer a new question about the same situation (a different combination).
  • stretch Invent your own two-item situation with chosen prices, then solve it back.

Submission choices: an annotated Scratchpad image, a photographed hand-written solution, a short spoken walkthrough, or a poster taking one situation from words to prices.
