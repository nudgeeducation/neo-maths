NEO Mathematics: Formulae and Rearranging

Changing the subject

Driving Question: How do I turn a formula round to find what I need?

Lesson type: Core — rearranging a formula so that a different letter stands alone.

Stepping Stone: change the subject of a formula in which the new subject appears once, by undoing operations in reverse order and doing the same to both sides; and use the rearranged formula.

You will need: solving one- and two-step equations, and inverse operations — and the optional ✏️ Scratchpad.

🌱 Open Learner Guide 📘 Open Supporting Adult Guidance
🌿 Connection — Reverse the machine

🔤 The idea for this lesson

The subject of a formula is the letter on its own: in v = u + at, the subject is v. Sometimes you need a different letter on its own — t, perhaps. Changing the subject turns the formula round, by undoing what was done to that letter, in reverse order.

What to do: each formula is drawn as a machine. Build the machine that runs it backwards: choose the first reverse step, then the next.

What you are trying to notice: each step is undone by its inverse (+ by −, × by ÷), and the last thing done is the first thing undone — like taking off shoes before socks.

🔁 Forwards, then backwards

Carry this forward: inverse operations, in reverse order. Next, do the same thing to a whole formula, keeping it balanced.

🚶 Movement — The formula balance

What to do: choose an operation and press Do it to both sides. Keep going until the letter you want stands alone. The numbers underneath show that the formula is still true after every step.

What you are trying to notice: rearranging is the same as solving an equation, but with letters. As long as you do the same to both sides, the balance stays level — and the numbers prove it.

⚖️ Keep it level

Carry this forward: find the letter you want; undo the last thing done to it first; do the same to both sides; stop when it stands alone.

🪞 Reflection — Spot the faulty rearrangement

What to do: each piece of working rearranges a formula. Find the first line that goes wrong, or decide there is no mistake. If you are unsure, press Test with numbers.

What you are trying to notice: a correct rearrangement stays true for the same numbers on every line. The usual slips are undoing in the wrong order, doing something to only part of a side, or using the wrong inverse.

🔬 Check the working

Carry this forward: numbers are a quick, honest check of any rearrangement.

🎨 Creativity — Build a converter

What to do: each formula answers the wrong question. Rearrange it so that it answers the question you need, and the lesson turns your formula into a converter that works for many values at once.

What you are trying to notice: you could substitute and then solve every time — but once a formula is rearranged, it answers the new question for any value straight away.

🔄 Rearrange, then use it

✨ Make your own stretch

Choose a formula from earlier in the unit — a taxi fare, a gym plan, a perimeter. Rearrange it to answer a "how many?" or "how long?" question, and make a small table showing it working for three values.

🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

Today you turned formulae round, step by step, without ever letting them tip. Take a slow breath.

Notice one thing: think of getting dressed, then undressing — the last thing on is the first thing off. You already know reverse order; algebra just writes it down.

You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: intellectual. The mathematics is the nourishment, so we name honestly what the learning feeds.

What it feeds: today feeds flexibility — taking a tool built for one question and reshaping it for another. Engineers, scientists and programmers rearrange formulae all the time, so one relationship can answer many different questions.

Carry one idea forward: when a tool answers the wrong question, see if you can turn it round.

🛠️ Final Task — Turn it round

Your task: show that you can change the subject of a formula, check it, and use it.

  • Make x the subject of y = 4x − 7, and make u the subject of v = u + at. Show each step done to both sides.
  • Check each rearrangement by choosing numbers and testing both the original and the new formula.
  • Rearrange F = 1.8C + 32 to make C the subject, and use it to convert 50 °F, 86 °F and 212 °F.
  • Explain, using a function machine, why the steps must be undone in reverse order.
  • stretch Make r the subject of A = 3r², and find r when A = 75.

Submission choices: an annotated Scratchpad image, a photographed page, a short slideshow, or a spoken/video walkthrough.
