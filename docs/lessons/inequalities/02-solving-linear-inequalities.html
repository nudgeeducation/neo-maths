NEO Mathematics: Inequalities

Solving linear inequalities

Driving Question: Can I solve an inequality like an equation — and where does it behave differently?

Lesson type: Core — solving by keeping the balance, with one surprise to discover rather than memorise.

Stepping Stone: solve a linear inequality by doing the same to both sides; explain why multiplying or dividing by a negative number reverses the inequality; and check a solution by testing a value.

You will need: solving one- and two-step equations with inverse operations, and the inequality symbols from Lesson 1 — and the optional ✏️ Scratchpad.

🌱 Open Learner Guide 📘 Open Supporting Adult Guidance
🌿 Connection — What keeps the order?

🔤 The idea for this lesson

Solving an equation means doing the same to both sides until x stands alone. An inequality can be solved the same way — as long as each step keeps the statement true. Before solving anything, find out which steps keep an inequality true. Take two numbers in order, do the same thing to both, and see whether the order survives.

What to do: choose an operation. First predict whether a will still be less than b, then watch both numbers move on the number line. Try every operation, and try the other pairs too.

What you are trying to notice: adding, subtracting, and multiplying or dividing by a positive number keep the order. Multiplying or dividing by a negative number reflects both numbers across 0 — and a reflection reverses their order.

🔍 The flip detector

Carry this forward: the order of two numbers survives every step except multiplying or dividing by a negative number. That step reverses it.

🚶 Movement — The tipped balance

What to do: the balance shows an inequality. Choose an operation and a number, then press Do it to both sides. Keep going until x stands alone. When you multiply or divide by a negative number, you decide which symbol keeps the statement true.

What you are trying to notice: the balance is drawn with a test value — one value of x that makes the inequality true. Most steps leave it tipped the same way. Multiplying or dividing by a negative tips it the other way, so the symbol must turn round to stay true.

⚖️ Solve by keeping the balance

Carry this forward: solve an inequality exactly like an equation — and whenever you multiply or divide both sides by a negative number, reverse the symbol.

🪞 Reflection — Does every line agree?

What to do: here is someone's working. Slide the test value and watch whether each line is true (✓) or false (✗) for that value. If every line always agrees, the working is correct. If two lines ever disagree, find the first line that goes wrong.

What you are trying to notice: each line of correct working says the same thing about x in a different way, so a test value is either true on every line or false on every line. Where the lines disagree, a step broke the truth — very often a forgotten reversal.

🧪 Test the working

Carry this forward: check a solution by testing a value in the original inequality. A value inside your answer should make it true; a value outside should make it false.

🎨 Creativity — Inequalities from real life

What to do: each situation has a limit. Choose the inequality that describes it, solve it, then decide what the answer means in real life.

What you are trying to notice: the algebra gives a range, but the situation decides which values make sense — a taxi cannot charge for half a mile, so a whole-number answer may be needed. Reading the answer back into the story is part of solving.

🧭 From story to solution

✨ Make your own stretch

Invent a situation with a limit where solving needs a step that reverses the inequality — for example, something that goes down each time, with a minimum you must keep. Write it, solve it on the Scratchpad, and explain the reversal in one sentence.

🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

You discovered something today rather than being told it: a reflection across zero turns an order round. Ideas you find yourself tend to stay. Take a slow breath.

Notice one thing: can you picture the number line flipping over, with every number landing on the other side of zero? Hold that picture for a moment. It is the reason for the rule.

You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: intellectual. The mathematics is the nourishment, so we name honestly what the learning feeds.

What it feeds: today feeds the habit of asking why a rule is true. "Flip the sign for a negative" can be learned as a trick and forgotten, or understood as a reflection and kept. Checking with a test value also feeds self-trust: you do not need anyone to tell you whether your answer is right — you can find out.

Carry one idea forward: when a rule surprises you, test it with real numbers until it stops being a surprise.

🛠️ Final Task — Solve, check and explain

Your task: show that you can solve inequalities and that you know why the method works.

  • Solve four inequalities, showing every step: 2x + 3 < 15, 4x − 1 ≥ 11, 9 − 3x > 0 and 6 − 2x ≤ 14.
  • Show each answer on a number line, with the right circle at the boundary.
  • For each, check with a test value from inside your answer and one from outside.
  • Explain, with your own numerical example, why multiplying or dividing by a negative number reverses the inequality.
  • stretch Write a real-life situation that leads to one of your inequalities and say what the answer means in the story.

Submission choices: an annotated Scratchpad image, a photographed page, a short slideshow, or a spoken/video walkthrough.
