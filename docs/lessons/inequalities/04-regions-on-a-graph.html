NEO Mathematics: Inequalities

Inequalities as regions on a graph

Driving Question: Where do all the allowed points live?

Lesson type: Core — the last lesson of the unit. A number line becomes a whole flat plane, and a solution set becomes a region.

Stepping Stone: represent and identify regions on a graph defined by simple linear inequalities such as x > 2, y ≤ 3 and x + y < 5; draw the boundary dashed when it is excluded and solid when it is included; and find the whole-number points in a region.

You will need: plotting coordinates in all four quadrants, and open and filled circles from Lesson 3 — and the optional ✏️ Scratchpad.

🌱 Open Learner Guide 📘 Open Supporting Adult Guidance
🌿 Connection — From a line to a plane

🔤 The idea for this lesson

On a number line, x > 2 is a ray: an open circle at 2 and an arrow to the right. On a graph there is a second coordinate, y, and the rule says nothing about it. So the ray can sit at every height — and all those rays together fill a whole region.

What to do: move the slider to choose a height. At each height, the ray for the rule is drawn and stays. Try several heights, then press Fill in every height. Then try the second rule.

What you are trying to notice: the open circles line up into a dashed line, and filled circles line up into a solid line. The boundary of a region keeps the same meaning as the circle: dashed for "not included", solid for "included".

🟩 Sweep the ray

Carry this forward: a rule about x alone gives a region beside a vertical line; a rule about y alone gives a region above or below a horizontal line.

🚶 Movement — The wandering point

What to do: drag the point around the grid (or use the arrow buttons). The table shows, for wherever the point is, whether it obeys each of three rules. Then take on the challenges one at a time.

What you are trying to notice: as the point crosses a boundary line, one tick turns into a cross. The allowed region is the place where every rule shows a tick.

📍 Move the point

Carry this forward: a point is in the region only if it obeys every rule. On a boundary, check whether the line itself is included.

🪞 Reflection — Allowed points

What to do: tap whole-number points on the grid to test them against the rule. A point that obeys turns green; one that does not turns grey. When you have tested at least six, press Show every point. Then decide where the boundary line goes, and whether it is dashed or solid.

What you are trying to notice: the green points are not scattered — they sit on one side of a straight line. Finding that line, and deciding whether points on it are allowed, is how you draw the whole region.

🎯 Test, then find the boundary

Carry this forward: to draw a region, draw the boundary line (dashed or solid), then test one point to see which side is allowed.

🎨 Creativity — The region builder

What to do: build up to four rules from parts, and watch the allowed region appear where they overlap. Each challenge asks you to trap a particular set of points. There is usually more than one way.

What you are trying to notice: each rule cuts the plane along a line, and every rule you add can only make the region smaller. Choosing the cuts is a design decision.

🧱 Build the region

✨ Make your own stretch

Design a map: a park, a car park or a games board on a grid, where something is only allowed in one area (a dog-walking zone, the area a sprinkler reaches, where a piece may move). Write the rules that describe the area, build them in the region builder, and list the whole-number points that are allowed.

🌙 Rest — A pause to close the unit

This unit began with a single question: when the answer isn't a single number, how do we describe all the numbers that work? You have answered it four ways — with symbols, by solving, on a number line, and now as a region. Take a slow breath.

Notice one thing: picture an open circle stretching upwards into a dashed line, and an arrow spreading out into a shaded region. The same idea, grown into a bigger space.

You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: intellectual. The mathematics is the nourishment, so we name honestly what the learning feeds.

What it feeds: today feeds the habit of noticing when an old idea grows. Open and filled circles became dashed and solid lines; overlapping number lines became overlapping regions. Mathematics often works like this — a small idea, understood well, carries into places you have not been yet. The same thinking is used to plan where a delivery van can reach, or which mix of two products a business can afford to make.

Carry one idea forward: when something new feels unfamiliar, ask which old idea it might be wearing.

🛠️ Final Task — Map the allowed region

Your task: show that you can draw, read and explain regions on a graph.

  • On a coordinate grid from −5 to 5, draw the region where x ≥ −2, y < 3 and x + y ≤ 4 are all true. Draw each boundary dashed or solid, and shade the region.
  • Mark and list every whole-number point in the region that has both coordinates positive.
  • Choose one point on each boundary line and say whether it is in the region, and why.
  • Explain how dashed and solid lines connect to open and filled circles on a number line.
  • stretch Design a map with an allowed area, write its rules, and explain what the whole-number points mean in your situation.

Submission choices: an annotated Scratchpad image, a photographed page, a short slideshow, or a spoken/video walkthrough.
