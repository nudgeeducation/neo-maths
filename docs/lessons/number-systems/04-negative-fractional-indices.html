NEO Mathematics: Number Systems and Calculator Judgement

Negative and fractional indices

Driving Question: What can a power of −2 or ½ possibly mean?

Lesson type: Core — extending powers below 1 and between whole numbers, so that the index laws keep working.

Stepping Stone: explain why a0 = 1; use a negative index as the reciprocal: a−n = 1/an; use a fractional index as a root: a1/2 = √a; and work out values such as 8−2/3 without a calculator.

You will need: the index laws for whole-number powers, squares and cubes and their roots — and the optional ✏️ Scratchpad. No calculator today: exact values are the point.

🌿 Connection — Two answers to one question

🔤 The idea for this lesson

An index (or power) counts how many times a number is multiplied by itself: 23 = 2 × 2 × 2. But what could 20, 2−3 or 91/2 mean? You cannot multiply 2 by itself −3 times. Today you will find the only meanings that keep the patterns and the index laws working — and prove that no other meaning would do.

What to do: use the index laws to write each answer as a single power. Then work out one division two different ways.

What you are trying to notice: the index laws can produce powers of 0 or negative powers — and those must agree with the answer you get by cancelling.

🔁 The index laws

Carry this forward: 54 ÷ 56 is both 5−2 and 1/25. So, if the laws are to keep working, 5−2 must be 1/25.

🚶 Movement — Down the power ladder

What to do: each rung of the ladder is one power. Going down one rung divides by the base. Climb down past the last rung you know, and fill in each new rung. Then try another base.

What you are trying to notice: the pattern does not stop at power 1. It carries on to power 0, and then to negative powers — and their values are fractions, not negative numbers.

🪞 Reflection — Reciprocal, not negative

What to do: work out each value exactly. Type fractions like 1/25.

What you are trying to notice: a negative index never makes the answer negative. It tells you to take the reciprocal. The reciprocal of 2/3 is 3/2: the numerator and denominator change places, because 2/3 × 3/2 = 1.

🔎 Exactly how much?

Carry this forward: negative index → reciprocal. Index 0 → 1.

🎨 Creativity — Sideways on the power ladder

What to do: now the ladder has rungs between the whole powers. Two half-steps make one whole step; three third-steps make one whole step. Find each missing rung. Then build a ladder of your own.

What you are trying to notice: if two equal steps multiply by 9, each step multiplies by 3 — because 3 × 3 = 9. So a power of ½ is a square root, and a power of ⅓ is a cube root.

🪜 The in-between rungs

✓ The rule: for a positive base a, a1/n = n√a (the nth root), and am/n = (n√a)m: take the root, then the power. A negative index still means the reciprocal.

stretch Why must a1/2 be √a? Prove it with the index laws.

🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

A question that seemed meaningless — multiply 2 by itself −3 times? — turned out to have exactly one sensible answer. Take a slow breath.

Notice one thing: picture the ladder running down for ever, each rung half the one above. It never reaches 0, and it never goes below it.

You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: intellectual. The mathematics is the nourishment, so we name honestly what the learning feeds.

What it feeds: today feeds the satisfaction of a definition that is forced. Nobody chose 20 = 1 on a whim. Once the index laws are agreed, there is only one value that keeps them true — and you proved it. Much of mathematics grows this way: stretching an idea further, while keeping what already works.

Carry one idea forward: when a rule meets a new case, ask what the case must mean for the rule to keep working.

🛠️ Final Task — The whole ladder

Your task: show that you can explain, work out and prove the meanings of zero, negative and fractional indices.

  • Without a calculator, work out exactly: 60, 4−2, (35)−1, 491/2, 272/3 and 16−3/4.
  • Draw a power ladder for base 2, from 23 down to 2−3. Use it to explain why 2−3 = 1/8.
  • Prove that a0 = 1 using the index laws. Explain why a cannot be 0.
  • Write each as a power of 2: 1/32, √2, 1/√8.
  • stretch Show that 82/3 gives the same answer whether you take the root first or the power first. Which is easier, and why?
