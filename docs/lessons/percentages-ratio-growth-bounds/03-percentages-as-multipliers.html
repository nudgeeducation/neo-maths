Percentages as multipliers

Driving Question: What single number does the change?

Lesson type: Core — every percentage question becomes one multiplication.

Stepping Stone: use percentages as multipliers: find a percentage of an amount, increase or decrease an amount by a percentage with a single multiplier, and write one number as a percentage of another.

You will need: fractions, decimals and percentages as one number, and multiplying by a decimal, plus the optional ✏️ Scratchpad. A calculator is allowed: estimate first.

🌿 Connection — The Multiplier Line

🔤 The idea for this lesson

A percentage is a number out of 100, so 35% = 35/100 = 0.35, and 35% of £80 = 0.35 × £80. The whole amount is 100%, a multiplier of 1. An increase of 15% leaves 115% of the amount: × 1.15. A decrease of 20% leaves 80%: × 0.8. To write one number as a percentage of another, divide the part by the whole and multiply by 100.

What to do: choose a mode and an item. Change the percentage with the buttons. The two number lines show the same point as a percentage and as a multiplier, and the bars show the amounts.

What you are trying to notice: where is 100% on the multiplier line? Which multipliers make the amount bigger, and which make it smaller?

🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

One number does the whole job. Take a slow breath.

Notice one thing: next time you see a sign in a shop window, such as “30% off”, think of its multiplier.

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: practical. Today the mathematics feeds decisions you make with money.

What it feeds: today feeds checking for yourself. Sale prices, VAT, pay rises and price increases are all percentage changes. With one multiplier you can check a price on your phone in seconds, and notice when a deal is not as good as it sounds.

Carry one idea forward: 100% is × 1. Up is more than 1; down is less.

🛠️ Final Task — Shop wisely

Your task: choose three real prices (from a shop, a website or a menu).

  • Apply a discount of your choice to one, using a single multiplier. Show the multiplier.
  • Add 20% VAT to another, using a single multiplier.
  • Write one of your prices as a percentage of another.
  • Explain, with a sketch of the Multiplier Line, why × 0.8 is a 20% decrease.
  • stretch Explain why increasing by 10% and then decreasing by 10% does not get back to the start.

Submission choices: an annotated Scratchpad image, a photographed page, a short slideshow, or a spoken or video walkthrough.
