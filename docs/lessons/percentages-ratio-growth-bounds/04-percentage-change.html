Percentage change

Driving Question: Change compared with what?

Lesson type: Core — every change is measured against where it started.

Stepping Stone: find a percentage increase or decrease: the change as a percentage of the original value, and compare changes fairly.

You will need: percentages as multipliers (Lesson 3) and one number as a percentage of another, plus the optional ✏️ Scratchpad. A calculator is allowed: estimate first.

🌿 Connection — The Before-and-After Board

🔤 The idea for this lesson

A percentage change compares the change with the original (starting) value: percentage change = changeoriginal × 100. A rise is a percentage increase; a fall is a percentage decrease. Equally, neworiginal is the multiplier from Lesson 3: 1.25 means a 25% increase, 0.8 a 20% decrease.

What to do: choose a situation and set the before and after values. The before bar is always 100%; the change shows as a green piece (a rise) or a dashed red gap (a fall). Try Swap before and after, then try Compare two changes.

What you are trying to notice: when you swap, the change in pounds stays the same. Does the percentage?

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: practical. Today the mathematics feeds how you read the news and adverts.

What it feeds: today feeds judging claims fairly. “Prices up by £20!” and “Prices up 5%!” can describe the same change. Knowing which original a percentage is measured against lets you see whether a change is big or small, and spot a headline that makes a small change sound dramatic.

Carry one idea forward: compared with what?

🛠️ Final Task — Change detective

Your task: find three real changes (a price you know, a sports statistic, a population, a journey time).

  • For each, find the change and the percentage change, showing the original you compared with.
  • Choose two of them and decide which is the bigger change in amount, and which is the bigger percentage change.
  • Take one change and swap it (after back to before). Find the new percentage change and explain why it is different.
  • stretch Explain why a rise of 25% is undone by a fall of only 20%.

Submission choices: an annotated Scratchpad image, a photographed page, a short slideshow, or a spoken or video walkthrough.
