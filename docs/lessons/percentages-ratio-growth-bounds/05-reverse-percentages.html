Reverse percentages

Driving Question: How do I undo a change?

Lesson type: Core — work back from the value after a change to the original.

Stepping Stone: solve a reverse percentage problem: after = original × multiplier, so the original is aftermultiplier. The percentage always belongs to the original.

You will need: percentages as multipliers (Lesson 3) and percentage change (Lesson 4), plus the optional ✏️ Scratchpad. A calculator is allowed: estimate first.

🔤 The idea for this lesson

A reverse percentage problem gives the value after a change and asks for the original value. The change is a percentage of the original, so after = original × multiplier. To undo multiplying, divide: original = aftermultiplier. For example, if a price after a 20% rise is £96, the original is 961.2 = £80, not £96 take away 20% of £96.

🪞 Reflection — The Undo Machine (today's main activity)

What to do: choose a situation, set the percentage change and the value after the change. Predict the original if you like, then press ◀ Run backwards. Press Show the slip to see what happens if you take the percentage of the after value instead.

What you are trying to notice: the percentage belongs to the original. Running backwards divides by the multiplier; the slip lands somewhere else, and running it forwards does not give the after value.

🛠️ Final Task — Undo detective

Your task: find or make three reverse percentage problems: a sale price, a price including 20% VAT, and something that grew or shrank.

  • For each, find the original value by dividing by the multiplier, showing the fraction aftermultiplier.
  • Check each answer forwards: original × multiplier = after.
  • For one of them, work out the slip (taking the percentage of the after value) and show why it does not check forwards.
  • stretch Explain why a 25% rise is undone by a 20% fall, using a multiplier.
