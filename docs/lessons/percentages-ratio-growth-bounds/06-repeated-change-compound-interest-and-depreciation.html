🔤 The idea for this lesson

Simple interest adds the same amount every year: a percentage of the original. Compound interest adds a percentage of the amount so far, so the interest itself earns interest. Each year multiplies by the same multiplier, so after n years: amount = original × multipliern. For example, £500 at 4% compound interest for 3 years is 500 × 1.043, not 500 + 3 × 20. Depreciation is the same with a multiplier below 1: losing 15% a year multiplies by 0.85 each year.

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: practical. Today the mathematics feeds how you understand savings and the value of things.

What it feeds: today feeds reading offers with a clear head. A savings account, a phone that loses value each year, or a population that grows each year all repeat the same percentage change. Knowing that the change compounds helps you compare offers and see why time matters.
