NEO Mathematics: Percentages, Ratio, Growth and Bounds

Growth models

Driving Question: Add the same, or multiply by the same?

Lesson type: Core — two kinds of growth, side by side.

Stepping Stone: recognise and build linear models (add the same amount each step: y = a + dn) and exponential models (multiply by the same number each step: y = a × mn); test a table by its differences and ratios, and choose a model that fits.

You will need: straight-line graphs, sequences and repeated change (Lesson 6), plus the optional ✏️ Scratchpad. A calculator is allowed.

Way B

On which day has Way B first reached more people than Way A?

🔤 The idea for this lesson

A linear model adds the same amount each step: y = a + dn. Its differences are constant and its graph is a straight line. An exponential model multiplies by the same number each step: y = a × mn. Its ratios (each value over the one before) are constant and its graph curves. When m is more than 1 it is exponential growth; between 0 and 1 it is exponential decay.

What to do: answer each question. Build it in the Model Builder if it helps.

🧩 Briefs

Carry this forward: constant differences → linear, y = a + dn. Constant ratios → exponential, y = a × mn. Exponential growth always overtakes linear growth in the end.

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: practical. Today the mathematics feeds how you read graphs and claims.

What it feeds: today feeds reading change in the world. “Up 100 a week” and “doubling every week” can sound alike at first, but they behave very differently. Spotting which model fits a story helps you judge a claim about views, followers, savings or a population.

🛠️ Final Task — Two models, one story

Your task: choose a situation that grows or shrinks (followers, a plant, a savings jar, a cooling drink).

  • Write a linear model and an exponential model that both start at the same value.
  • Make a table for each with a differences column and a ratios column.
  • Draw both on one graph with numbered, labelled axes, and mark where one overtakes the other.
  • Decide which model fits your situation better, and explain why.
  • stretch Explain why the exponential model must overtake the linear one in the end.

Submission choices: an annotated Scratchpad image, a photographed page, a short slideshow, or a spoken or video walkthrough.
