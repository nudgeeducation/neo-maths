Calculating with bounds

Driving Question: Which bounds give the biggest answer?

Lesson type: Core — choosing the right bound for each part of a calculation.

Stepping Stone: find the upper and lower bounds of a calculated result (a sum, a difference, a product or a division) by choosing which bound of each value to use.

You will need: bounds of a rounded value (Lesson 8) and multiplying and dividing decimals, plus the optional ✏️ Scratchpad. A calculator is allowed.

🌿 Connection — Will it fit?

Start here: a sofa is 210 cm long, to the nearest 10 cm. A gap between two cupboards is 212 cm, to the nearest cm. Can you be sure the sofa fits?

🔤 The idea for this lesson

When a calculation uses rounded values, its answer has bounds too. Each value can be anywhere from its lower bound to its upper bound, so try the bounds and choose: to make a result as large as possible, make everything that adds to it as large as possible and everything that takes away from it as small as possible. For a division, the largest result is upper boundlower bound: a smaller denominator gives a larger answer.

Today's main activity, the Bounds Calculator, is in Movement.

🚶 Movement — The Bounds Calculator (today's main activity)

What to do: choose a situation. Set each measurement and how it was rounded. Then flip each one between its lower and upper bound and watch the result move. Choose an aim, largest or smallest, and flip until you find it. If it helps, raise a hand for upper and lower it for lower as you flip.

What you are trying to notice: for which calculation does the largest answer not come from both upper bounds?

🧮 The Bounds Calculator

Carry this forward: largest sum or product: upper with upper. Largest difference: upper − lower. Largest division: upperlower. For the smallest, swap every choice.

🪞 Reflection — True or not?

What to do: decide whether each statement is true. Test it on the calculator if it helps.

What you are trying to notice: the part that takes away (or divides) needs the opposite bound.

🛠️ Final Task — Can I be sure?

Your task: choose a real planning question that uses two rounded measurements (will it fit? is there enough? how fast?).

  • Write each measurement with its degree of accuracy and its bounds.
  • Find the upper and lower bounds of the result, saying which bound of each value you used and why.
  • Answer the question: can you be sure? Explain using your bounds.
  • stretch Include a division, and explain why the smaller denominator gives the larger answer.
