NEO Mathematics: Perimeter and Area

Perimeter or area?

Driving Question: Can two shapes have the same perimeter but different areas?

Lesson type: Core — perimeter goes round a shape; area fills it. Fixing one does not fix the other.

Stepping Stone: distinguish perimeter from area, and show that shapes with the same perimeter can have different areas (and the reverse).

You will need: counting squares, metres (Place Value Lesson 8), and the optional ✏️ Scratchpad. No calculator needed.

🌿 Connection — Fence and Field

🔤 The idea for this lesson

The perimeter of a shape is the distance all the way round its edge, like a fence. It is a length, measured in metres (m). The area is the space inside, like the grass. It is counted in squares: a square 1 m by 1 m is 1 square metre (1 m²). Perimeter and area are different stories: the same fence can hold very different amounts of grass.

What to do: each grid square is 1 m by 1 m. Drag the yellow corner to change the plot (or click the grid and use the arrow keys). Then fix the fence, or fix the grass, and find every rectangle you can.

What you are trying to notice: when the fence stays the same, does the grass? When the grass stays the same, does the fence?

🌱 The plot

stretch Why does the square hold the most grass for 20 m of fence? A pattern suggests; a reason convinces.

A 5 m by 5 m square. The squares taken away (red) and added back (green) are shown as you go.

Carry this forward: perimeter is the distance round (m); area is the squares inside (m²). The same perimeter can hold different areas.

🚶 Movement — Perimeter or area?

What to do: for each job, decide whether you need the perimeter or the area. If you can, trace the edge of a table or book with your finger for perimeter, and sweep your hand across it for area.

What you are trying to notice: does the job go round the edge, or cover the inside?

🧭 Round or inside?

🪞 Reflection — True or not?

What to do: decide whether each statement is true. Test it on the plot if it helps.

What you are trying to notice: which mistakes come from mixing up “round” and “inside”?

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: intellectual. The mathematics is the nourishment, so we name honestly what the learning feeds.

What it feeds: today feeds careful questions. “How big is it?” can mean how far round or how much inside. Asking which one you mean saves money when buying fencing, paint or carpet.

Carry one idea forward: round the edge is perimeter; inside is area.

🛠️ Final Task — Same fence, different fields

Your task: make a short report on a fixed fence.

  • Choose a fence length (an even number of metres, such as 16 m or 24 m).
  • Draw every whole-metre rectangle it can make, and record each one's perimeter and area in a table.
  • Say which rectangle holds the most grass, and which the least.
  • stretch Explain why the square holds the most, using the idea of taking away a column and adding a row.

Submission choices: an annotated Scratchpad image, a photographed page, a short slideshow, or a spoken or video walkthrough.
