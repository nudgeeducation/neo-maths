Area of rectangles and triangles

Driving Question: Why is a triangle half a rectangle?

🌿 Connection — Cut and Turn

🔤 The idea for this lesson

A rectangle is rows of equal squares, so its area = length × width. A triangle sits inside a rectangle with the same base and the same height. The height is the straight-up distance from the base line to the top corner, at right angles to the base (the perpendicular height). Cut the triangle along its height and turn the two pieces: they fill the rest of the rectangle exactly. So a triangle is half its rectangle: area = ½ × base × height.

What to do: each grid square is 1 m by 1 m. In Rectangle, drag the corner and watch the rows. In Triangle, drag the top corner anywhere and the base end along the base, then press Cut and turn.

What you are trying to notice: however you change the triangle, how much of its rectangle does it fill?

🛠️ Final Task — Half a rectangle

Your task: show, on squared paper or the Scratchpad, why a triangle is half its rectangle.

  • Draw a rectangle and a triangle inside it with the same base and height. Cut and turn the pieces (or draw them turned) to show they fill the rest.
  • Find the areas of three different triangles with the same base and height, and explain why they are equal.
  • Find the area of one rectangle and one triangle from real life (a door, a sail, a roof end), with units.
  • stretch Show why ½ × base × height still works for an obtuse triangle, whose height is outside it.

Submission choices: an annotated Scratchpad image, a photographed page, a short slideshow, or a spoken or video walkthrough.
