NEO Mathematics: Perimeter and Area

Composite shapes and design choices

Driving Question: How much floor does this room really have?

Lesson type: Core — split a room into rectangles, find missing walls, then design rooms to a brief.

Stepping Stone: find the perimeter and area of composite shapes, in metres and centimetres, and use them to make design choices.

You will need: area of rectangles (Lesson 2), metres and centimetres (Place Value Lesson 8), and the optional ✏️ Scratchpad. No calculator needed.

🌿 Connection — The Room Splitter

🔤 The idea for this lesson

A composite shape is made of simpler shapes joined together. To find its area, split it into rectangles and add their areas. To find its perimeter, add every outside edge; a missing length can be found from the edges on the opposite side, because together they match.

What to do: each grid square is 1 m by 1 m. Choose a room, then press a cut button to split the floor. Cuts can be added and taken away. Use Show the missing sides to check the walls marked ?.

What you are trying to notice: the pieces change when you cut in a different place. Does the total floor change?

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: practical. This mathematics is used every day by people who fit floors, build walls and plan rooms.

What it feeds: today feeds independence. Knowing how much flooring, paint or skirting a room needs means you can plan, compare prices and check a quote for yourself.

Carry one idea forward: split it into pieces you know, then add.

🛠️ Final Task — Plan a room

Your task: on squared paper or the Scratchpad, draw the floor plan of a real or imagined room that is not a plain rectangle.

  • Label every wall in metres, working out any you did not measure from the walls opposite.
  • Split the floor into rectangles in two different ways, and show both give the same area.
  • Find the perimeter, and say how many centimetres of skirting board the room needs.
  • Choose a flooring price per m² and find the cost of the floor.
  • stretch Explain why every way of splitting the floor gives the same area.

Submission choices: an annotated Scratchpad image, a photographed page, a short slideshow, or a spoken or video walkthrough.
