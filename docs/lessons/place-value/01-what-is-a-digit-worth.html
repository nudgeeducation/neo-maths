NEO Mathematics: Place Value

What is a digit worth?

Driving Question: Why is the 3 in 300 worth more than the 3 in 30?

Lesson type: Core — where a digit sits decides what it is worth.

Stepping Stone: state the value of any digit in a whole number of any size, explain why each column is worth ten times the column to its right, and read and write large numbers in words and figures.

You will need: place value up to the thousands, and the optional ✏️ Scratchpad. No calculator needed.

🌿 Connection — The Digit Value Magnifier

🔤 The idea for this lesson

A digit is one of the ten symbols 0 to 9. What a digit is worth depends on the column it stands in. In 4,073,250 the 7 is in the ten-thousands column, so it is worth 70,000. Each column is worth ten times the column to its right.

What to do: choose a number. Tap any digit to put it under the magnifier. Then slide a digit along the columns and watch what happens to its value.

What you are trying to notice: the digit itself never changes. Only its column does, and each step to the left makes it worth ten times as much.

↔️ Slide a digit

stretch Why must each column be worth ten times the next? A pattern suggests; a reason convinces.

Carry this forward: a digit's value = the digit × the value of its column. One column left: ten times as much.

🚶 Movement — Column hop

What to do: move quickly between the ways of saying one amount: column names, words and figures.

What you are trying to notice: "6 ten thousands", "sixty thousand" and 60,000 are the same amount.

🦘 Hop between forms

Carry this forward: you can type a number with or without commas, or in words.

🪞 Reflection — Read it, write it

What to do: real large numbers, written in figures and in words. Choose the match.

What you are trying to notice: splitting a number into groups of three from the right, millions | thousands | ones, makes it easy to read. Watch out for zeros holding a column open.

📖 Match figures and words

🎨 Creativity — Digit detective

What to do: use each of the digits 3, 0, 7 and 5 once to make four-digit numbers that do what each case asks.

What you are trying to notice: the same four digits make very different numbers. The largest digit in the largest column gives the largest number.

🕵️ Digits: 3 · 0 · 7 · 5

🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

Ten fingers. Ten symbols. Ten of one column make one of the next. Take a slow breath.

Notice one thing: with only ten symbols, 0 to 9, you can write any whole number at all, however large. Position does all the work.

You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: intellectual. The mathematics is the nourishment, so we name honestly what the learning feeds.

What it feeds: today feeds a sense of size. Knowing whether a number means thousands or millions helps you judge a price, a distance or a headline, and spot when something does not add up.

Carry one idea forward: before you read a big number, find its groups of three.

🛠️ Final Task — A place-value exhibition

Your task: choose a large number from real life, at least six digits long. It could be a crowd, a distance, a population or a price.

  • Write it in a place-value chart, one digit per column.
  • Write it in words.
  • State what each digit is worth.
  • Explain why the same digit can be worth different amounts in different numbers.
  • stretch Show what happens to the value of one of its digits when the number is multiplied by 10, and explain why.

Submission choices: an annotated Scratchpad image, a photographed page, a short slideshow, or a spoken or video walkthrough.
