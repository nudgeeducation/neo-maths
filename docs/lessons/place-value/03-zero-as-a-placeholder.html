Zero as a placeholder

Driving Question: When does a zero change a number?

🌿 Connection — The Zero Detective

🔤 The idea for this lesson

A zero is worth nothing, but it can hold a column open. In 4,050 the first 0 holds the hundreds column: without it, the 4 slides into the hundreds and the number becomes 450. A zero at the front of a whole number, like 0450, or at the end of a decimal, like 3.50, holds nothing and can go.

What to do: choose a number. Tap any zero to take it away, and see what is left.

What you are trying to notice: when a zero goes, do any other digits have to slide into a new column? If they do, the number changes.

🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

Nothing, holding a place. Take a slow breath.

Notice one thing: zero is the only digit that is worth nothing in every column, yet it is the one that makes large numbers possible.

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: intellectual. The mathematics is the nourishment, so we name honestly what the learning feeds.

What it feeds: today feeds care with detail. One missing zero turns £4,050 into £450. Noticing small things that matter is a skill for maths and for life.

Carry one idea forward: when you write a number, count its columns.

🛠️ Final Task — The zero guide

Your task: make a short guide that helps someone decide when a zero matters.

  • Show two numbers where taking away a zero changes the number, and explain why using a place-value chart.
  • Show two numbers where taking away a zero changes nothing, and explain why.
  • Write three numbers with zeros in words and in figures.
  • stretch Write a rule, in your own words, for when a zero can safely be left out, and test it on a number of your own.

Submission choices: an annotated Scratchpad image, a photographed page, a short slideshow, or a spoken or video walkthrough.
