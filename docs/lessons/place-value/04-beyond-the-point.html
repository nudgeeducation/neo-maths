NEO Mathematics: Place Value

Beyond the point: tenths, hundredths, thousandths

Driving Question: What lives to the right of the decimal point?

Lesson type: Core — the columns carry on past the point, each a tenth of the one before.

Stepping Stone: read and write tenths, hundredths and thousandths, say what each digit after the point is worth, and place decimals on a number line.

You will need: how digits move when you divide by 10 (Lesson 2), zero as a placeholder (Lesson 3), and the optional ✏️ Scratchpad. No calculator needed.

🌿 Connection — The Decimal Zoom

🔤 The idea for this lesson

The columns do not stop at the ones. Straight after the decimal point come tenths, then hundredths, then thousandths. Split one whole into ten equal parts and each part is a tenth. Split a tenth into ten and each part is a hundredth. Split a hundredth into ten and each part is a thousandth.

What to do: the line runs from 0 to 1. Tap a gap to zoom into it. Each zoom adds one digit after the point. Choose a Find button to hunt for a number.

What you are trying to notice: every zoom splits one gap into ten. Which column does each new digit go in?

🚶 Movement — Say it, write it

What to do: write each amount as a decimal.

What you are trying to notice: each kind of part has its own column. Empty columns before the last digit need a 0.

🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

Zoom in, and there is always more room. Take a slow breath.

Notice one thing: between 0.3 and 0.4 there are ten hundredths, and between any two of those there are ten thousandths. You could keep zooming for ever.

You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: intellectual. The mathematics is the nourishment, so we name honestly what the learning feeds.

What it feeds: today feeds precision. Sprint times are measured in hundredths of a second, and a hundredth can separate first from second. Knowing what each small part is worth helps you notice when small differences matter.

Carry one idea forward: tenths, hundredths, thousandths, each a tenth of the one before.

🛠️ Final Task — The zoom guide

Your task: make a short guide that shows where a decimal lives, by zooming in.

  • Choose a decimal with three digits after the point. Draw the three zooms that find it, labelling each line.
  • Put your decimal in a place-value chart, and say what each digit is worth.
  • Write two amounts (such as 4 tenths and 9 thousandths) as decimals, using a 0 where a column is empty.
  • stretch Explain, in your own words, why the column after the ones has to be tenths.

Submission choices: an annotated Scratchpad image, a photographed page, a short slideshow, or a spoken or video walkthrough.
