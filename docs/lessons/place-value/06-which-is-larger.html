NEO Mathematics: Place Value

Which is larger?

Driving Question: Is a longer decimal always larger?

Lesson type: Core — compare numbers column by column from the left; the first column that differs decides.

Stepping Stone: compare and order whole numbers and decimals using place value, and explain why the number of digits does not decide.

You will need: tenths, hundredths and thousandths (Lesson 4), and the optional ✏️ Scratchpad. No calculator needed.

🌿 Connection — The Column Race

🔤 The idea for this lesson

To compare two numbers, line them up on the decimal point and compare the columns from the left. The first column where the digits differ decides which number is larger. An empty column counts as 0. So 0.5 is larger than 0.45: they first differ in the tenths, and 5 tenths beats 4 tenths.

What to do: choose a pair. Predict which is larger, then press Compare next column until a column decides.

What you are trying to notice: once a column decides, do the digits after it matter?

🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

The first difference is the one that counts. Take a slow breath.

Notice one thing: dictionaries order words the same way: compare the first letters, and only look further when they match. Numbers lined up on the point work like that.

You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: intellectual. The mathematics is the nourishment, so we name honestly what the learning feeds.

What it feeds: today feeds judgement. Prices, times and measurements are compared every day, and “it has more digits” is a trap that can lead to the wrong choice. A reliable method beats a hunch.

Carry one idea forward: line up the points, then compare from the left.

🛠️ Final Task — The comparison guide

Your task: make a short guide that shows someone how to compare decimals reliably.

  • Show the method on 0.5 and 0.45, with the numbers lined up on the point.
  • Order a set of four decimals of your own, including one where the longest is not the largest.
  • Show a pair that are equal but look different, such as 0.3 and 0.30.
  • stretch Explain why the first column that differs decides, whatever digits come after.
