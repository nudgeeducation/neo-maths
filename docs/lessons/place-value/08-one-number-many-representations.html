NEO Mathematics: Place Value

One number, many representations

Driving Question: Which way of showing this number helps most?

Lesson type: Core — one number can be shown in words, digits, a chart, on a number line, as money and as a length. Each shows something the others hide.

Stepping Stone: move between representations of a number, including money (pounds and pence) and length (metres and centimetres), and choose the one that suits a job.

You will need: everything from this unit, and the optional ✏️ Scratchpad. No calculator needed.

🌿 Connection — The Place-Value Museum

🔤 The idea for this lesson

A number can wear different clothes. 3.45 is “three point four five”, a chart with 3 ones, 4 tenths and 5 hundredths, a point on a line between 3 and 4, £3.45 = 345p and 3.45 m = 345 cm. Pence are hundredths of a pound and centimetres are hundredths of a metre, so the digits keep their order: only the unit changes.

What to do: change the number in any display case: type it in digits or words, as money or as a length, press ▲ or ▼ in the chart, or slide it along the line. Watch every other case.

What you are trying to notice: which case shows each idea best: the size of the number, the value of each digit, or how to say it?

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: intellectual. The mathematics is the nourishment, so we name honestly what the learning feeds.

What it feeds: today feeds choice. Mathematicians switch between representations all the time, picking the one that makes a problem easy. Measuring lengths and angles comes next, and reading scales is the same skill.

Carry one idea forward: if one picture does not help, switch to another.

🛠️ Final Task — Your place-value museum

Your task: build a small museum for one number of your own with two digits after the point.

  • Show it in all six ways: words, digits, a chart, a number line, money and a length.
  • Write a label for each case saying what that representation shows best.
  • Include one “visitor question” that is easier to answer in one representation than another, with its answer.
  • stretch Explain why £3.45 = 345p and 3.45 m = 345 cm use the same digits, and what would change for millimetres.

Submission choices: an annotated Scratchpad image, a photographed page, a short slideshow, or a spoken or video walkthrough.
