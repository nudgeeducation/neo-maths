NEO Mathematics: Pythagoras' Theorem

Finding a Shorter Side

Driving Question: How can I use the hypotenuse and one shorter side to find the missing shorter side?

Lesson type: Worked-solution builder for finding a missing shorter side

Stepping Stones: identify the right angle; identify the hypotenuse; identify the missing shorter side; subtract the known shorter-side square from the hypotenuse square; square-root to find the missing side length.

NEO Cornerstones: Connection · Movement · Reflection · Creativity · Rest · Nutrition

45-minute shape: Connect 8 · Move 24 · Reflect/Create 6 · Rest/Nourish 3 · Evidence 4

🌱 Open Learner Guide 📘 Open Supporting Adult Guidance
🌿 Connection — Which side is missing?

What to do: look for the small right-angle marker first. Find the hypotenuse, then look for the shorter side that is missing.

What you are trying to notice: the missing shorter side can appear in different places. The structure is always: hypotenuse square minus known shorter-side square.

Orientation warm-up: find the missing shorter side

Which side is the missing shorter side?

Carry this idea forward: every worked solution begins by finding the hypotenuse, then naming the shorter side that is missing.
🚶 Movement — How do I rearrange the theorem step by step?

What to do: choose one example, then complete the seven steps in order. Use the Socratic prompt or Hint whenever the next move is unclear.

What you are trying to notice: the hypotenuse square is the largest square. To find a shorter side, subtract the known shorter-side square before taking the square root.

🧭 Shorter-Side Worked-Solution Builder

What does “Focus this step” do? It tells the Socratic Prompt and Hint buttons which step you want support with. It does not mark your answer; it simply moves the support panel to that step.
Carry this idea forward: the final answer is only secure when the route is visible: right angle → hypotenuse → subtract known square → root.
🪞 Reflection — What usually goes wrong when the unknown is not the hypotenuse?

What to do: choose one mistake below and explain how you would prevent it.

  • Choosing a side next to the right angle instead of the side opposite it.
  • Adding the known side squares even though the unknown is a shorter side.
  • Adding the two side lengths before squaring them.
  • Subtracting the side lengths instead of subtracting the square areas.
🎨 Creativity — Can I design a clear shorter-side example?

What to do: create a mini teaching card showing how to find the hypotenuse in one of the four orientations.

  1. Draw or copy the triangle.
  2. Mark the right angle.
  3. Trace to the opposite side and name the hypotenuse.
  4. Write the full worked solution.
🌙 Rest — How can I slow the rearrangement down?

You can reduce the load by using one decision at a time:

right angle → hypotenuse → missing shorter side → subtract square → root

🥣 Nutrition — What is this practice nourishing?

This lesson uses intellectual nourishment. It strengthens precision, method confidence, orientation flexibility and sense-checking.

🌱 Precision
I use the correct hypotenuse letter.
🌱 Confidence
I know where to start.
🌱 Flexibility
I can read tilted or rotated triangles.
🌱 Sense-checking
The hypotenuse must be longest.
🛠 Final Task — My shorter-side worked solution

Build and explain one complete shorter-side solution

What to do: choose one of the four examples, or design your own. Create a clear worked solution that another learner could follow.

  • identify the right angle;
  • identify the hypotenuse and the missing shorter side;
  • write the theorem, then rearrange it so the missing side square is alone;
  • show the squared values;
  • show the subtraction step;
  • square-root to find the side length;
  • include one sentence explaining why the answer makes sense.
