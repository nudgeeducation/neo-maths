NEO Mathematics: Pythagoras' Theorem

Using Pythagoras to solve geometrical problems

Driving Question: How can a hidden right triangle unlock a geometry problem?

Lesson type: Step-reveal worked example and geometry problem solving.

Stepping Stones: identify where a right triangle is hidden; decide which length is the hypotenuse; choose whether to add or subtract square areas; calculate carefully; interpret the answer in the original geometry problem.

NEO Cornerstones: Connection · Movement · Reflection · Creativity · Rest · Nutrition

🌱 Open Learner Guide 📘 Open Supporting Adult Guidance
🌿 Connection — Where is the right triangle hiding?

What to do: look at the isosceles triangle. Before pressing any solution buttons, decide what extra line could turn the problem into a right-triangle problem.

What you are trying to notice: Pythagoras is not always shown directly. Sometimes you have to create or notice a right triangle inside a larger shape.

Problem

An isosceles triangle has equal sides of 6.62 cm and base 8.22 cm.

Question: What is the height of the triangle?

Try to predict the hidden right triangle before opening the Movement section.

Carry this idea forward: to use Pythagoras in a geometry problem, first locate or create a right-angled triangle.
🚶 Movement — How is the solution built step by step?

What to do: press the reveal buttons in order. After each step, look back at the diagram and explain what changed.

What you are trying to notice: the height of the isosceles triangle becomes one side of a right triangle after the base is split in half.

🧭 Step-Reveal Worked Example: Height of an Isosceles Triangle

Carry this idea forward: the formula is not the first step. The first step is seeing the right triangle clearly.
🪞 Reflection — What decisions made the method work?

What to do: complete the reflection sentence after using the step-reveal interactive.

The key decision in this problem was...

What you are trying to notice: the calculation only makes sense because the altitude splits the isosceles triangle into two congruent right triangles.

🎨 Creativity — Can I make the hidden triangle visible?

What to do: create a mini teaching diagram for this problem.

  1. Draw the original isosceles triangle.
  2. Add the height.
  3. Label the half-base.
  4. Circle the right triangle you used.
  5. Write one sentence explaining why Pythagoras is allowed.
🌙 Rest — How can I reduce the problem to one right triangle?

Use this quiet route:

whole shape → hidden right triangle → known sides → add or subtract → answer in context

🥣 Nutrition — What is this problem nourishing?

This lesson uses intellectual nourishment. It nourishes the habit of looking for structure before calculating.

🌱 Geometric noticing
I can find a useful right triangle inside a larger shape.
🌱 Decision-making
I choose add or subtract from the role of the unknown side.
🌱 Interpretation
I return the answer to the original problem.
🌱 Confidence
I can solve a problem even when Pythagoras is not obvious at first.
🛠 Final Task — Solve one geometry problem using Pythagoras

Build one clear geometry solution

What to do: choose one problem from the Practice Companion and create a clear worked solution with a diagram.

  • show where the right triangle is hidden;
  • label the known lengths;
  • identify whether the unknown is the hypotenuse or a shorter side;
  • write the correct Pythagoras equation;
  • calculate accurately;
  • state the answer in the context of the original problem.
