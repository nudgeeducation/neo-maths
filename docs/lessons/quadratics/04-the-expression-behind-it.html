🌿 Connection — The rectangle behind the brackets

🔤 The idea for this lesson

Two lessons ago you met a quadratic as a growing sequence. Today you meet it as an expression — the symbol with an x² in it — and the picture that makes it make sense is area. The area of a rectangle is its width times its height. So (x + 2)(x + 3) is just the area of a rectangle whose sides are x + 2 and x + 3. Split that rectangle where the x ends and the numbers begin, and it falls into four pieces: a square of side x (that is x²), two thin strips (that make the x terms), and a small rectangle of the two numbers — the constant term (the term with no x in it — just a number on its own). Add the four areas and you have expanded the brackets.

This picture does two jobs. Read it forwards and you expand: sides → area. Read it backwards and you factorise: area → sides. Same rectangle, both ways.

What to do: choose the two numbers a and b, and watch the rectangle for (x + a)(x + b) split into its four areas.

What you are trying to notice: the top-left square is always x²; the two strips are the x terms; the little corner is the two numbers multiplied. The two strips join to give the middle term.

Carry this forward: (x + a)(x + b) = x² + ax + bx + ab = x² + (a + b)x + ab. The middle is added; the constant is multiplied.

🚶 Movement — Expand it

What to do: expand each product into x² + □ x + □. The middle number is a + b (added); the constant term is a × b (multiplied). Press Show the area model if a picture would help.

What you are trying to notice: once you see it as area, expanding is just "fill the four pieces and collect". You never need to remember a rule — you can rebuild it from the rectangle every time.

🪞 Reflection — Spot the slip

What to do: each card shows someone's expansion of a product. Decide whether it is right or a slip, then check.

What you are trying to notice: the two classic slips are multiplying the two numbers for the middle term (the middle is added, not multiplied), and forgetting to multiply for the constant (or forgetting the middle term altogether). The area picture catches both.

🔎 Spot the slip

Carry this forward: middle term = the two numbers added; constant = the two numbers multiplied.

🎨 Creativity — Factor Finder stretch

What to do: this is the reverse challenge, for when you are ready. You are given the area — a quadratic like x² + 7x + 12 — and you find the sides. You need two numbers that multiply to the constant term (the number on its own) and add to the middle number.

What you are trying to notice: factorising is expanding run backwards. Instead of "add for the middle, multiply for the end", you ask "which two numbers multiply to the end and add to the middle?" — and the area model gives them back to you as the sides.

🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

You have turned a pair of brackets into a picture you can see. That shift — from a rule to a reason — is worth a quiet moment. Take a breath before moving on.

Notice one thing: did seeing (x + a)(x + b) as an area change how the expanding felt — from something to remember to something you could rebuild? There is no right answer — just what your own attention noticed.

You can jot it on the Scratchpad, say it aloud, or simply hold it in mind. You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: intellectual. The rectangle is an honest mathematical picture, not the nourishment itself — so we name honestly what the learning feeds.

What it feeds: today feeds the power of a good representation — finding a picture that makes an abstract symbol make sense. x² + 5x + 6 is just marks on a page until you see it as an area; then it has parts you can point to, and expanding and factorising become the same rectangle read two ways. Great mathematicians and scientists are, more than anything, people who find the right way to see a problem. Learning that a hard idea often becomes easy once you draw it the right way is a confidence that lasts far beyond this lesson.

Carry one idea forward: when a symbol feels abstract, ask "what could this be a picture of?" — the picture usually holds the meaning.

🛠️ Final Task — Evidence for today

Your task: show that you can expand a pair of brackets using the area model — and, if you are ready, factorise one back.

  • Take a product like (x + a)(x + b). Draw its area rectangle, label the four pieces, and expand it to x² + (a + b)x + ab. Do this for two different products.
  • Explain in a sentence why the middle term is added and the constant is multiplied.
  • stretch Take a quadratic such as x² + 7x + 12 and factorise it — find the two numbers that multiply to the constant and add to the middle — showing how the area model gives back the sides.
