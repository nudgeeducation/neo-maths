NEO Mathematics: Quadratics

Stretch and reflect

Driving Question: What does the number in front of x² do to the curve?

Lesson type: Core — the first of two lessons on moving and reshaping the parabola.

Stepping Stone: describe how the coefficient a stretches the curve in y = ax², and how a negative a reflects it into a ∩.

You will need: plotting y = x² (Lesson 5), multiplying by a whole number and by a half, and the optional ✏️ Scratchpad. No calculator today.

🌿 Connection — Same curve, scaled

🔤 The idea for this lesson

Last lesson you built y = x². Today you ask what happens when you put a number in front of the x² — a coefficient we call a, as in y = ax². The quickest way to feel it is a table. For every x, the height of y = 2x² is just twice the height of y = x², and the height of y = ½x² is half of it. So a stretches every point away from the x-axis (when a > 1) or pulls it in towards the axis (when a is between 0 and 1) — but the turning point at (0, 0) never moves, because a × 0 is still 0.

What to do: fill in the heights for y = 2x² and y = ½x² (the y = x² row is given), then press Plot all three.

What you are trying to notice: all three curves share the same turning point and line of symmetry; only their steepness differs. 2x² is the narrow one; ½x² is the wide one.

▦ Compare the heights

Carry this forward: in y = ax², every height is the x² height multiplied by a. Bigger a → narrower; smaller a → wider.

🚶 Movement — The a-dial

What to do: move the dial to change a and watch y = ax² stretch, flatten and flip. The faint dashed curve is y = x² (a = 1), kept still so you can compare.

What you are trying to notice: a bigger a makes a narrower, steeper U; a smaller a (between 0 and 1) makes a wider, flatter one; and a negative a flips the whole curve upside-down into a ∩. Through it all, the turning point stays at (0, 0).

🎛️ The a-dial

a = 1

Carry this forward: a controls steepness and direction. |a| > 1 narrower · 0 < |a| < 1 wider · a < 0 opens downward. The turning point does not move.

🪞 Reflection — Read the steepness

What to do: a parabola is drawn for a secret value of a. Read its steepness and direction, and type the a you think produced it.

What you are trying to notice: a narrow curve means a large |a|; a wide one means a small |a|; a ∩ means a is negative. A common slip is to think a bigger number makes a wider curve — it is the opposite.

🔎 Name the a

y = x²    

Carry this forward: steeper = larger |a| · wider = smaller |a| · upside-down = negative a.

🎨 Creativity — Make it pass through a point stretch

What to do: choose a so that y = ax² passes exactly through a given point. Since the point (x, y) must fit the rule, you need a = y ÷ x².

What you are trying to notice: one point is enough to pin down the whole curve. Working backwards from a point to the coefficient is the seed of "fitting" a curve to data.

🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

You have taken one curve and made a whole family of them — steeper, flatter, flipped — just by changing a single number. That is worth a quiet moment. Take a breath before moving on.

Notice one thing: which felt more surprising — that a bigger a makes the curve narrower, or that a negative a turns it upside-down? There is no right answer — just what your own attention noticed.

You can jot it on the Scratchpad, say it aloud, or simply hold it in mind. You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: intellectual. The stretching curve is a mathematical idea, not the nourishment itself — so we name honestly what the learning feeds.

What it feeds: today feeds the powerful idea of a transformation — that one small change to a rule reshapes its whole picture in a predictable way. Instead of memorising dozens of separate curves, you learn one move (multiply by a) and you can picture them all. That habit — looking for the single control that governs a whole family — is how mathematicians and scientists tame complicated things: find the dial, and you understand the machine.

Carry one idea forward: when a rule has a number you can change, ask "what does that number do to the shape?" — the answer is usually one clean movement.

🛠️ Final Task — Evidence for today

Your task: show that you can describe what a does to y = ax².

  • On one grid, sketch y = x², a narrower curve and a wider curve, and label each with its value of a.
  • Sketch a curve with a negative a and explain in a sentence how it differs from the others, and what stays the same (the turning point and line of symmetry).
  • stretch Find the value of a so that y = ax² passes through a point you choose, showing that a = y ÷ x².
