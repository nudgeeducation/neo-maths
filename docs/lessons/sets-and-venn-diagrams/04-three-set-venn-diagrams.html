NEO Mathematics: Sets and Venn Diagrams

Three-set Venn diagrams

Driving Question: Where does it go when there are three?

Lesson type: Core — three sets, eight regions, and counting each one.

Stepping Stone: represent three sets on a three-set Venn diagram and use n(…) to count its regions, including “in exactly two sets” and “in at least two sets”.

You will need: Lessons 1 to 3, and the optional ✏️ Scratchpad.

NEO Cornerstones: Connection · Movement · Reflection · Creativity · Rest · Nutrition

🌱 Open Learner Guide 📘 Open Supporting Adult Guidance
🌿 Connection — Region sort

Start here: ℰ = {1, 2, …, 12}, A = {even numbers}, B = {x : x < 8} and C = {multiples of 3}. Three circles overlap inside ℰ. Place each number in its region.

What you are trying to notice: every number answers three questions (in A? in B? in C?), and those answers pick exactly one of eight regions.

📦 Region sort

🔤 The idea for this lesson

Three circles make eight regions: the centre (in all three), three regions in exactly two sets, three regions in exactly one set, and the region outside every circle. A ∩ B ∩ C is the centre. A ∩ B is everything in both A and B, so it includes the centre; A ∩ B ∩ C′ is the part of it outside C.

To place elements, or to fill in counts, start in the centre and work outwards.

Today's main activity, the Three-Set Builder, is in Creativity.

🚶 Movement — The three-circle Region Lighter

What to do: tap regions to light them, or use the buttons, and watch the notation and the listing change. Then switch to Shade the card: read the card and light exactly the right regions.

What you are trying to notice: A ∩ B lights two regions (with and without C); “exactly two” lights three regions; “at least two” adds the centre.

🪞 Reflection — From the centre out

What to do: use the clues to fill in how many are in each of the eight regions. Start in the centre. Then check: each clue turns green when your diagram fits it.

What you are trying to notice: a clue such as n(A ∩ B) = 5 counts the centre too, so the region for A and B only gets what is left.

Carry this forward: centre first, then the “exactly two” regions, then the “only” regions, then what is left outside.

🎨 Creativity — The Three-Set Builder (today's main activity)

What to do: choose a rule for each of A, B and C. Every number from 1 to 20 drops into its region. Meet each brief, then check. Most briefs have more than one answer.

What you are trying to notice: how the choice of rules decides which of the eight regions fill up, and which stay empty.

stretch Why do three sets make eight regions? A pattern suggests; a reason convinces.

🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

Everything has a place, even when there are three groups. Take a slow breath.

Notice one thing: pick three things you might enjoy on a free afternoon. Which one would sit in the centre of your own diagram?

You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: practical. Today the mathematics feeds how you find a time that works for everyone.

What it feeds: today feeds planning with several people. If each of three friends lists the evenings they are free, each list is a set. The evenings all three are free are A ∩ B ∩ C: the centre. If the centre is empty, the “exactly two” regions show the best second choices.

Carry one idea forward: start in the centre.

🛠️ Final Task — My three-set diagram

Your task: choose a universal set and three sets inside it (numbers, or something from your life, such as music, sport or places).

  • Draw the three-set Venn diagram with every element in its region, working from the centre outwards.
  • Find n(A ∩ B ∩ C), n(A ∩ B), the number in exactly two sets, and n((A ∪ B ∪ C)′).
  • Shade one region and describe it in notation and in words.
  • stretch Explain why a three-set diagram needs exactly eight regions.

Submission choices: an annotated Scratchpad image, a photographed page, a short slideshow, or a spoken or video walkthrough.
