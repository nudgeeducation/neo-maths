Rise, run and two points

Driving Question: How can two points reveal the gradient without showing the equation?

Lesson type: Calculating — finding the gradient from two points using rise and run.

Stepping Stone: find the gradient from two points — gradient = rise ÷ run = change in y ÷ change in x.

🌿 Connection — Same line, different triangle

🔤 New words for this lesson

Rise — the change in y between two points: how far up (or down) you go.
Run — the change in x between two points: how far across you go.
Gradient — rise ÷ run = change in y ÷ change in x. Written as Δy ÷ Δx ("delta" means "change in").

What to do: each line shows two different rise-and-run triangles at once — a small one and a wider one. Compare the gradient each triangle gives. Press Another line for a fresh example.

What you are trying to notice: on any one line the two triangles have different rises and runs — but rise ÷ run comes out the same every time. The gradient belongs to the line, not to the points you pick.

🔺 Same Line, Different Triangle

Carry this forward: wherever you take the two points on a straight line, rise ÷ run gives the same gradient. That is what makes a line straight.

🚶 Movement — Gradient Triangle Builder

What to do: drag point A and point B anywhere you like. The line is drawn through them, and the rise-and-run triangle appears between them. Watch how rise ÷ run gives the gradient.

What you are trying to notice: the run is how far across (change in x), the rise is how far up or down (change in y), and gradient = rise ÷ run. Move A and B closer or wider — as long as they stay on the same tilt, the gradient stays the same.

🔺 Gradient Triangle Builder

Drag the two dots. Keep them on different x positions (a run of zero has no gradient).

Carry this forward: any two points on a line give the gradient by rise ÷ run — the change in y divided by the change in x. You do not need the equation to find it.

🪞 Reflection — Right or slip?

What to do: each card shows two points and someone's working for the gradient. Decide whether the working is right or a slip, then check.

What you are trying to notice: the two classic slips are rise and run swapped (run ÷ rise instead of rise ÷ run) and a sign lost when the line falls. Rise ÷ run, in that order, with signs kept.

🔎 Right or slip?

Carry this forward: gradient is rise ÷ run — always in that order, and keep the minus sign if the line falls.

🎨 Creativity — Rate from two readings

What to do: each situation gives you two readings — like a value at two different times. Work out the rate (the gradient) as rise ÷ run, type it in, and check.

What you are trying to notice: a real rate comes from two readings, not from an equation. When the second reading is smaller, the rise is negative — so the rate is negative.

🌍 Rate from Two Readings

Make it your own: on the Scratchpad, invent a situation, give two readings, and work out its rate — is it positive or negative?

🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

Seeing that any two points give the same gradient — that rise ÷ run is fixed for a line — is a real shift in thinking. Take a breath before moving on.

Notice one thing: when you dragged A and B wider apart, both the rise and the run grew — yet the gradient stayed the same. What did it feel like to see that stay steady? There is no right answer — just what your own attention noticed.

You can jot it on the Scratchpad, say it aloud, or simply hold it in mind. You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — Where does this show up in the real world?

Mode: contextual. A gradient is a rate — and rates run the world.

Why it matters: speed is a gradient (distance per hour), a wage is a gradient (pay per hour), a phone tariff's cost per gigabyte, the price per litre at a fuel pump, a medicine dose per kilogram of body weight — all gradients. A steeper line means a faster rate; a negative gradient means something falling — a battery draining, a balance dropping, a temperature cooling. Reading how steep a line is, and which way it leans, tells you how fast something is changing and whether it is growing or shrinking.

Next time you see the word "per" — miles per hour, cost per item — you are looking at a gradient.

🛠️ Final Task — Evidence for today

Your task: find a gradient from two points.

  • Plot two points of your choosing, draw the line through them, and build the rise-and-run triangle between them. Label the rise and the run.
  • Work out the gradient as rise ÷ run, showing your working. Then take a different pair of points on the same line and show it gives the same gradient.
  • Give two readings from a real situation where the amount goes down (so the rise is negative), and work out the negative rate.

Submission choices: an annotated Scratchpad image, a written solution, a labelled poster, or a spoken/video walkthrough.
