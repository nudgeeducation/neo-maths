What you are trying to notice: a full turn is 360°, so a quarter turn clockwise ends where three quarters of a turn anticlockwise ends.

🔄 Same turn, different names

🔤 The idea for this lesson

A rotation turns a shape about a fixed point, the centre of rotation. Every point turns through the same angle, and stays the same distance from the centre. The centre itself does not move.

A rotation is fully described by three things: the centre (as coordinates), the angle and the direction. Anticlockwise is the positive direction and clockwise is negative, so −90° means 90° clockwise.

    🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

    A wheel turns, and its centre stays still. Take a slow breath.

    Notice one thing: find something near you that turns about a centre: a clock hand, a door, a lid. Where is its centre of rotation?

    🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

    Mode: noticing. Today the mathematics feeds how you see designs.

    What it feeds: many logos, wheel trims, flowers and window designs are made by rotating one piece about a centre. Counting how many times a design fits onto itself in one full turn gives its order of rotational symmetry. Animators use the same idea: a turntable lets them draw a character from every side by rotating it.

    Carry one idea forward: centre, angle, direction.

    🛠️ Final Task — Rotations described fully

    Your task: rotate a shape about a centre of your choice, twice, and describe each rotation fully.

    • Choose a centre on a grid point, and rotate a shape through 90° in one direction.
    • Rotate the same shape through a different angle or the other way.
    • For each rotation, write its full description: centre (as coordinates), angle and direction.
    • stretch Rotate an image back onto the original, and describe the rotation that does it.
