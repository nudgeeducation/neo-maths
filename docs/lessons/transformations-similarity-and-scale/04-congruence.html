NEO Mathematics: Transformations, Similarity and Scale

What transformations keep: congruence

Driving Question: What stays the same when a shape moves?

Lesson type: Core — what reflections, rotations and translations keep, and what that means.

Stepping Stone: explain why reflections, rotations and translations give congruent images, and recognise congruent shapes.

You will need: Lessons 1 to 3, and equal sides and angles — and the optional ✏️ Scratchpad.

🌿 Connection — Congruent or not?

Start here: is the green triangle exactly the same shape and size as triangle A? It might be turned, flipped or slid.

What you are trying to notice: turning, flipping and sliding never change the lengths; making it bigger, or changing its shape, does.

🔍 Congruent or not?

🔤 The idea for this lesson

Two shapes are congruent if they are exactly the same shape and the same size: every side and every angle of one matches a side and an angle of the other. One may be turned over or moved somewhere else.

Reflections, rotations and translations keep every length and every angle, so the image is always congruent to the object.

Today's main activity, the Keep or change lab, is in Reflection.

🚶 Movement — The tracing test

What to do: the red outline is a tracing of triangle A. Slide it, turn it and flip it until it fits exactly over the green shape. If it can never fit, say so. Then answer five questions.

What you are trying to notice: if a tracing fits exactly, the shapes are congruent; turning it over is allowed.

📄 The tracing test

Carry this forward: slide, turn and flip keep every length and angle.

🪞 Reflection — The Keep or change lab (today's main activity)

What to do: choose a transformation. Before you see the image, predict for each property whether it is kept or changed. Then reveal the image and the measurements, and compare.

What you are trying to notice: reflections, rotations and translations keep every length and angle (and so the area). Only a reflection turns the shape over. An enlargement keeps the angles but not the lengths.

🎨 Creativity — Match by moves

What to do: triangle ABC and a congruent triangle A′B′C′ are drawn. Choose the transformation that maps one exactly onto the other, corner to corner. Your choice is drawn dashed.

What you are trying to notice: congruent shapes can always be matched by moves that keep lengths and angles; the corners must match too.

🧩 Match by moves

🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

A shape can move a long way and still be exactly itself. Take a slow breath.

Notice one thing: find two objects near you that are congruent: two keys from the same set, two tiles, two coins. What would you check to be sure?

You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: practical. Today the mathematics feeds how things are made to fit.

What it feeds: factories make parts that must be congruent, so that any one fits where it is needed: bricks, screws, tiles, phone cases. A machine that cuts the same shape again and again is making congruent copies; a part that is “turned over” still fits a mirror-image slot, which is why left and right shoes are different.

Carry one idea forward: same lengths, same angles, same shape and size.

🛠️ Final Task — Congruent images

Your task: make three congruent images of one shape, one by each transformation, and show the evidence.

  • Reflect, rotate and translate the same shape, describing each transformation fully.
  • Measure one side and one angle on the shape and on each image.
  • Explain in a sentence why all three images are congruent to the shape.
  • stretch Say which image could be slid onto the shape without turning it over, and why.
