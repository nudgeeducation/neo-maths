NEO Mathematics: Transformations, Similarity and Scale

Similar shapes

Driving Question: Same shape, different size: how can we be sure?

Lesson type: Core — what makes two shapes similar, and how to check.

Stepping Stone: recognise similar shapes: corresponding angles are equal and corresponding sides are in the same ratio; match corresponding corners, even when a shape is turned or flipped; find the scale factor between similar shapes.

You will need: Lesson 6 (enlargement), and ratios as fractions — and the optional ✏️ Scratchpad.

🔤 The idea for this lesson

Two shapes are similar when one is an enlargement of the other, perhaps also turned or flipped: the same shape, possibly a different size.

To be sure, check both: every pair of corresponding angles is equal, and every pair of corresponding sides is in the same ratio. That ratio is the scale factor.

🚶 Movement — Same shape? Photo prints

What to do: two photo prints are shown with their sizes. Decide: similar or not similar? Then read the ratios.

What you are trying to notice: every corner of a rectangle is 90°, so for rectangles the ratios of the sides decide.

🪞 Reflection — The Similarity Checker (today's main activity)

What to do: for each pair of shapes, first match each corner of the first shape with its corresponding corner in the second; the second shape may be turned or flipped. The Checker then lines up the evidence. Decide: similar or not similar? If similar, give the scale factor.

What you are trying to notice: for shapes with four or more sides you need both tests: equal angles alone are not enough, and equal ratios alone are not enough. (Triangles are special: for them, either test on its own is enough. You will use that later in the unit.)

🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

Same shape, different size. Take a slow breath.

Notice one thing: look for two things near you that are the same shape but different sizes: two mugs from a set, a photo and its thumbnail on a screen, or a leaf and a smaller leaf from the same plant. Are they exactly similar, or only nearly?

You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: practical. Today the mathematics feeds how models and screens keep their shape.

What it feeds: a model railway at scale 1 : 76 is similar to the real train: every length is divided by 76, and every angle is the same. A widescreen picture is 16 by 9 at every size, so a phone, a laptop and a cinema screen show similar rectangles. Resizing a photo with its proportions locked keeps it similar; unlocking them stretches it.

Carry one idea forward: equal angles and one ratio for every pair of sides.

🛠️ Final Task — Similar or not: show the evidence

Your task: make a shape, a similar copy of it and a copy that is not similar, and show the evidence for each.

  • Draw a shape with four corners.
  • Make a similar copy: enlarge it, then turn or flip the image.
  • Make a copy that has the same angles but is not similar, such as a rectangle stretched in one direction.
  • For each copy, match corresponding corners, compare corresponding angles, and work out the ratio of each pair of corresponding sides.
  • stretch Explain why, for shapes with four or more sides, checking only the angles, or only the ratios, is not enough.
