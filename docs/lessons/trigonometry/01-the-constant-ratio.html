NEO Mathematics: Introduction to Trigonometry

The constant ratio

Driving Question: Why does the ratio of two sides stay the same when only the size of a right-angled triangle changes?

Lesson type: Discovering — noticing that same-angle right-angled triangles share fixed side ratios, whatever their size.

Stepping Stone: recognise that right-angled triangles with the same angle are similar, so the ratio of two chosen sides is constant.

NEO Cornerstones: Connection · Movement · Reflection · Creativity · Rest · Nutrition

🌿 Connection — Ratio Locks

🔤 New words for this lesson

Right-angled triangle — a triangle with one 90° corner. The angle we watch is marked θ ("theta").
Similar — two shapes are mathematically similar when their corresponding angles are equal and their corresponding sides are in the same ratio. One is then an exact scale-factor enlargement of the other.
Ratio — one length divided by another. Here we compare the vertical side with the horizontal side: vertical ÷ horizontal. (Next lesson we give these sides their proper names.)

What to do: drag the scale-factor slider to enlarge and shrink the triangle. Every side length is multiplied by the same scale factor, and the angle θ stays the same, so the triangle stays similar. Watch the three side lengths change — and watch the ratio vertical ÷ horizontal.

What you are trying to notice: the sides all change as you scale, but the ratio vertical ÷ horizontal locks at one value and never moves. That fixed number belongs to the angle, not the size.

🔒 Ratio Locks

Carry this forward: for a fixed angle, vertical ÷ horizontal is always the same, however big the triangle. The ratio is a property of the angle.

🚶 Movement — Stack the similar triangles

What to do: press Add a bigger one to stack another triangle on top, all sharing the same corner and the same angle θ. Each new triangle is a scaled-up copy. Work out vertical ÷ horizontal for each as it appears.

What you are trying to notice: however tall the stack grows, every triangle in it gives the same ratio. Same shape → same ratio, at any size.

📐 Stack the Similar Triangles

Carry this forward: a whole family of similar triangles shares one ratio. The ratio does not grow when the triangle does.

🪞 Reflection — Similar or not?

What to do: each card shows two right-angled triangles with their vertical and horizontal sides given. Work out vertical ÷ horizontal for each and decide whether the two triangles are similar or not similar, then check.

What you are trying to notice: two right-angled triangles are similar exactly when their ratios are equal — not when they are the same size. Equal ratio means the angle θ is equal, which is what makes them similar.

🔎 Similar or not?

Carry this forward: to tell if two right-angled triangles are similar, compare their ratios — not their sizes. Equal ratios mean equal angles.

🎨 Creativity — Find the hidden length

What to do: here is a right-angled triangle the same shape as one you know (its ratio vertical ÷ horizontal is given). You are told one side. Use the locked ratio to work out the side you cannot see. Type your answer and check.

What you are trying to notice: once you know the ratio for a shape, one measurement is enough to find the rest. You can reach a length you never measured — the first taste of what trigonometry is for.

🔦 Find the Hidden Length

Make it your own: on the Scratchpad, choose a ratio, draw a right triangle to fit it, cover one side and challenge a friend to find it.

🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

Watching everything about a triangle change — every side, its whole size — while one number stays perfectly still is a quietly beautiful thing. Take a breath before moving on.

Notice one thing: when the triangle grew and grew but the ratio did not move at all, what did that feel like? There is no right answer — just what your own attention noticed.

You can jot it on the Scratchpad, say it aloud, or simply hold it in mind. You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: intellectual. This mathematics does not live in food or the body, so we name honestly what the learning nourishes.

What it feeds: today's idea grows one of the most powerful habits in all of mathematics — looking for what stays the same while everything else changes. The triangle grew, every length shifted, yet one ratio held perfectly still. Trained to hunt for that kind of invariant, a mind starts to find hidden order everywhere: the constant that survives the chaos is often where the real understanding lives. It also feeds a quiet confidence — that careful reasoning can pin down something exact even when the picture keeps moving.

Carry one idea forward: when something feels like it is changing wildly, ask — is anything staying the same? That question is the start of a great deal of mathematics.

🛠️ Final Task — Evidence for today

Your task: show that the ratio belongs to the angle, not the size.

  • Draw a right-angled triangle, then draw a bigger one with the same angle. Measure the vertical and horizontal sides of each and work out vertical ÷ horizontal for both — show they match.
  • Explain in your own words why the ratio stays the same when the triangle is scaled up.
  • Use a known ratio to find a hidden side: given the ratio of a shape and one side of a new triangle, work out the other side, and say how you did it.

Submission choices: an annotated Scratchpad image, a written solution, a labelled poster, or a spoken/video walkthrough.
