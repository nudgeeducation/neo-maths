NEO Mathematics: Introduction to Trigonometry

Naming the sides

Driving Question: Which side is which — and why does it depend on the angle you choose?

Lesson type: Naming — giving the three sides of a right-angled triangle their proper names relative to a chosen angle.

Stepping Stone: identify the hypotenuse, opposite and adjacent relative to a marked angle θ.

NEO Cornerstones: Connection · Movement · Reflection · Creativity · Rest · Nutrition

45-minute shape: Connect 12 · Move 12 · Reflect 10 · Create 6 · Rest/Nourish 3 · Evidence 2

🌿 Connection — Side Labeller

🔤 New words for this lesson

Hypotenuse — the longest side, always opposite the right angle. It never changes, whichever acute angle you look from.
Opposite — the side across from the angle θ you are looking at (it does not touch θ).
Adjacent — the side next to θ (it touches θ) that is not the hypotenuse.

What to do: the three sides are labelled from the marked angle θ. Press Switch the angle to move θ to the other corner, and New triangle to see a different one. Watch which labels change and which stays put.

What you are trying to notice: the hypotenuse never moves — it is always opposite the right angle. But opposite and adjacent swap when you look from the other angle. Their names depend on the angle you choose.

🏷️ Side Labeller

Carry this forward: the hypotenuse is opposite the right angle (fixed). The opposite is across from θ; the adjacent is next to θ — and these two swap if you change the angle.

🚶 Movement — Label it yourself

What to do: one side of the triangle is highlighted. Looking at the marked angle θ, choose whether it is the hypotenuse, the opposite or the adjacent. A new triangle, angle and orientation come each round.

What you are trying to notice: you cannot go by position on the screen — you have to use the definitions: opposite the right angle is the hypotenuse; across from θ is the opposite; next to θ is the adjacent.

✍️ Label it Yourself

Carry this forward: name a side by its job relative to the angle and the right angle — never by where it sits on the page.

🪞 Reflection — Which side is it?

What to do: each card shows a triangle and a claim about the highlighted side. Decide whether the claim is right or a slip, then check.

What you are trying to notice: the classic slip is calling the adjacent the hypotenuse — but the hypotenuse is always opposite the right angle, not next to θ. Check each name against its definition.

🔎 Which side is it?

Carry this forward: hypotenuse = opposite the right angle; opposite = across from θ; adjacent = next to θ. Test every claim against the definition.

🎨 Creativity — Same triangle, two viewpoints

What to do: here is one triangle. Look at it first from angle A, then from angle B. Each time, read off the hypotenuse, opposite and adjacent. Fill the table and see what changes.

What you are trying to notice: the hypotenuse is the same from both viewpoints, but the opposite and adjacent swap. The very side you called "vertical" in Lesson 1 is the opposite from one angle and the adjacent from the other — it all depends on where you look from.

🔀 Same Triangle, Two Viewpoints

Make it your own: on the Scratchpad, draw a right-angled triangle and label its three sides from both acute angles — write down which two names swap and which stays the same.

🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

Realising that a side's name is not fixed to it — that "opposite" and "adjacent" depend on where you choose to look from — is a genuine shift in how you see a triangle. Take a breath before moving on.

Notice one thing: did it feel odd that the same side could have two different names? There is no right answer — just what your own attention noticed.

You can jot it on the Scratchpad, say it aloud, or simply hold it in mind. You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: intellectual. This mathematics does not live in food or the body, so we name honestly what the learning nourishes.

What it feeds: today feeds precision of language — the discipline of naming things by their exact role rather than by where they happen to sit. "Opposite" and "adjacent" are not fixed labels stuck to particular sides; they describe a relationship to the angle you are looking from. Learning to hold a definition steady and apply it — even when the picture is rotated or the answer feels counter-intuitive — is the same clear-headedness that mathematics, science and careful argument all rely on. Precise names are how mathematicians avoid talking past each other.

Carry one idea forward: before you name something, ask what is its job here? — not just where is it?

🛠️ Final Task — Evidence for today

Your task: name the sides — and show the names depend on the angle.

  • Draw a right-angled triangle, mark one acute angle θ, and label the hypotenuse, opposite and adjacent. Explain how you knew each one.
  • Now mark the other acute angle and label the three sides again. Say clearly which two names swapped and which stayed the same, and why.
  • Draw the triangle in a different orientation (rotated, so the right angle is not at the bottom) and label the sides for a marked angle — showing the names follow the angle, not the page.

Submission choices: an annotated Scratchpad image, a written solution, a labelled poster, or a spoken/video walkthrough.
