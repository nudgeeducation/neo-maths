NEO Mathematics: Introduction to Trigonometry

Sine and cosine

Driving Question: How do the other two ratios complete the picture? (SOH · CAH · TOA)

Lesson type: Defining — meeting the sine and cosine ratios, bringing in the hypotenuse, and organising all three as SOH CAH TOA.

Stepping Stone: define and use sin θ = opposite ÷ hypotenuse and cos θ = adjacent ÷ hypotenuse, alongside tan θ = opposite ÷ adjacent.

🌿 Connection — Ratio Trio (SOH · CAH · TOA)

🔤 New words for this lesson

Sine — sin θ = oppositehypotenuse  (SOH).
Cosine — cos θ = adjacenthypotenuse  (CAH).
Tangent — tan θ = oppositeadjacent  (TOA, from Lesson 3).
Hypotenuse — the longest side, opposite the right angle. Sine and cosine both use it; the tangent does not.

What to do: here is a 3–4–5 right-angled triangle with all three sides labelled. Press sin θ, cos θ or tan θ — the two sides that ratio uses light up, and its value appears as a fraction.

What you are trying to notice: each ratio is just two of the three sides, in a fixed order. SOH · CAH · TOA is the map: Sine = Opposite over Hypotenuse, Cosine = Adjacent over Hypotenuse, Tangent = Opposite over Adjacent.

🎺 Ratio Trio

Carry this forward: SOH · CAH · TOA. Sine and cosine bring in the hypotenuse; the tangent stays with opposite and adjacent.

🚶 Movement — Ratio Explorer

What to do: drag the angle slider. The hypotenuse stays the same length; watch the opposite and adjacent sides change, and watch sin θ and cos θ change with them.

What you are trying to notice: as θ grows, the opposite side grows so sin θ climbs towards 1, while the adjacent side shrinks so cos θ falls towards 0. Sine and cosine move in opposite directions — and both always sit between 0 and 1, because a side can never be longer than the hypotenuse.

📐 Ratio Explorer

Carry this forward: sin θ increases with the angle; cos θ decreases. Both are always between 0 and 1.

🪞 Reflection — Which ratio is it?

What to do: each card highlights two sides of a right-angled triangle and gives their ratio. Decide whether that ratio is sin θ, cos θ or tan θ, then check.

What you are trying to notice: the name is decided by which two sides are used: opposite & hypotenuse → sine; adjacent & hypotenuse → cosine; opposite & adjacent → tangent. Watch for the classic mix-up of sine and cosine.

🔎 Which ratio is it?

Carry this forward: SOH · CAH · TOA. Read off which two sides are used, then name the ratio.

🎨 Creativity — The other angle's cosine

What to do: here is one right-angled triangle with two acute angles, A and B. Work out sin of angle A, then cos of angle B. Look from each angle and compare.

What you are trying to notice: the side that is opposite angle A is adjacent to angle B (you saw this swap in Lesson 2). So sin A = cos B — and because A and B add to 90°, sin θ = cos(90° − θ). Sine and cosine are the same ratio, read from the two different angles.

🔗 The other angle's cosine

Make it your own: on the Scratchpad, draw any right-angled triangle, mark both acute angles, and check that the sine of one equals the cosine of the other.

🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

Three ratios, one small map — SOH · CAH · TOA — and two of them turning out to be the same idea seen from different angles. There is a quiet neatness here. Take a breath before moving on.

Notice one thing: as the angle grew, sine rose while cosine fell. Did that trade-off feel surprising, or somehow expected? There is no right answer — just what your own attention noticed.

You can jot it on the Scratchpad, say it aloud, or simply hold it in mind. You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: intellectual. This mathematics does not live in food or the body, so we name honestly what the learning nourishes.

What it feeds: today feeds the habit of organising a set of ideas into a structure you can hold in mind. Three ratios could be three things to memorise in isolation — or they could be one tidy pattern, SOH · CAH · TOA, where each name tells you exactly which two sides to use. Learning to compress information into a memorable structure, and to notice hidden symmetry (that sine and cosine are the same ratio read from the two angles), is what turns a pile of facts into understanding. It also feeds trust in system over recall: when you can rebuild a fact from its structure, you never truly forget it.

Carry one idea forward: a good structure means you remember less and understand more.

🛠️ Final Task — Evidence for today

Your task: show that you can define and use all three ratios.

  • Draw a right-angled triangle, mark an angle θ, label the opposite, adjacent and hypotenuse, and work out sin θ, cos θ and tan θ. Show each as a fraction.
  • Explain, using SOH · CAH · TOA, which two sides each ratio uses and why the hypotenuse appears in two of them but not the tangent.
  • Mark the other acute angle and show that its cosine equals the sine of the first angle — explain why, using the idea that opposite and adjacent swap between the two angles.

Submission choices: an annotated Scratchpad image, a written solution, a labelled poster, or a spoken/video walkthrough.
