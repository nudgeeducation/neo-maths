NEO Mathematics: Introduction to Trigonometry

Finding a side — when the unknown is in the denominator

Driving Question: What changes when the unknown is in the denominator of the ratio?

Lesson type: Applying — solving the ratio equation when the unknown side sits in the denominator (the bottom of the fraction), which changes the rearrangement.

Stepping Stone: when substituting gives ratio = known ÷ x (the unknown in the denominator), rearrange in two steps to get x = known ÷ ratio — the answer is a division, not a multiplication.

You will need: a calculator in degrees — use the 🧮 Calculator button in the bottom-right of the screen (it is already set to degrees), or your own. A √ Symbols palette sits under each answer box too.

🌿 Connection — Numerator or denominator?

🔤 The idea for this lesson

Same four lines as last lesson — write the ratio, substitute, rearrange, evaluate — and still no rule to memorise. The only new thing is where x lands when you substitute — in the numerator (the top of the fraction) or the denominator (the bottom):

x in the numerator (last lesson):  sin θ = x8  → multiply both sides by 8  → x = 8 × sin θ.
x in the denominator (today):  sin θ = 6x  → multiply both sides by x, then divide by sin θ  → x = 6 ÷ sin θ.

x is in the denominator whenever you are finding the hypotenuse (with sin or cos), or the adjacent (with tan) — the known side is in the numerator. Same algebra thinking; this time it ends in a division. For line 4, open the 🧮 Calculator (bottom-right).

What to do: the triangle shows the marked angle, one known side, and the unknown x (which will be in the denominator). Press sin, cos or tan, then reveal the working one line at a time and watch the two-step rearrangement.

What you are trying to notice: after you substitute, look at where x is. In the denominator means you multiply by x and then divide — the answer is known ÷ ratio.

🧩 Numerator or denominator?

Carry this forward: substitute, then look at x. In the numerator → multiply. In the denominator → multiply by x, then divide (x = known ÷ ratio).

🚶 Movement — Side Finder (rearrange)

What to do: build the solution line by line. Choose the ratio (that writes lines 1 and 2). Then type the rearrangement that gets x on its own — here x is in the denominator, so you will divide. Finally work out the answer with your calculator (to 1 d.p.).

What you are trying to notice: line 2 is ratio = known ÷ x. Multiply both sides by x to get ratio × x = known, then divide both sides by the ratio: x = known ÷ ratio. Same equation-solving, ending in a division.

📐 Side Finder (rearrange)


Carry this forward: x in the denominator → multiply by x, then divide by the ratio. The equation tells you what to do.

🪞 Reflection — Multiply or divide?

What to do: each card shows a substituted equation — the known number and x arranged as a fraction. Look at where x is and decide how to finish: x = known × ratio or x = known ÷ ratio. Then check.

What you are trying to notice: do not pattern-match on the last lesson. If x is in the numerator, you multiply; if x is in the denominator, you divide. The position of x in the equation decides it — every time.

🔎 Multiply or divide?

Carry this forward: look at where x sits. In the numerator → multiply; in the denominator → divide. Read it from the equation, not from memory.

🎨 Creativity — Reach the unmeasurable (the long way)

What to do: each scenario needs a hypotenuse — the sloping length you cannot measure directly: a ladder to a window, a zip-wire, a ramp. You are given a height (or drop) and an angle. Set it up and find the sloping length.

What you are trying to notice: when the unknown is the hypotenuse, it lands in the denominator — so you divide. Same power as before, reaching a length you could never stretch a tape along.

🪜 Reach the unmeasurable (the long way)

Make it your own: on the Scratchpad, invent a sloping length you cannot reach — a ladder, a wire, a ramp — with a height and an angle, then find it by dividing.

🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

The whole method stayed the same — you just watched where x landed and let the equation tell you whether to multiply or divide. That steadiness, of trusting the algebra instead of a remembered rule, is worth a quiet moment. Take a breath before moving on.

Notice one thing: did seeing that "a denominator means divide" fall out of the equation — rather than being a new rule to learn — feel reassuring? There is no right answer — just what your own attention noticed.

You can jot it on the Scratchpad, say it aloud, or simply hold it in mind. You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: intellectual. The ladders and wires are honest problem settings, not the nourishment itself — so we name honestly what the learning feeds.

What it feeds: today feeds transfer — the ability to meet a situation that looks new and recognise it as something you already understand, seen from a different angle. A learner who memorised "multiply" last lesson is stuck the moment x moves in the denominator; a learner who understood the equation simply reads off what to do. This is one of the deepest habits in mathematics and in thinking generally: reducing an apparently new problem to principles you already trust, rather than collecting an ever-longer list of special cases. It also feeds self-trust — the calm that comes from knowing you can reason your way through, not just remember.

Carry one idea forward: when something looks new, ask "what do I already know that this is a version of?"

🛠️ Final Task — Evidence for today

Your task: show that you can find a side when the unknown is in the denominator.

  • Take a right-angled triangle where the unknown is the hypotenuse. Write the ratio, substitute (unknown = x, which lands in the denominator), rearrange in two steps — multiply both sides by x, then divide by the ratio — and evaluate. Show all four lines.
  • Do a case where the unknown is the adjacent, found from the opposite using the tangent (again x in the denominator).
  • Explain, in your own words, how you know whether to multiply or divide — using the position of x in the equation, not a remembered rule. Include one on-top and one in the denominator example side by side.

Submission choices: an annotated Scratchpad image, a written solution, a labelled poster, or a spoken/video walkthrough.
