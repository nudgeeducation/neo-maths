NEO Mathematics: Introduction to Trigonometry

Putting it together

Driving Question: Where does right-triangle trigonometry show up, and how do I choose my method?

Lesson type: Consolidating — bringing the whole unit together and, above all, deciding which method a problem needs.

Stepping Stone: for any right-angled-triangle problem, diagnose it — am I finding a side or an angle? which ratio do the sides make? — then solve it with the matching method.

You will need: a calculator in degrees (with the inverse keys) — use the 🧮 Calculator button in the bottom-right, or your own. A √ Symbols palette sits under each answer box too.

🌿 Connection — Side or angle?

🔤 The idea for this lesson

You now have every tool for a right-angled triangle: SOH · CAH · TOA, finding a side, and finding an angle. The one skill left is choosing — reading a problem and deciding which method it needs. Every right-triangle problem answers the same two questions first:

1. Am I finding a side or an angle?  Look at what is unknown. If the angle is given and a length is missing, you are finding a side. If two lengths are given and the angle is missing, you are finding an angle.
2. Which ratio do the sides make?  Opposite & hypotenuse → sine; adjacent & hypotenuse → cosine; opposite & adjacent → tangent.

Once those two are answered, the method follows. Finding a side: write the ratio, substitute, rearrange, evaluate. Finding an angle: write the ratio, substitute, apply the inverse, evaluate. The 🧮 Calculator (bottom-right) has sin, cos, tan and their inverses, in degrees.

What to do: for each problem, the triangle shows what is given. Decide first a side or an angle?, then which ratio the sides make — and reveal the full method.

What you are trying to notice: the hard part of trigonometry is rarely the arithmetic — it is diagnosis. Naming the two questions turns every new problem into one you have already met.

🧭 Side or angle?

Carry this forward: two questions first — side or angle? then which ratio? The method follows from the answers.

🚶 Movement — Trig Problem Studio

What to do: each problem could be either kind. Work through the diagnosis yourself: Step 1 — a side or an angle?  Step 2 — which ratio?  Then the set-up appears and you work out the answer on your calculator.

What you are trying to notice: mixing side and angle problems forces the real skill — you can no longer run one method on autopilot. Read, diagnose, then solve.

🛠️ Trig Problem Studio

Carry this forward: diagnose first — side or angle, then the ratio — and the method chooses itself.

🪞 Reflection — Spot the flaw

What to do: each card shows a triangle and a complete worked solution — for a side or an angle. Read it critically and decide: is it right, or is there a flaw? Then check.

What you are trying to notice: the flaws are the whole unit's classic errors gathered in one place — the wrong ratio, multiplying when you should divide (unknown in the denominator), and forgetting the inverse (giving a ratio like 0.75 as the angle). Judging finished work is how you learn to check your own.

🔎 Spot the flaw

Carry this forward: check every solution against the two questions — right ratio? right method for a side or an angle? — before trusting the number.

🎨 Creativity — Reveal it in the world

What to do: each scenario is a real object — a ramp, a ladder, a roof, a zip-wire. Some ask for a missing length, some for a missing angle. Diagnose which, set it up and solve it with your calculator.

What you are trying to notice: the same two triangle questions unlock the built world — the height a ladder reaches, the drop of a zip-wire, the pitch of a roof — from measurements you could actually take.

🌍 Reveal it in the world

Make it your own: on the Scratchpad, invent a real problem — decide whether it needs a side or an angle, then solve it and show every line.

🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

You have gathered a whole unit into a single habit: ask two questions, then solve. That is worth a moment's rest — the pieces have joined up. Take a breath before moving on.

Notice one thing: which of the two questions — side or angle? or which ratio? — feels most automatic now, and which still needs a moment's thought? There is no right answer, just what your own attention noticed.

You can jot it on the Scratchpad, say it aloud, or simply hold it in mind. You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: intellectual. The ramps and ladders are honest problem settings, not the nourishment itself — so we name honestly what the learning feeds.

What it feeds: today feeds the skill of diagnosis — reading a problem and deciding which tool it needs before reaching for one. This is what separates knowing techniques from being able to use them. A learner who can find a side and find an angle but cannot tell which a problem calls for is still stuck; a learner who asks the two questions first is unstuck for good. Diagnosis is the quiet, transferable half of mathematics: choosing the method matters as much as running it, in trigonometry and far beyond it.

Carry one idea forward: before you calculate, ask what kind of problem this is — the right question chooses the method for you.

🛠️ Final Task — Evidence for today

Your task: show that you can diagnose and solve any right-angled-triangle problem, choosing the method for yourself.

  • Solve one finding-a-side problem and one finding-an-angle problem. For each, state the two diagnosis questions first — side or angle? and which ratio? — then show every line of the method.
  • Include one problem where the unknown is in the denominator (so you divide rather than multiply), and explain how you knew.
  • Solve one real-world problem (a ramp, a ladder, a roof or a zip-wire), naming which kind it is and why.
  • Write a short checklist another learner could follow to decide, for any problem, which method to use.

Submission choices: an annotated Scratchpad image, a written solution, a labelled poster, or a spoken/video walkthrough.
