# NEO Mathematics — Curriculum Overview: Intermediate (Year 10)
**Pathway:** Intermediate (internal stage code Y10 — iGCSE Year 1) · **Academic year:** 2026–27
**Rhythm:** 3 × 45-minute lessons per week × 38 weeks = 114 guided lesson opportunities (GreenPrint entry v2.2). Spring and Summer 2026–27 give 66 lessons: 15, 15, 18 and 18 by half-term. Each remaining unit is sized to fit inside one half-term.
**Qualification overlay:** Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Mathematics A (4MA1), Higher tier, which assumes all Foundation tier content. The overlay informs the route; it does not define it.
**Sources:** Lesson Architecture Map GreenPrint v0.4, the 4MA1 specification and the iGCSE alignment map for the planned route; the live site and Google Classroom for the built units. Where they differ, the built lessons are the record.
**Status (v1.0, 14 October 2026):** Confirmed by Gerry Docherty, curriculum lead. Unit order matches Google Classroom and the live site. Autumn is built and live; Spring and Summer are planned to the 2026–27 term dates; lesson counts for planned units may flex when each unit is blueprinted.

**Planned:** 66 lessons, 15 + 15 + 18 + 18 across the four Spring and Summer half-terms.
**Changes from the Architecture Map:** Circle Theorems replaces the Connected Maths Performance Project for now; Bounds is cut to 3 lessons and moved next to the number units; Transformations, Vectors and Similarity, Sets and Venn Diagrams, and Probability are new units built on Higher tier content.

## Autumn term — built and live

### 1 · Straight Lines and Connected Representations (10 lessons)
A line as a relationship, seen as a table, a graph, an equation and a story.
A line as a relationship · A table can predict a line · Where does the line cross the y-axis? · How fast is the line changing? · Rise, run and two points · Can I write the line? · Parallel stories of change · When two lines meet · Conversion graphs as models · What can I now explain about a line?

### 2 · Introduction to Trigonometry (8 lessons)
The constant ratios inside right-angled triangles, and how they measure the unreachable.
The constant ratio · Naming the sides · The tangent ratio · Sine and cosine · Finding a side · Finding a side — when the unknown is in the denominator · Finding an angle · Putting it together

### 3 · Equations, Inequalities and Simultaneous Relationships (11 lessons)
Keeping the balance, and what it means when two relationships hold at once.
Keeping the balance · Both sides, and brackets · Equations with fractions · Forming an equation · A line is a set of solutions · Where two lines meet · Solving by substitution · Solving by elimination · Forming and solving a pair · Inequalities on a number line · Inequalities as regions

### 4 · Circle Theorems (10 lessons)
A circle looks featureless — so where do all its fixed angle rules come from, and how do I use and prove them?
The circle and its parts · The angle at the centre · The angle in a semicircle · Angles in the same segment · Cyclic quadrilaterals · Tangents to a circle · The alternate segment theorem · The perpendicular from the centre to a chord · Combining the theorems · Proof and problem-solving

## Spring term — planned

**Spring HT1 · 15 lessons**

### 5 · Number Systems and Calculator Judgement (7 lessons)
How do numbers really behave, and when can I trust my calculator?
Fractions, decimals and percentages fluently · Recurring decimals as fractions · Order of operations and calculator judgement · Negative and fractional indices · Surds: meaning and simplifying · Surds: manipulating and rationalising · Estimating and judging reasonableness
4MA1 1.1–1.3, 1.11; Higher 1.3A, 1.4A–C, 2.1A.

### 6 · Algebraic Expressions and Identities (8 lessons)
When are two expressions the same, and how do I reshape one into another?
Expressions, equations, formulae and identities · Expanding and factorising · Expanding three brackets · Simplifying algebraic fractions · Adding and subtracting algebraic fractions · Changing the subject, harder cases · Algebraic proof · Function notation, domain and range
4MA1 2.1–2.3; Higher 2.2A, C, E, 2.3A, 3.2A–C. Composite and inverse functions (3.2D) are left for Year 11.

**Spring HT2 · 15 lessons**

### 7 · Percentages, Ratio and Growth Models (9 lessons)
How do quantities share, scale and grow?
Ratio and sharing · Proportion and the unitary method · Percentages as multipliers · Percentage change · Reverse percentages · Repeated percentage change · Compound interest and depreciation · Direct and inverse proportion · Growth models
4MA1 1.6–1.7; Higher 1.6A–B, 2.5A.

### 8 · Bounds (3 lessons)
How exact is a measurement, and how far can an answer drift?
Bounds of a rounded value · Calculating with bounds · Bounds in problems
4MA1 1.8C; Higher 1.8A.

### 9 · Sets and Venn Diagrams (3 lessons)
How do sets organise information and help us count?
Set notation and Venn diagrams · Subsets and sets defined algebraically · Venn diagram problems
4MA1 1.5; Higher 1.5A–D. Year 9 meets the Foundation tier set content in its Probability unit.

## Summer term — planned

**Summer HT1 · 18 lessons**

### 10 · Transformations, Vectors and Similarity (9 lessons)
How can transformations, vectors and scale factors become tools for solving problems?
Reconnect: the four transformations, described fully · Combining transformations · Vectors: column vectors and scalar multiples · Adding vectors, resultants and magnitude · Proving geometric results with vectors · Transforming graphs: y = f(x) + a and y = f(x + a) · Transforming graphs: stretches and reflections · Similar shapes: the area scale factor · Similar shapes: the volume scale factor and problems
4MA1 5.2 (assumed Foundation content); Higher 5.1A–G, 3.3B–C, 4.11A–C.
**How this differs from Year 9:** the iGCSE has no extra transformation content at Higher tier (5.2 says "See Foundation Tier"). Year 9 performs and describes single transformations; Year 10 reconnects to that in one lesson, then uses transformations as tools for the Higher tier content that builds on them: vectors, transforming graphs, and area and volume of similar shapes. The graph lessons extend Year 9 Quadratics Lessons 6–7.

### 11 · Frequency, Scatter and the Shape of Data (9 lessons)
What is the shape of a data set, and how do we compare two?
Representing data · Averages from grouped data · Scatter graphs and correlation · Histograms with unequal class widths · Reading histograms · Cumulative frequency diagrams · Median and interquartile range from cumulative frequency · Measures of spread · Comparing distributions
4MA1 6.1–6.2; Higher 6.1A–C, 6.2A–D.

**Summer HT2 · 18 lessons**

### 12 · Quadratics: Factors, Roots and Graphs (12 lessons)
What do the roots of a quadratic tell us, and how many ways can we find them?
Expanding and factorising x² + bx + c · Factorising ax² + bx + c · Solving by factorisation · Forming quadratics from a context · Completing the square · Solving by completing the square · The quadratic formula · Choosing a method · Quadratic graphs: roots, intercepts and turning points · Linear and quadratic simultaneous equations · Quadratic inequalities · Putting it together
4MA1 2.2F, 2.7A; Higher 2.2B, D, 2.7A–D, 2.8A, 3.3E.

### 13 · Probability (3 lessons)
How do we combine chances, and what changes when one event affects another?
Tree diagrams · Independent events · Conditional probability
Higher 6.3A–D. Foundation probability is a Reconnection Route to Year 9 Unit 10.
