## Unit Driving Question
When are two expressions really the same, and how do I reshape one into another?

## The mathematical story
Two expressions can look completely different and still always give the same value: (*x* + 3)² and *x*² + 6*x* + 9 agree for **every** *x*. That is an **identity**, written with ≡. The unit is about seeing that sameness, and moving between shapes on purpose.

It begins by sorting the four kinds of statement: an **expression** (a value), an **equation** (true for particular values), a **formula** (linking quantities that vary) and an **identity** (true for every value). Testing many values suggests an identity, and expanding proves it.

Next comes **reshaping**. **Expanding** turns a product into a sum; **factorising** turns a sum back into a product, seen as the same rectangle drawn two ways. Three brackets extend this to a box: (*x* + 1)(*x* + 2)(*x* + 3) is a volume.

**Algebraic fractions** follow, where the rules of number fractions still hold. Learners cancel only **common factors**, never common terms: (*x* + 3)/3 is not *x* + 1. They then find common denominators, exactly as with 2/3 + 1/4.

With those tools, **harder rearranging** becomes possible. When the subject appears twice, collect its terms and factorise it out; when it appears as a square, undo it with a root.

The unit then asks the deepest question: how can we be **sure** something is true for every number? **Algebraic proof** answers it. Writing an odd number as 2*n* + 1, and three consecutive integers as *n*, *n* + 1, *n* + 2, turns a pattern into a certainty.

It ends with **functions**: a rule as a machine that maps inputs to outputs, written f(*x*). Some inputs must be **excluded**, such as values that would divide by zero or take the square root of a negative number. This links back to Number Systems.

**Arc:** four kinds of statement → expanding and factorising → three brackets → cancelling safely → adding algebraic fractions → the subject appearing twice → proving for every number → functions, domains and ranges.

## Stepping Stones
- Distinguish **expressions, equations, formulae and identities**, using ≡, and show an identity is true by expanding.
- **Expand** single and double brackets and **factorise** by taking out common factors and as *x*² + *bx* + *c*.
- **Expand three brackets** and simplify.
- **Simplify algebraic fractions** by factorising and cancelling common factors only.
- **Add and subtract algebraic fractions** using a common denominator.
- **Change the subject** of a formula where the subject appears twice, or as a power.
- Construct simple **algebraic proofs**, using 2*n*, 2*n* + 1 and consecutive integers.
- Use **function notation**, and find **domains and ranges**, including values that must be excluded.

## Notes for building
- **Contexts** should be calm and age-respectful: areas and volumes of real objects, costs and fees, speed and time, phone and data plans, number puzzles and magic tricks (for proof).
- **Vocabulary** (GreenPrint v2.0): use *expression, equation, formula, identity, term, coefficient, factor, expand, factorise, simplify, subject, function, domain, range* precisely, with ≡ for identities. Say *numerator* and *denominator* throughout. The word may appear once as a gloss.
- **Proof** (the "pattern, then proof" principle): every lesson where a rule is spotted from examples includes a short "Why must it be?" stretch. Lesson 7 makes proof the main aim. Be honest that testing values only suggests; expanding or reasoning proves.
- **Calculators** are not needed in this unit; the point is structure.
- **Answer checking** (QA gate 6a) must accept every equivalent form. An **algebraic-expression checker** will be built once in Lesson 1 and reused:
  - it compares expressions by value at several test points, not by surface form;
  - it accepts any order of terms and implicit multiplication (2*x*, 2 × *x*, 2\**x*);
  - it reports separately whether an answer is fully expanded, fully factorised or fully simplified, where that is the task.
- **Symbols palette** on every in-lesson answer box that needs symbols (², ³, ≡, fraction, brackets), using the `neo-mathfield` class.
- **Rubric labels:** Approaching Proficiency · Developing Proficiency · Proficiency · Refining Proficiency.
- **Graphs** in any resource follow GreenPrint v2.3 (numbered, labelled axes).
- **Field kit:** add the new unit to `FIELDKIT_UNITS` and to `scripts/fieldkits/make_fieldkits.js` (from the Lighthouse) when the first lesson is built.
- **Year 9 distinction:** no lesson repeats a Year 9 objective as its main aim. Lesson 1 revisits the four kinds of statement only to introduce ≡ and proof by expanding; Lesson 2 uses the Year 9 area model as a starting point for factorising.
