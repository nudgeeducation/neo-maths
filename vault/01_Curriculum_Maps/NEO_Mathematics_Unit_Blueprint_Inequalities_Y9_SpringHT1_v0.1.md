# NEO Mathematics — Unit Blueprint: Inequalities (Mathematics with Boundaries)
**Pathway:** Foundations (internal stage code Y9) · **Term:** Spring, Half-Term 1 — first of two HT1 units (followed by Formulae and Rearranging).
**Shape:** 4 lessons · 45 minutes each · with Formulae and Rearranging (6 lessons) this fills the 10-lesson Spring HT1 (2 lessons a week × 5 weeks; GreenPrint entry v2.2). *(May flex from evidence, per the responsive envelope.)*
**Qualification overlay:** Pearson Edexcel International GCSE (4MA1) Foundation tier **2.8A–E**: the symbols <, >, ≤, ≥, including double-ended inequalities; open and closed intervals on a number line; solving simple linear inequalities in one variable and representing the solution set on a number line; representing and identifying regions on Cartesian graphs defined by simple linear inequalities. *Conventions for including boundaries on graphs are not required at Foundation tier.* **Out of scope:** quadratic inequalities and harder regions (Higher 2.8A–B), which Year 10 meets.
**Strand:** Algebra — builds on **ordering numbers, including negatives**, **one- and two-step equations** and **coordinates in four quadrants**. These are exactly the three checks in the unit's diagnostic, *The Walled Garden*, plus one on whole numbers between two bounds.
**Relationship to Year 10:** Intermediate meets this content in two lessons of its Equations unit (Lessons 10–11). Foundations takes four, with more time on meaning and representation before procedure, and reuses those lessons' number-line and region tools in a simpler form.

## Unit Driving Question
When the answer isn't a single number, how do we describe all the numbers that work?

## The mathematical story
Most of the mathematics learners have met so far has a single answer: *x* = 5, the missing angle is 40°. But everyday life is full of **boundaries** rather than single values: you must be *at least* 12 to ride, a bag can weigh *at most* 23 kg, a room should be *between* 18 °C and 22 °C. Each of these describes not one number but a whole **set** of allowed values, and the mathematics of boundaries is the mathematics of **inequalities**.

The unit starts with **meaning**. The four symbols <, >, ≤ and ≥ say how two quantities compare, and the crucial decision at every boundary is whether the boundary value itself is **included**. Double-ended inequalities such as −1 < *x* ≤ 3 trap a value between two boundaries. Learners decide, number by number, which values get through, and see the set those decisions build.

It then moves to **solving**: an inequality can be solved like an equation, by keeping the balance, with one surprise to *discover* rather than memorise. Multiplying or dividing both sides by a negative number reverses the direction of the inequality.

Next comes **representation on a number line**: an open circle when the boundary is excluded, a filled circle when it is included, and an arrow or a segment for the rest of the set. Learners move fluently between words, symbols and the picture.

Finally, inequalities move into **two dimensions**: *x* > 2 on a graph is not a point or a range but a whole **region** of the plane, bounded by a line. Combining two or three simple inequalities picks out the region where all of them hold. This is the same idea as the combined loci in the Constructions unit.

**Arc:** compare and decide (meaning, symbols, inclusion) → solve, keeping the balance → picture the solution set on a number line → see the solution set as a region on a graph.

## Stepping Stones
Read and use the symbols <, >, ≤ and ≥, including **double-ended** inequalities · decide whether a **boundary value is included**, and list the **whole numbers** that satisfy an inequality · translate a **real-world boundary** into an inequality and back · **solve** a linear inequality by doing the same to both sides, and explain why multiplying or dividing by a negative **reverses** the inequality · represent a solution set on a **number line** with **open** and **closed** circles, and read an inequality from a number line · represent and identify **regions** on a graph defined by simple linear inequalities such as *x* > 2, *y* ≤ 3 and *x* + *y* < 5.

## Notes for building
- **Contexts** should be calm and age-respectful: temperature ranges, luggage limits, ride heights, safe speeds, opening hours.
- **Negatives** appear from Lesson 1, because comparing negatives is the most common prerequisite gap (the Walled Garden's first lock).
- **The sign-reversal** in Lesson 2 must be *seen*, not stated: for example, −2 < 3 becomes 2 > −3 after multiplying by −1, shown on a number line as a reflection about 0.
