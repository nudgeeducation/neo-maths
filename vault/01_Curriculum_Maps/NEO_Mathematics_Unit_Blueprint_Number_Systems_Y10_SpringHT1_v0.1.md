## Unit Driving Question
How do numbers really behave, and when can I trust my calculator?

## The mathematical story
Learners have used fractions, decimals, powers and calculators for years, often as separate tools. This unit asks what these numbers really **are**, and how they **connect**.

It begins with **fluency across forms**. One number can be a fraction, a decimal and a percentage, and moving between them is a matter of choice, not a trick. That leads to a surprise: some fractions give decimals that **never end**, yet always **repeat**. Every recurring decimal turns out to be a fraction, which a little algebra can find (10*x* − *x*).

Next comes **order and judgement**. A calculator follows the order of operations exactly, so it is only as good as the keys pressed. Learners compare what they meant with what they typed, and learn to predict and check calculator output.

**Powers** then stretch beyond whole numbers. Continuing the pattern 2³, 2², 2¹, 2⁰ shows what 2⁻¹ must mean. Asking which number multiplied by itself gives 9 shows what 9^½ must mean. The index laws already known make these meanings unavoidable rather than arbitrary.

**Surds** follow. √2 cannot be written exactly as a decimal, so mathematicians keep it as √2. Learners simplify surds by finding square factors (√72 = 6√2), seen as squares on a grid. They then calculate with them, expanding brackets and **rationalising** a denominator so that no surd is left in the denominator.

The unit ends with **judging reasonableness**: estimating before calculating, checking the size and sign of an answer, and catching the slips a calculator cannot.

**Arc:** one number, many forms → decimals that never end → order and the calculator → what powers really mean → exact roots as surds → calculating with surds → is my answer about right?

## Stepping Stones
- Move fluently between **fractions, decimals and percentages**, and calculate with fractions and mixed numbers.
- Explain why some fractions give **recurring decimals**, and convert a recurring decimal to a **fraction**.
- Apply the **order of operations**, including brackets, powers and fractions, and use a **scientific calculator** correctly, predicting and checking its output.
- Understand and use **zero, negative and fractional indices**, explaining their meaning from the index laws.
- Explain what a **surd** is and **simplify** surds using square factors.
- **Calculate with surds**, including expanding brackets, and **rationalise** a denominator.
- **Estimate** answers and **judge** whether a result is reasonable, including calculator results.

## Notes for building
- **Contexts** should be calm and age-respectful: recipes and scaling, measurements, money and interest, music (frequency ratios), screen sizes and aspect ratios (√2 in paper sizes: A4).
- **Vocabulary** (GreenPrint v2.0): use *terminating, recurring, rational, irrational, surd, index (indices), reciprocal, rationalise* precisely. Say "the reciprocal" for the meaning of a power of −1, not "flip it". The word may appear once as a gloss.
- **Calculators** are used deliberately in Lesson 3 and Lesson 7, and kept away in Lessons 2, 4, 5 and 6, where exact forms are the point.
- **Answer checking** (QA gate 6a) must accept every equivalent form:
  - fractions, decimals and percentages;
  - recurring notation (0.3̇, 0.333…, 1/3);
  - surds in any equivalent simplified form (2√3, √12 only where simplification is not the task);
  - index forms (2⁻³, 1/8, 0.125).

  The surd and index checker will be built once and reused across the unit.
- **Graphs** in any resource follow GreenPrint v2.3 (numbered, labelled axes).
- **Field kit:** add `number-systems` to `FIELDKIT_UNITS` and to `scripts/fieldkits/make_fieldkits.js` (from the Observatory) when the first lesson is built.
- **Year 9 distinction:** no lesson repeats a Year 9 objective as its main aim. Estimation and the order of operations reappear only at Higher level, with surds, indices and calculator output.
