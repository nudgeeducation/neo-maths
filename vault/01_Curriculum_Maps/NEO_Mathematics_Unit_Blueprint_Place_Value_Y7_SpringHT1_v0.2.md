## Unit Driving Question
Can the same digit represent completely different amounts?

## The mathematical story
A digit on its own means little: the 3 in 300 and the 3 in 0.03 are worlds apart. **Position gives a digit its value**, and every column is worth **ten times** the column to its right. That single idea explains everything in the unit.

It starts with **whole numbers of any size**, reading and writing them, and saying exactly what each digit is worth. Then it shows what happens when a number is **multiplied or divided by 10, 100 or 1000**: the digits move to new columns, and the decimal point stays where it is. Learners see this happen, so they never need the misleading rule "add a zero".

**Zero as a placeholder** comes next: 4050 and 450 differ by a zero that is doing real work. The columns then continue to the right of the point, into **tenths, hundredths and thousandths**, shown on a number line that zooms in by a factor of ten each time.

With the columns secure, numbers become flexible. A number can be **partitioned** in many ways (3.47 is 3 + 0.4 + 0.07, but also 34.7 tenths). Numbers can be **ordered** reliably, and the common trap that "a longer decimal is larger" (0.45 against 0.5) is met head on. **Rounding** then asks which multiple of 10, 100 or 0.1 a number is nearest to, using the number line, not a rule.

It ends by moving freely between **representations**: words, digits, a place-value chart, the number line, money and measures.

**Arc:** what a digit is worth → ten times bigger and smaller → zero holding a place → beyond the point → many ways to split a number → which is larger → nearest to → one number, many representations.

## Stepping Stones
- State the **value of any digit** in an integer of any size, and read and write large numbers in words and figures.
- Explain the **tenfold relationship** between neighbouring columns, and multiply and divide by **10, 100 and 1000**.
- Use **zero as a placeholder**, and explain when a zero changes a number and when it does not.
- Use **tenths, hundredths and thousandths**, and place decimals on a number line.
- **Partition** and recombine numbers, including decimals, in more than one way.
- **Order and compare** integers and decimals, using <, > and =.
- **Round** to the nearest 10, 100, 1000, whole number and one decimal place.
- **Move between representations** of the same number and choose the clearest.

## Notes for building
- **Measures:** because geometry comes next on the January route, Lessons 4, 7 and 8 use measurement contexts (centimetres and millimetres, angles in degrees, metres and kilometres), so that reading scales and decimals is fresh for the angle and area units.
- **Age and tone:** this is likely the first unit a Year 7 learner meets at NEO, possibly after an interrupted primary education. Keep reading load light, give every idea a picture first, and never imply that this "should already be known". Learner-facing text says Foundations, never Year 7.
- **Contexts** should be calm and age-respectful: money, distances, populations, sports results, phone storage, recipes and measures.
- **Vocabulary** (GreenPrint v2.0, v1.4 place-value language standard): *digit, value, place value, column, integer, decimal, decimal point, tenth, hundredth, thousandth, placeholder, partition, round, nearest, estimate*. Say "the digits move" when multiplying by 10, never "add a zero" or "move the decimal point".
- **Pattern, then proof:** where a rule is spotted, such as "×10 moves every digit one column left", add a short "Why must it be?" stretch: each column is worth ten times the next.
- **Calculators** are not needed.
- **Answer checking** (QA gate 6a): accept every equivalent form, including number words ("four thousand and fifty"), commas or spaces in large numbers (4,050 and 4 050), trailing zeros (3.50 and 3.5) and £ or p in money answers.
- **Symbols palette** on in-lesson answer boxes that need <, >, ≤, ≥ or a decimal point.
- **Rubric labels:** Approaching Proficiency · Developing Proficiency · Proficiency · Refining Proficiency.
- **Number lines** follow GreenPrint v2.3 (numbered and labelled).
- **Reuse:** the KS3 Library place-value lesson stays in the library. Its interactives (the Digit Value Magnifier, the drag-and-drop chart, "Find and Fix the Place-Value Mistake" and "Build a Place-Value Museum") are adapted into Lessons 1, 5, 3 and 8 in the current shell, not copied as they are.
- **Site:** a new unit `place-value` under a new Foundations Year 7 heading; Classroom gets a new topic when the first lesson goes live.
