## Unit Driving Question
How can a shape move, turn, flip or grow, and what stays the same?

## The mathematical story
One idea runs through the whole unit: **a transformation is a rule that sends every point of a shape to a new point, and what it keeps tells you what kind it is.**

It begins with the three **rigid** transformations. A **reflection** sends each point to the same distance on the other side of a mirror line, along a perpendicular; it is fully described by the mirror line. A **rotation** turns every point through the same angle about a centre; it is described by the centre, the angle and the direction (anticlockwise positive, clockwise negative). A **translation** slides every point the same distance in the same direction; a column vector records the move. All three keep every length and every angle, so the image is **congruent** to the object: the same shape and size.

Then learners **describe transformations fully**. Given an object and its image, they decide which transformation it is and give every detail: a reflection and its mirror line, a rotation with its centre, angle and direction, a translation with its vector, an enlargement with its centre and scale factor. One missing detail leaves a description incomplete.

**Enlargement** is the bridge to similarity. Every point moves along a ray from the centre, and its distance from the centre is multiplied by the scale factor. Lengths are multiplied by the scale factor, but angles stay the same; a scale factor between 0 and 1 makes the shape smaller. The image is not congruent, but it is **similar**: the same shape, a different size.

**Similar shapes** have corresponding angles equal and corresponding lengths in the same ratio. The scale factor finds missing lengths, and **maps and scale drawings** are similarity at work: every length on the map is the real length multiplied by the same scale. Finally, as a stretch, areas grow by the **square** of the scale factor.

**Arc:** flip, turn, slide (what stays the same?) → describe fully → grow from a centre → same shape, different size → scale in the world.

## Stepping Stones
- Reflect a shape in a mirror line, including *x* = *a*, *y* = *b*, *y* = *x* and *y* = −*x*, and find the mirror line from an object and its image.
- Rotate a shape about a centre through 90°, 180° or 270°, clockwise or anticlockwise, and find the centre of a rotation.
- Translate a shape by a column vector, and write the column vector for a translation.
- Explain why reflections, rotations and translations give **congruent** images, and recognise congruent shapes.
- Give a **complete description** of a single transformation.
- **Enlarge** a shape from a centre by a positive scale factor, including fractions, and find the centre and scale factor of an enlargement.
- Recognise **similar** shapes, and use the scale factor to find missing lengths.
- Use and interpret **maps and scale drawings**.
- (Stretch, Higher tier) Explain why areas of similar shapes are in the ratio of the **square** of the scale factor.

## The Geometry Studio release plan
The Studio's own rule stands: one task registry (`TASKS`), launch links checked on every build by `validate_studio_links()`, and task instructions that are explicit and neurodivergent-friendly (name the exact tool and menu command, one action per step, say what to select first).

- **v0.5 (before Lesson 1):**
  - **Graph → Show coordinate grid:** numbered, labelled axes with italic *x* and *y* and O at the origin (GreenPrint v2.3), with points that snap to grid points.
  - **Transform → Mark mirror:** select a line or segment, or choose *x* = *a*, *y* = *b*, *y* = *x* or *y* = −*x* from a dialog.
  - **Transform → Reflect, Rotate… and Translate…:** each takes the selected objects. Rotate asks for a marked centre and an angle, with anticlockwise positive, clockwise negative, and quick buttons for 90°, 180° and 270°. Translate asks for a column vector.
  - **Images follow their objects:** each image is labelled with primes (*A*′, *B*′, *C*′) and moves when the object is dragged, so learners can test what is kept.
  - **Measuring:** Measure → Length and Angle work on images too.
  - **Tasks for Lessons 1–4:** reflect in a given line; find a mirror line; rotate about a centre; translate by a vector; measure to show congruence.
- **v0.6 (before Lesson 5):**
  - **Transform → Enlarge…:** a marked centre and a scale factor, including fractions such as ½ and ⅓, with optional rays from the centre.
  - **A task checker:** the Studio compares the learner's image with the target and says what matches. For example: “same size and shape, but the mirror line is not *y* = *x*”.
  - **Tasks for Lessons 5–8.**
- **Later (optional):** Graph → Plot function and Define axes with other scales, for Year 10.

Each release is versioned in `05_Lesson_Templates/Dynamic_Geometry_Studio` (the highest version is published to `docs/tools/geometry-studio.html` on every build), with its own QA harness. Earlier construction tasks must keep working, so every release re-runs the existing task checks.

## Notes for building
- **Grids and axes** (GreenPrint v2.3): every coordinate grid has numbered scales, italic *x* and *y*, and O at the origin. Shapes sit on grid points so that every image lands on grid points (exact values on screen).
- **Language:** *object* and *image*; *mirror line*; *centre of rotation*; *angle of rotation*, with direction; *column vector*, with its top and bottom numbers; *scale factor*; *centre of enlargement*; *congruent*; *similar*; *corresponding*. Say “vertical” and “horizontal”, never “up/along”. Read a column vector aloud on first use (“3 to the right, 2 down”). Use precise words for the parts of a fraction scale factor (numerator and denominator).
- **Direction of rotation:** always state it; anticlockwise is positive and clockwise is negative, so −90° means 90° clockwise. Accept “90° clockwise” and “−90°” as the same answer.
- **Complete descriptions** (5.2M) are checked part by part, with feedback naming the missing part. A rotation needs a centre, an angle and a direction; an enlargement needs a centre and a scale factor.
- **Contexts** should be calm and age-respectful: tile and fabric patterns, logos, animation frames, map reading, model making and plans. No sorting of people.
- **Exact values on screen:** lengths and areas are exact; scale factors are written as whole numbers or stacked fractions (GreenPrint v2.6) and accepted in any equivalent form.
- **Proof** (the “pattern, then proof” principle): candidates for “Why must it be?” stretches with labelled diagrams and per-step highlights:
  - why a reflection keeps lengths (perpendicular distances);
  - why rotating by 90° twice is a half turn;
  - why the centre of enlargement lies on every line through a point and its image;
  - why area scales by *k*² (unit squares).
- **Answer checking** (QA gate 6a): coordinates in any spacing, with or without brackets; column vectors typed as “3, −2” or top and bottom boxes; scale factors as fractions, decimals or words; angles with or without the degree sign; directions as words or signs.
- **Symbols palette** (`neo-mathfield`) on answer boxes that need −, °, ′, fractions or the column-vector key (a two-box vector entry, to build in Lesson 3).
- **Rubric labels:** Approaching Proficiency · Developing Proficiency · Proficiency · Refining Proficiency.
- **Field kit:** add the new unit to `FIELDKIT_UNITS` and to `scripts/fieldkits/make_fieldkits.js` when the first lesson is built.
