Area of a Circle

Driving Question: How much space is inside a circle — and how does it depend on the radius?

Lesson type: Relationship investigation — discovering where the area formula of a circle comes from, and how the area depends on the radius.

Stepping Stone: work with circles — find the area of a circle using A = π r², and understand π as the constant of proportionality between area and the square of the radius.

🌿 Connection — Where does the area formula come from?

🔤 New words for this lesson

Area (A) — the amount of flat space inside the circle, measured in square units (cm², m²).
Radius (r) — the distance from the centre to the edge.
Radius squared (r²) — the radius multiplied by itself, r × r.
Sector — a "pizza slice" of a circle, between two radii.
Arc — the curved part of the circle's edge that bounds a sector.

What to do: slide the number of sectors up. Watch the slices rearrange into a shape you already know how to measure — and use it to build the area formula for yourself.

What you are trying to notice: a circle can be rearranged into a parallelogram whose perpendicular height is the radius and whose base is half the circumference (π r). The pieces are sectors bounded by curved arcs, so the shape only becomes a true parallelogram as the number of sectors grows — then the area is π r × r = π r².

🚶 Movement — How does the area depend on the radius?

What to do: change the radius, then plot the point G = (r², A). Plot several and watch where they land. Try to find the gradient of the line and its equation before you reveal them.

What you are trying to notice: plotting area against r² (not r) gives a straight line through the origin — so the area is proportional to the square of the radius, and the gradient is π.

Discover it yourself.

1. The gradient. Divide any area by its r². What number do you get each time?

2. The equation. Write a formula for the area A in terms of the radius r.

Carry this forward: area against r² is a straight line of gradient π — so A = π r². Because the area depends on r², doubling the radius makes the area four times as big, not twice.

🪞 Reflection — True or false, and how do you know?

What to do: decide whether each statement is true or false, then reveal the reasoning. You are being asked to justify, not just answer.

What you are trying to notice: because area depends on r², squaring the radius is the key to every claim about the area of a circle.

⚖️ Circle-Area Reasoner

Carry this forward: the surprise that doubling the radius quadruples the area comes straight from the r² — a small change in radius makes a big change in area.

🎨 Creativity — Can you estimate the space inside real things?

What to do: pick a round object, read its radius, and predict its area before you check with A = π r².

What you are trying to notice: the same rule, A = π r², measures the space inside a coin, a pizza and a pond alike — and a small change in radius makes a surprisingly large change in area.

🍕 Estimate then check

A £1 coin — radius 1.15 cm.

Investigate: the pizza is twice the radius of the tea plate — is its area twice as big, or more? Try both and compare.

🌙 Rest — A pause to let the formula settle

You have not just used a formula today — you have built it, by rearranging a circle into a shape you already understood. That is quiet, powerful work.

Notice one thing: which made A = π r² feel true for you — watching the sectors become a parallelogram, or seeing the points line up on the graph? There is no right answer; just the one that convinced you.

You can jot it on the Scratchpad, say it aloud, or simply hold it in mind. You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — Where does this show up in the real world?

Mode: contextual. This lesson reaches something you can hold — and eat: the pizza. The amount of pizza is its area, A = π r².

Why it matters: a 12-inch pizza is twice the width of a 6-inch one — but it has four times the pizza, because area depends on r². So one large pizza usually gives far more for your money than two small ones. The r² you met today is the reason.

Where else does area decide "how much" — turf for a round lawn, paint for a circular table, dough for a pie?

🛠️ Final Task — Evidence for today

  • Explain, in your own words, how rearranging a circle into a parallelogram shows that A = π r². (What is the base? What is the height?)
