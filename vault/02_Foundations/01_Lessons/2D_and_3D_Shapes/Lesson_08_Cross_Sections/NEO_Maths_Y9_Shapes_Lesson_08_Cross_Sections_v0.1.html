🌿 Connection — Carry a shape along to build a prism

🔤 New words for this lesson

Cross-section — the flat 2D shape you get when you slice straight through a solid.
Prism — a solid with the same cross-section all the way along its length.
Face-on — looking at the cross-section straight on, so you see its true 2D shape.

What to do: choose a 2D cross-section, then drag the Length slider to carry it along and build a prism. Watch every slice along the way stay identical.

What you are trying to notice: a prism is one cross-section carried along a length — every slice across it is the same shape and the same size.

🧱 Carry-it-Along Extruder

Carry this forward: a prism is a constant cross-section carried along a length. Hold onto that — in the Volume lesson it becomes cross-section area × length.

🚶 Movement — Slice a solid and see the cross-section

What to do: pick a solid, then drag Height to move the slicing plane up and down. Drag Tilt to cut on a slant. The 2D cross-section appears face-on on the right.

What you are trying to notice: prisms and cylinders give the same cross-section all the way up; a pyramid and a cone shrink as you climb. Tilt the cut and a circle becomes an ellipse.

🔪 Slicing Plane Studio

The solid, with the slicing plane

Carry this forward: the shape of a slice depends on the solid and the direction you cut. A prism's cross-section stays constant; a cone's runs through circle, ellipse and even a parabola.

🪞 Reflection — Name the slice

What to do: read how the solid is sliced, predict the 2D shape, then check and reveal the reasoning.

What you are trying to notice: the same solid can give different cross-sections depending on the cut — and you can reason out which shape appears.

🧩 What shape is the slice?

Carry this forward: to name a slice, picture the plane cutting through — which edges does it cross, and what shape do those crossing points make?

🎨 Creativity — Design your own prism

What to do: choose any cross-section and a length, and build your own prism. Then predict: what does a slice taken halfway along it look like?

What you are trying to notice: whatever cross-section you choose, a prism carries it unchanged from end to end — so you can design a whole solid just by choosing its cross-section.

🌙 Rest — A pause between dimensions

Moving between flat shapes and solids asks a lot of your spatial imagination — picturing a 2D shape hidden inside a 3D object. Take a breath before moving on.

Notice one thing: which slice surprised you most? Perhaps the ellipse from a tilted cylinder, or the triangle hiding inside a cube. There is no right answer — just what your own attention noticed.

You can jot it on the Scratchpad, say it aloud, or simply hold it in mind. You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — Where does this show up in the real world?

Mode: contextual. Every slice reveals a cross-section — from a loaf of bread to a scan of the human body.

Why it matters: a sliced loaf is a prism — every slice is the same shape. A CT or MRI scanner builds a picture of the inside of the body from hundreds of thin cross-section "slices". Architects and engineers draw "sections" to show what a building or a tunnel looks like cut through. Cut an orange one way and you get a circle; cut it another way and the shape changes. Seeing the 2D shape inside the 3D object is exactly how designers, doctors and makers understand solid things.

Next time you slice something, pause: what cross-section did you just make?

🛠️ Final Task — Evidence for today

Your task: explore cross-sections.

  • Choose a solid and show at least two different cross-sections you can make by slicing it in different directions. Name each 2D shape.
  • Explain, in your own words, what makes a solid a prism — and why every slice across a prism is the same.
  • Find one real object that is a prism (its slices are all the same) and one that is not. How can you tell them apart?

Submission choices: an annotated Scratchpad image, a written explanation, a labelled poster, or a spoken/video walkthrough.
