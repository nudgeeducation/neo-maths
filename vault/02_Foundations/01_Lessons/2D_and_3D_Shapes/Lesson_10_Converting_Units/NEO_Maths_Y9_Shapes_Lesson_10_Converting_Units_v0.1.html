Converting spatial units

Driving Question: Why does 1 cm² become 100 mm² — not 10?

Lesson type: Measurement — converting between units of length, area and volume.

Stepping Stone: convert between spatial units — length scales by 10, area by 100 and volume by 1000.

NEO Cornerstones: Connection · Movement · Reflection · Creativity · Rest · Nutrition

🌱 Open Learner Guide 📘 Open Supporting Adult Guidance
🌿 Connection — Zoom in and reveal the small units

🔤 New words for this lesson

Square unit — a unit of area, like mm² or cm². A cm² is a square 1 cm on each side.
Cubic unit — a unit of volume, like mm³ or cm³. A cm³ is a cube 1 cm on each side.
Conversion factor — the number you multiply by to change units: ×10 for length, ×100 for area, ×1000 for volume.

What to do: choose length, area or volume, then press Zoom in to fill one centimetre unit with millimetre units — and count them.

What you are trying to notice: 1 cm = 10 mm, but 1 cm² = 100 mm² and 1 cm³ = 1000 mm³. Each extra dimension multiplies by another 10 — powers of ten (10, 10², 10³).

🔍 Zoom & Reveal

Carry this forward: length ×10, area ×100, volume ×1000. The exponent matches the number of dimensions: mm→cm is 10¹, 10², 10³.

🚶 Movement — Fill it up and count

What to do: choose area or volume, then drag the Fill slider to pack a 1 cm unit with mm units — a row (or layer) at a time — and watch the count climb.

What you are trying to notice: a cm² fills with 10 rows of 10 = 100 mm²; a cm³ fills with 10 layers of 100 = 1000 mm³. Filling it yourself makes the 100 and the 1000 real.

🧮 Fill It Up

Carry this forward: converting is just counting the small units inside the big one: 10 in a line, 100 in a square, 1000 in a cube.

🪞 Reflection — Spot the slip

What to do: each card shows a unit conversion. Decide whether it is right or has a slip in it, then reveal the reasoning.

What you are trying to notice: conversions go wrong in a few predictable ways — using ×10 for area, forgetting the extra powers for volume, or going the wrong way.

🔍 Right or slip?

Carry this forward: length ×10, area ×100, volume ×1000 — and to go the other way, divide. Match the factor to the number of dimensions.

🎨 Creativity — Convert a real measurement

What to do: pick a real-world measurement, then convert it to the units asked for. Predict, then check.

What you are trying to notice: the same amount can be written in different units — and choosing the right conversion factor (×10, ×100, ×1000) is what makes them match.

🌍 Real measures

Make it your own: on the Scratchpad, measure something at home and write it in two different units (for example, a table's area in cm² and m²).

🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

Powers of ten hiding inside squares and cubes is a subtle, beautiful idea. Take a breath before moving on.

Notice one thing: did it surprise you that a cm² holds 100 mm² — not 10? There is no right answer — just what your own attention noticed.

You can jot it on the Scratchpad, say it aloud, or simply hold it in mind. You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — Where does this show up in the real world?

Mode: contextual. Getting the units right — and the conversion factor with them — matters everywhere measurements are used.

Why it matters: a builder ordering floor tiles works in m² but buys tiles sized in cm²; a nurse converts millilitres and cubic centimetres; a farmer measures land in hectares. Mixing up ×10 and ×100 can mean ordering ten times too little — or too much. Famously, engineers have lost whole spacecraft to a units slip. Knowing that area scales by 100 and volume by 1000 is a small idea that quietly prevents big, expensive mistakes.

Next time you see two units for the same thing — ml and cm³, m² and cm² — picture the little units packed inside the big one.

🛠️ Final Task — Evidence for today

Your task: convert and explain.

  • Convert one length, one area and one volume between units (for example cm→mm, cm²→mm², cm³→mm³). Show the factor you used each time.
  • Explain, in your own words, why area scales by 100 and volume by 1000 when length only scales by 10.
  • Find two labels at home that use different units for the same kind of thing (such as ml and litres, or mm and cm) and convert one into the other.

Submission choices: an annotated Scratchpad image, a written solution, a labelled poster, or a spoken/video walkthrough.
