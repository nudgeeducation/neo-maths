NEO Mathematics: Angles Tell Us Why

Angles on a line and at a point

Driving Question: Why must angles on a straight line make 180°?

Lesson type: Core — a straight line is half a turn and a point is a full turn, so the angles there always add up the same way.

Stepping Stone: use angles on a straight line (180°), angles at a point (360°) and vertically opposite angles, giving the reason for each step.

You will need: half and full turns (Lesson 1), and the optional ✏️ Scratchpad. No calculator needed.

🌿 Connection — The Straight-Line Splitter

🔤 The idea for this lesson

A straight line is half a turn, so the angles that sit together on a straight line always add up to 180°. All the way round a point is a full turn, so the angles at a point add up to 360°. When two straight lines cross, the angles opposite each other are vertically opposite, and they are always equal. In this lesson, every step needs a reason: because …

What to do: choose a scene. Drag a yellow handle to move a ray (or select it with Next ray and use the arrow keys). Watch the angles and their total.

What you are trying to notice: the angles change, but which total never does?

What it feeds: today feeds reasoning. “Because …” turns a guess into an explanation that anyone can check. It is how mathematicians, scientists and good arguments work.

🛠️ Final Task — Because cards

Your task: make three “because cards”, one for each fact from today.

  • For each fact (straight line, point, vertically opposite), draw a diagram with one missing angle.
  • Solve it, writing the answer and the reason: “x = 50°, because angles on a straight line add up to 180°.”
  • Make one puzzle that needs two facts, one after the other.
