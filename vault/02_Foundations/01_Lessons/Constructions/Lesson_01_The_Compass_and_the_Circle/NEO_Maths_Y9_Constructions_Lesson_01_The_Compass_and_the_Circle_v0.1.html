The compass and the circle

Driving Question: How can one tool guarantee "exactly the same distance"?

Lesson type: Core — the opening lesson of the constructions unit, where drawing becomes exact.

Stepping Stone: understand a circle as the set of points a fixed distance from a centre, use compasses to copy a length, and construct an equilateral triangle from a given side.

You will need: the idea of measuring a length, what a triangle is, and the optional ✏️ Scratchpad. No calculator today — the compass does the measuring.

🌿 Connection — Every point the same distance

🔤 The idea for this lesson

Most drawing is a little approximate. Construction is exact — and the tool that makes it exact is a pair of compasses. The secret is what a circle really is. A circle is not just "a round shape": it is every point the same distance from a centre. That fixed distance is the radius. A compass holds that distance while you swing it, so every mark it makes is guaranteed the same distance from where the point sits. Once you see that, every construction in this unit grows from one idea: equal distance.

What to do: the centre O is fixed. Drag the point P around it, trying to keep it the same distance away (the readout shows the distance). Watch what shape the "same-distance" points make.

What you are trying to notice: when you keep the distance fixed, the points you visit trace out a circle. The circle is the set of all points that far from O.

🎯 Keep the same distance

Carry this forward: a circle is every point a fixed distance (the radius) from its centre. The compass is a machine for "the same distance".

🚶 Movement — Build an equilateral triangle

What to do: you have a side AB. Follow the steps to construct an equilateral triangle (all sides equal) with a compass — leaving the arcs showing, exactly as in an exam. Each arc has radius AB.

What you are trying to notice: because both arcs are swung with radius AB, the point where they cross is the same distance from A and from B — and that distance is AB. So all three sides are equal, with no measuring at all.

🧭 The construction

Carry this forward: to construct an equilateral triangle on AB — swing an arc of radius AB from A, another from B, and join their crossing to A and B. Drag A or B: the whole construction stays exact.

🪞 Reflection — Why is it exactly equilateral?

What to do: the triangle looks equilateral — but why is it exact, with no ruler? Click each side to check how long it must be.

What you are trying to notice: the arc from A had radius AB, so AC = AB. The arc from B had radius AB, so BC = AB. And AB is AB. Three sides, all equal to the one radius — that is the proof.

🔎 Check the three sides

Carry this forward: the construction is exact because both arcs share the same radius, AB — so every side equals AB.

🎨 Creativity — Any triangle from three sides stretch

What to do: the same idea builds any triangle from three given side lengths (this is called SSS). Choose a set of three side lengths, then press Construct: a base, an arc of one length from each end, and the crossing is the third corner.

What you are trying to notice: the two arcs still do the work — each keeps a corner the right distance from an end of the base. Watch the warning: if two sides together are shorter than the third, the arcs never meet and no triangle exists (the triangle inequality).

🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

You have made something exactly right with two humble tools and one idea. That shift — from "near enough" to "provably exact" — is worth a quiet moment. Take a breath before moving on.

Notice one thing: did it feel different to construct a triangle rather than measure and draw one? What changed? There is no right answer — just what your own attention noticed.

You can jot it on the Scratchpad, say it aloud, or simply hold it in mind. You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: intellectual. A construction is a mathematical idea, not the nourishment itself — so we name honestly what the learning feeds.

What it feeds: today feeds a taste for certainty from constraint — the deep satisfaction that two simple tools, used honestly, can produce a result you can prove, not merely eyeball. And it feeds the elegance of one idea doing a lot of work: "equal distance" gives you the circle, the equilateral triangle, and (soon) every other construction in this unit. Learning that a whole subject can grow from a single clear idea is one of the most nourishing experiences mathematics offers.

Carry one idea forward: when something must be exact, look for the constraint that forces it — here, the shared radius.
