NEO Mathematics: Formulae and Rearranging

Substituting positive, negative and fractional values

Driving Question: What happens when the numbers get awkward?

🌱 Open Learner Guide 📘 Open Supporting Adult Guidance
🌿 Connection — Bracket or no bracket?

🔤 The idea for this lesson

To substitute is to replace a letter with its value. With positive whole numbers that is easy. With negative numbers and fractions, two habits keep you safe: write the value in brackets, and then follow the order of operations. A power belongs only to what it is attached to — and a bracket decides what that is.

What to do: each card shows a calculation. First predict its value, then see how it works out.

What you are trying to notice: in (−3)², the square belongs to the whole of −3. In −3², the square belongs only to the 3, and the minus sign is applied afterwards.

🔍 Predict, then reveal

Carry this forward: when you substitute a negative number, put it in brackets. Then x² with x = −3 is (−3)² = 9, and nothing gets lost.

🚶 Movement — The substitution machine

What to do: choose a formula and turn the dial to choose a value of x — try negatives and fractions. Type what you predict will come out, then run the machine and watch each stage.

What you are trying to notice: the value always drops in with its brackets. Then powers come first, then × and ÷, then + and −. Subtracting a negative number is the same as adding.

⚙️ Predict, then run

Carry this forward: substitute in brackets, then follow the order of operations, one step at a time. Writing every stage is not slow — it is how the awkward numbers stay under control.

🪞 Reflection — Find the slip

What to do: each piece of working has at most one mistake. Find the first line that goes wrong — or decide that the working is correct.

What you are trying to notice: most slips with awkward numbers happen in the same few places: a lost bracket, a square of a negative number, or subtracting a negative.

🔎 Check the working

Carry this forward: when an answer looks odd, check the brackets and the signs first. They are where awkward numbers bite.

🎨 Creativity — Real formulae, real values

What to do: each card is a formula people really use. Substitute the values, then decide what your answer means.

What you are trying to notice: negative values and fractions are not just puzzles — a temperature can be below zero, and a car can be slowing down. The answer only matters once you read it back into the situation.

🌡️ From formula to meaning

✨ Make your own stretch

Invent a formula for a situation where a negative value makes sense — a bank balance, a temperature, a change in height. Choose a value that makes the answer negative, and one that makes it positive, and explain what each answer means.

🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

Awkward numbers ask for care, not speed. Take a slow breath.

Notice one thing: picture a pair of brackets as a pair of hands, holding a negative number safely while it is carried into a formula. Nothing falls out on the way.

You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: intellectual. The mathematics is the nourishment, so we name honestly what the learning feeds.

What it feeds: today feeds care with detail. Spreadsheets, medicine doses and engineering calculations all depend on substituting values correctly — and the mistakes people make there are the same ones as in this lesson: a lost bracket or a sign.

Carry one idea forward: slowing down at the awkward step is a strength, not a weakness.

🛠️ Final Task — Awkward values, careful working

Your task: show that you can substitute any kind of value, and explain the habits that keep it accurate.

  • Work out 5x − 3, x² + 2x and 12 − x² for x = 4, x = −2 and x = ½. Show the value in brackets on your first line each time.
  • Use F = 1.8C + 32 to convert −15 °C to °F, and say whether it is above or below freezing (32 °F).
  • Explain the difference between −4² and (−4)², with the value of each.
  • Find and correct the mistake: "when a = −3, 7 − 2a = 7 − 6 = 1".
  • stretch Find a value of x for which x² + 2x is negative, and explain why.

Submission choices: an annotated Scratchpad image, a photographed page, a short slideshow, or a spoken/video walkthrough.
