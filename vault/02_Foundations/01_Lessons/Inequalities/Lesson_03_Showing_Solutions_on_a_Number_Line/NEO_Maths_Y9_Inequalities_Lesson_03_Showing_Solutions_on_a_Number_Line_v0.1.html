NEO Mathematics: Inequalities

Showing solutions on a number line

Driving Question: How can one picture show every answer at once?

Lesson type: Core — you draw and read the picture of a solution set yourself, and move between four ways of saying the same thing.

Stepping Stone: represent a solution set on a number line with open and filled circles; read an inequality from a number line; move between words, symbols, the picture and the whole numbers; and solve a double-ended inequality by doing the same to all three parts.

You will need: placing whole numbers, including negatives, on a number line, and solving inequalities from Lesson 2 — and the optional ✏️ Scratchpad.

🌿 Connection — Four ways to say it

🔤 The idea for this lesson

An inequality such as x ≥ 2 has far too many answers to list. A number line can show every one of them at once: a circle marks the boundary — filled when the boundary is included, open when it is not — and an arrow or a segment shows all the rest. The same set can also be said in words, in symbols, or (for whole numbers) as a list.

What to do: three of these four cards say exactly the same thing. Find the one that does not belong and tap it.

What you are trying to notice: the cards usually differ only at the boundary — an open circle where it should be filled, a list that starts one number too late. The boundary is where every decision is made.

🃏 Odd one out

Carry this forward: words, symbols, a number line and a list of whole numbers can all describe one set. Check the boundary first.

🚶 Movement — The three-part balance

What to do: a double-ended inequality has three parts. Choose an operation and a number, then press Do it to all three parts. Keep going until x stands alone in the middle. Then look at the picture.

What you are trying to notice: it is the same balance as Lesson 2 — whatever you do to the middle, you do to both ends. The answer traps x between two boundaries, so its picture is a segment with a circle at each end.

⚖️ Solve all three parts

Carry this forward: a double-ended inequality is solved like any other — do the same to every part. Its picture is a segment between two circles.

🪞 Reflection — Where two rules overlap

What to do: two rules are drawn, one above the other. On the bottom line, tap every whole number that obeys both rules, then press Check. Then choose the single inequality that says the same thing.

What you are trying to notice: "both rules" means the part where the two pictures overlap. Sometimes the overlap is a segment, sometimes one rule swallows the other — and sometimes there is no overlap at all.

🔗 Both rules at once

Carry this forward: two inequalities together allow only the numbers in the overlap of their pictures. That idea returns next lesson, as overlapping regions on a graph.

🎨 Creativity — The picture maker

What to do: make the picture yourself. Tap a whole number on the line to place a boundary circle. Tap the circle again to fill it in; a third tap removes it. With one circle, choose which way to shade. With two circles, the part between them is shaded. Then press Check my picture.

What you are trying to notice: the lesson reads your picture back to you — in symbols, in words and as whole numbers — so you can see exactly what your picture says. If that is not what you meant, the read-back shows you where.

🖌️ Draw the solution set

✨ Make your own stretch

Choose a real limit — opening hours, a safe temperature, an age band, a luggage weight. Draw its picture with the picture maker, then write it four ways on the Scratchpad: in words, in symbols, as a number line, and as a list of whole numbers (if that makes sense for your situation). Which way would you put on a sign, and why?

🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

One picture today held infinitely many answers. That is a lot for one small drawing to carry. Take a slow breath.

Notice one thing: picture an arrow running along a number line, on and on without stopping. Every number it passes is an answer. Let your eyes rest on the circle where it begins — the one place where a decision was made.

You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: intellectual. The mathematics is the nourishment, so we name honestly what the learning feeds.

What it feeds: today feeds representation — the ability to say one idea in several ways and choose the clearest for the moment. A sign at a theme park, a weather warning and a dosage label each pick the representation their reader needs. Moving between words, symbols and pictures is how mathematicians, and good communicators, make ideas easy to share.

Carry one idea forward: when something is hard to say in words, try drawing it.

🛠️ Final Task — Four ways, one set

Your task: show that you can move between the four ways of describing a solution set.

  • For each of x > −3, x ≤ 4 and −2 ≤ x < 3: write it in words, draw it on a number line, and list the whole numbers it allows (use "…" where the list goes on for ever).
  • Solve −1 < 2x + 3 ≤ 11, showing each step done to all three parts, and draw the answer.
  • Draw your own number line with two circles. Swap with someone (or the adult supporting you), and each read the other's picture back as an inequality.
  • stretch Design a sign for a real situation with a limit. Choose which representation goes on the sign and explain why it is the clearest for that reader.

Submission choices: an annotated Scratchpad image, a photographed page, a short slideshow, or a spoken/video walkthrough.

Showing solutions on a number line — Practice Companion

Use support in this order: 🤔 Socratic prompt → 💡 hint → ✅ check → 👀 worked solution. The worked solution unlocks separately for each question, once you have used the prompt, used the hint and checked two answers.

Type inequalities like x > 5 or −2 < x ≤ 3. You can use the palette, or write <= for ≤ and >= for ≥. Separate lists of numbers with commas.
