🔤 The idea for this lesson

A parallelogram has two pairs of parallel sides. Slide the triangle from one end to the other and it becomes a rectangle with the same base and the same perpendicular height, so its area = base × height. A trapezium has one pair of parallel sides, a and b. A copy turned half a turn fits alongside it to make a parallelogram with base a + b, so a trapezium's area = ½ × (a + b) × height.

What to do: each grid square is 1 m by 1 m. Drag the yellow corners to change the shape (or choose a corner with the buttons and use the arrow keys). Then press the button to rearrange it.

What you are trying to notice: when you change the slant, what stays the same? What shape does the rearranging make?

🛠️ Final Task — Rearrange it

Your task: on squared paper or the Scratchpad, show how each shape rearranges into one you know.

  • Draw a parallelogram. Show the triangle that slides across to make a rectangle, and find the area.
  • Draw a trapezium and a turned copy beside it. Show the parallelogram they make, and find the trapezium's area.
  • Find the area of one parallelogram or trapezium from real life (a ramp side, a field, a bag), with units.
  • stretch Explain why a trapezium's area is ½ × (a + b) × height, in your own words.
