NEO Mathematics: Place Value

Partitioning in more than one way

Driving Question: How many ways can I split 3.47?

Lesson type: Core — exchanging one counter for ten in the next column changes how a number is split, never the number.

Stepping Stone: partition and recombine whole numbers and decimals in more than one way, and explain why every split is the same number.

You will need: tenths and hundredths (Lesson 4), and the optional ✏️ Scratchpad. No calculator needed.

🌿 Connection — The Exchange Counter Chart

🔤 The idea for this lesson

To partition a number is to split it into parts that add back to it. 3.47 is 3 + 0.4 + 0.07, but that is not the only way. One one is worth ten tenths, so you can swap 1 one for 10 tenths: 3.47 = 2 + 1.4 + 0.07. The split changes; the number does not.

What to do: choose a number. Press 1 → 10 under a column to break one counter into ten in the next column. Press 10 → 1 to group ten counters back into one. Or choose a split to make.

What you are trying to notice: the split under the chart changes with every exchange. Does the total ever change?

What to do: type a split that fits each description, with the parts joined by +, such as 2 + 1.4 + 0.07. You can also write parts such as 14 tenths.

What you are trying to notice: each number can be split in many ways. Try to find one nobody else would think of.

🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

Many shapes, one amount. Take a slow breath.

Notice one thing: a £10 note, two £5 notes and ten £1 coins look different, but they are worth the same. Exchanging counters works the same way.

You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: intellectual. The mathematics is the nourishment, so we name honestly what the learning feeds.

What it feeds: today feeds flexibility. Seeing 3.47 as 2 + 1.4 + 0.07 is the move behind subtracting with exchanges, and behind working things out in your head. There is usually more than one good way.

Carry one idea forward: ten of one column make one of the next.

🛠️ Final Task — One number, many splits

Your task: choose a number with two digits after the point, and show how many ways you can split it.

  • Show the standard split (such as 3 + 0.4 + 0.07).
  • Show at least three other splits, each made by breaking or grouping, with a counter chart for one of them.
  • For one split, explain why it is still the same number.
  • stretch Show the number as hundredths only, and explain how many exchanges that needed.

Submission choices: an annotated Scratchpad image, a photographed page, a short slideshow, or a spoken or video walkthrough.
