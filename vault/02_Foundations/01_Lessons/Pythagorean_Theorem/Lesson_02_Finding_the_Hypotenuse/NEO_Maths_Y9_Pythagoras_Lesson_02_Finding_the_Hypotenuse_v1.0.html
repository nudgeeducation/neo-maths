NEO Mathematics: Pythagoras' Theorem

Finding the Hypotenuse

Driving Question: How can I recognise the hypotenuse and build a correct step-by-step solution for its length?

Lesson type: Worked-solution builder for finding the hypotenuse

Stepping Stones: identify the right angle; identify the side opposite it as the hypotenuse; use the correct opposite-side letter; square the two shorter side lengths; add; square-root to find the hypotenuse length.

🌱 Open Learner Guide 📘 Open Supporting Adult Guidance
🌿 Connection — Which side is the hypotenuse?

What to do: look for the small right-angle marker first. The hypotenuse is always the side opposite the right angle, and it is always the longest side.

What you are trying to notice: the hypotenuse may appear on the right, on the left, as the base, or in a tilted triangle. Its position on the page does not matter.

Orientation warm-up

Which side is the hypotenuse?

Carry this idea forward: every worked solution begins by finding the right angle and tracing to the side opposite it.
🚶 Movement — How do I build the solution step by step?

What to do: choose one example, then complete the six steps in order. Use the Socratic prompt or Hint whenever the next move is unclear.

What you are trying to notice: the letter on the left of the equation changes when the hypotenuse changes. The structure of the method stays the same.

🪞 Reflection — What usually goes wrong?

What to do: choose one mistake below and explain how you would prevent it.

  • Choosing a side next to the right angle instead of the side opposite it.
  • Using c automatically even when the right angle is not at C.
  • Adding the two side lengths before squaring them.
  • Forgetting the square root at the end.
🎨 Creativity — Can I teach someone else the method?

What to do: create a mini teaching card showing how to find the hypotenuse in one of the four orientations.

  1. Draw or copy the triangle.
  2. Mark the right angle.
  3. Trace to the opposite side and name the hypotenuse.
  4. Write the full worked solution.
🌙 Rest — How can I slow the method down?

You can reduce the load by using one decision at a time:

right angle → opposite side → hypotenuse → square → add → root

🛠 Final Task — My hypotenuse worked solution

Build and explain one complete hypotenuse solution

What to do: choose one of the four examples, or design your own. Create a clear worked solution that another learner could follow.

  • identify the right angle;
  • identify the hypotenuse and its correct letter;
  • write the theorem with the hypotenuse square on the left;
  • show the squared values;
  • show the addition step;
  • square-root to find the side length;
  • include one sentence explaining why the answer makes sense.
