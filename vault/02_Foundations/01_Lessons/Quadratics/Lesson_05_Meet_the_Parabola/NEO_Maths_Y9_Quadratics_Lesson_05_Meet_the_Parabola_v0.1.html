🌿 Connection — Build the curve from a table

🔤 The idea for this lesson

You have met a quadratic as a growing sequence and as an expression. Today you meet its third face: a shape. The rule is the simplest quadratic of all, y = x² — "the height is the input, squared". To draw it, you do exactly what you already know: substitute an x, work out y, and plot the point (x, y).

Here is the surprise that makes the shape. Squaring a negative number gives a positive answer: (−3)² = 9, the same as 3² = 9. So the negative side of the table climbs back up to match the positive side — and the points fall into a symmetric U. The line down the middle, where the curve turns, is its line of symmetry. That U-shaped curve has a name: a parabola.

What to do: fill in y = x² for each x in the table, then press Check & plot to drop the points onto the grid. When the table is complete, press Join into a curve.

What you are trying to notice: the two ends of the table are mirror images — x = −3 and x = 3 both give y = 9 — so the curve is symmetric, and it turns at its lowest point, (0, 0).

🚶 Movement — Predict the height

What to do: choose an x-value. Work out y = x² in your head, type it, and check — then watch the point land exactly on the curve.

What you are trying to notice: every point you predict sits on the same curve, and the negative inputs land high because squaring removes the minus sign. That is why the left arm rises just like the right.

🪞 Reflection — The mirror

What to do: a point is marked on one arm of the parabola. Find its mirror twin on the other arm — the point at the same height — by giving its x-value.

What you are trying to notice: the mirror line is x = 0. A point at x = 3 has a twin at x = −3, because 3² and (−3)² are both 9. Knowing one arm tells you the whole curve.

🎨 Creativity — Between the whole numbers stretch

What to do: the sequence lived only at whole-number positions. But the parabola exists everywhere — even between the dots. Choose a half-way x such as 1.5 or 2.5, predict y = x², and see where it lands.

What you are trying to notice: the point drops neatly onto the smooth curve, between the whole-number points. The curve is continuous — it has a height for every x, not just the whole ones.

🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

You have watched a rule become a shape — a row of numbers fold into a symmetric curve. That is worth a quiet moment. Take a breath before moving on.

Notice one thing: when did the U "appear" for you — while filling the table, or when the points were joined? There is no right answer — just what your own attention noticed.

You can jot it on the Scratchpad, say it aloud, or simply hold it in mind. You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: intellectual. The parabola is a mathematical shape, not the nourishment itself — so we name honestly what the learning feeds.

What it feeds: today feeds a taste for symmetry — one of the deepest sources of certainty and economy in mathematics. Once you know a parabola is symmetric, one half tells you the other: you can work out half the points and reflect the rest for free. Seeing that a plain rule like y = x² carries a hidden balance — and that the same quadratic you met as numbers and as symbols is now a single graceful curve — builds the confidence that mathematics is connected and beautiful, not a pile of separate tricks.

Carry one idea forward: when something is symmetric, you never have to do all the work — find one side and mirror it.
