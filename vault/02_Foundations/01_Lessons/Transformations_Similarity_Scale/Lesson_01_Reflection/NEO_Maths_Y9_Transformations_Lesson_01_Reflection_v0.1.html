🔤 The idea for this lesson

A reflection flips a shape over a mirror line. The shape you start with is the object; the shape you get is the image. If the object's corner is A, the image's matching corner is A′, read “A dash” or “A prime”.

Every image point is the same distance from the mirror as its object point, on the other side, along a line at right angles to the mirror. A reflection is fully described by its mirror line, such as x = 1, y = −2 or y = x.

Today's main activity, the Mirror Line Lab, is in Movement.

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: noticing. Today the mathematics feeds how you read the world around you.

What it feeds: reflections are everywhere once you look. The word on the front of many ambulances is written as its own reflection, so that a driver ahead reads it the right way round in a rear-view mirror. Many logos and buildings are designed with a mirror line so they look balanced.

Carry one idea forward: same distance, other side.

🛠️ Final Task — Two reflections

Your task: reflect a triangle in two different mirror lines, and describe each reflection fully.

  • Reflect the triangle in y = x, and compare the coordinates of each corner with its image.
  • Reflect the same triangle in a vertical or horizontal line of your choice, such as x = 2.
  • For each, write the equation of the mirror line and one sentence about what happened to the coordinates.
  • stretch Reflect the first image in a second mirror line. Describe where the triangle ends up.
