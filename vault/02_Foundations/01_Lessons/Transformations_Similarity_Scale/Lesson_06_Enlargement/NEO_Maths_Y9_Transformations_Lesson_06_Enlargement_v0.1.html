Enlargement

Driving Question: How does a shape grow from a centre?

Lesson type: Core — enlarging from a centre, with whole-number and fractional scale factors.

🔤 The idea for this lesson

An enlargement is described by its centre of enlargement and its scale factor. Each point moves along the line from the centre, until its distance from the centre is multiplied by the scale factor.

Every length is multiplied by the scale factor; every angle stays the same. A scale factor of 12 halves every length: the “enlargement” is smaller.

🚶 Movement — Grow or shrink?

What to do: look at the centre and the scale factor. Predict: will the image be bigger, smaller or the same size? Will each corner move further from the centre, closer to it, or stay in the same place? Then reveal the image.

What you are trying to notice: the scale factor alone decides the size; the centre decides where the image goes.

🪞 Reflection — The ray finder

What to do: a triangle and its enlargement are drawn. Draw rays through matching corners to find the centre. Then find the scale factor: how many times as long is each side of the image?

What you are trying to notice: every ray through a corner and its image passes through the centre; the scale factor is an image length divided by the matching object length.

🎨 Creativity — The logo resizer

What to do: a designer needs copies of a logo to fit a frame. Choose a brief, set the scale factor, and click the grid to place the centre until the copy meets the brief.

What you are trying to notice: the scale factor sets the size, and moving the centre slides the copy along the rays. Often more than one centre works.

🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

A shape can grow or shrink and still keep its shape exactly. Take a slow breath.

Notice one thing: hold your hand between a lamp and a wall. As you move your hand, its shadow grows and shrinks from the lamp, like an enlargement from a centre.

You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: practical. Today the mathematics feeds how images are resized.

What it feeds: a projector enlarges a picture from its lens; a photocopier set to 50% uses scale factor 12; design software resizes a logo from a chosen anchor point, which is the centre of enlargement. Keeping the same scale factor across and up is what stops a picture from looking stretched.

Carry one idea forward: every length times the scale factor, every angle the same.

🛠️ Final Task — Grow and shrink

Your task: make two enlargements of one shape, one with a whole-number scale factor and one with a fractional scale factor, and show the evidence.

  • Mark a centre of enlargement, and draw rays through each corner.
  • Enlarge by a whole-number scale factor and by a fractional scale factor, such as 12 or 13.
  • Measure one side and one angle of the shape and of each image, and show how they compare.
  • Describe each enlargement fully: the scale factor and the centre.
