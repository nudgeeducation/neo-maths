NEO Mathematics: Transformations, Similarity and Scale

Maps and scale drawings

Driving Question: How do maps shrink the world exactly?

Lesson type: Core — map scales as ratios, and scale drawings.

Stepping Stone: use a map scale written as a ratio 1 : n, changing between cm, m and km; change between “1 : n” and “1 cm represents …”; find real distances from a map and map lengths from real distances; choose a scale for a drawing.

You will need: Lessons 6 to 8, units of length, and Constructions Lesson 7 (scale plans) — and the optional ✏️ Scratchpad.

🌿 Connection — The same walk, three maps

Start here: the same 2 km walk is drawn on three maps with different scales. Which scale gives the longest line? Predict, then reveal.

What you are trying to notice: in 1 : n, every real length is n times the map length. A larger n makes a smaller map.

🔤 The idea for this lesson

A map scale 1 : 50 000 means every length on the ground is 50 000 times the length on the map, in the same units: 1 cm on the map is 50 000 cm on the ground, which is 500 m.

Map to ground: multiply by 50 000, then change the units. Ground to map: change into cm, then divide by 50 000. The map is similar to the ground, with scale factor 150 000.

🚶 Movement — The same scale, written two ways

What to do: for each scale, choose the one that means the same thing, then read the conversion.

What you are trying to notice: “1 cm represents …” is the ratio with the second number changed into m or km.

🎨 Creativity — Plan a room

What to do: a room is 6 m by 4.5 m. Choose a scale so the plan fits a page 10 cm by 8 cm, then work out the plan size of the bed, the desk and the wardrobe.

What you are trying to notice: a plan uses the same ratio idea as a map, with smaller numbers; the best scale is the one that fits and is as large as possible.

🛠️ Final Task — Make a scale drawing

Your task: make a scale drawing of a real space you know, and use it to answer a question.

  • Choose a space: a room, a garden, a sports pitch or a route. Measure or estimate its real lengths.
  • Choose a scale so the drawing fits your page, and write it as 1 : n and as “1 cm represents …”.
  • Draw it, showing each conversion from real length to drawing length.
  • Measure one length on your drawing that you did not measure in real life, and work out its real length.
  • stretch Explain why angles on your drawing are the same as in real life.
