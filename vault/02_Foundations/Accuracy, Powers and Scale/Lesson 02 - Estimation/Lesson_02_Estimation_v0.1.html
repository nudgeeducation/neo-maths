NEO Mathematics: Accuracy, Powers and Scale

Estimation

Driving Question: How can an estimate help us decide whether an answer makes sense?

Lesson type: Estimation Laboratory, reasonableness investigation and supported practice.

Stepping Stone: estimate calculations by rounding values appropriately and use the estimate to judge reasonableness.

NEO Cornerstones: Connection · Movement · Reflection · Creativity · Rest · Nutrition

45-minute shape: Connect 8 · Move 18 · Practise 10 · Reflect/Create 5 · Rest/Nourish 2 · Evidence 2

🌱 Open Learner Guide 📘 Open Supporting Adult Guidance
🌿 Connection — Which answer could make sense?

What to do: look at the calculation and choose the answer that has a sensible size. Do not calculate it exactly yet.

What you are trying to notice: an estimate can reject an impossible answer before an exact calculation is completed.

Calculation

48.7 × 19.6

Carry this idea forward: estimate the size first; exact calculation can come second.
🚶 Movement — How does an estimate change when the numbers move?

What to do: choose or enter a calculation. Round each value to 1 significant figure, predict the estimate, then compare it with the exact result.

What you are trying to notice: friendly rounded values make mental calculation possible while preserving the overall magnitude.

🧪 Estimation Laboratory

Carry this idea forward: round → calculate mentally → compare → judge.
🪞 Reflection — Is the estimate close enough for its purpose?

What to do: compare the current estimate with the exact result, then decide whether it is useful.

This estimate is useful because...

What you are trying to notice: an estimate is not an exact answer. Its job is to give a sensible scale and support a decision.

🎨 Creativity — Can I design a convincing suspicious answer?

What to do: create one calculation, one plausible answer and one suspicious answer.

  1. Choose two values and an operation.
  2. Round to friendly values and estimate.
  3. Create one answer near the estimate.
  4. Create one answer with the decimal point or place value deliberately wrong.
  5. Explain how estimation exposes the suspicious answer.
🌙 Rest — What is the calm three-step route?

Look away from the screen, then retrieve this route in your own words:

round → calculate → check

🥣 Nutrition — What is this lesson nourishing?

This lesson uses intellectual nourishment. It nourishes judgement: noticing scale, resisting an implausible calculator display and deciding when an approximation is useful.

🌱 Magnitude
I notice the likely size first.
🌱 Judgement
I ask whether an answer is reasonable.
🌱 Efficiency
I use friendly numbers when exactness is unnecessary.
🌱 Confidence
I can challenge an answer rather than accepting it automatically.
🛠 Final Task — Become an Estimation Investigator

Investigate two calculations

What to do: choose two calculations, including at least one decimal calculation. For each one:

  • record the original values;
  • round to friendly values;
  • calculate an estimate;
  • find or check the exact result;
  • judge whether the exact answer is reasonable; and
  • explain whether the estimate was useful.
