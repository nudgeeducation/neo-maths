NEO Mathematics: Accuracy, Powers and Scale

Calculating with Standard Form

Driving Question: Can I calculate with giant and tiny numbers without ever writing out their zeros?

Lesson type: chain-of-equalities working and supported practice.

Stepping Stones: multiply and divide numbers in standard form by regrouping the factors and applying the index laws; rewrite an answer whose coefficient breaks the definition of standard form; add and subtract by matching powers first; interpret an answer in context.

NEO Cornerstones: Connection · Movement · Reflection · Creativity · Rest · Nutrition

45-minute shape: Connect 5 · Explore 20 · Create or Apply 12 · Reflect 8

🌱 Open Learner Guide 📘 Open Supporting Adult Guidance
🌿 Connection — Why may the factors change places?

What to do: here is a multiplication of two standard-form numbers. Tap the line of working that may come next — and notice why it is allowed.

What you are trying to notice: multiplication can be done in any order, so the factors are free to regroup — ordinary numbers together, powers of ten together.

(6 × 10⁵) × (7 × 10³)

The definition, precisely: a number in standard form is written a × 10ⁿ, where 1 ≤ a < 10 and n is an integer. Every chain of working in this lesson finishes by checking its last line against this definition.
Carry this idea forward: regroup → simplify each part → check against the definition. The next panel lets you grow a full chain yourself.
🚶 Movement — Growing the working

What to do: choose a story, then grow the written working one line at a time. Every new line must be equal to the line above it, and every line has a reason in the margin.

What you are trying to notice: each line follows from a named mathematical fact — regrouping, the index law, or the definition of standard form. Nothing else is needed, however large the numbers.

Carry this idea forward: a finished chain is a complete mathematical argument. When you meet an unfamiliar calculation, you are not looking for a method to remember — you are looking for the next true line.
🪞 Reflection — Check this working

What to do: three pieces of working have been handed in. Read each chain line by line, then judge it: complete and correct, or is there a line that does not follow?

What you are trying to notice: a chain can go wrong in two ways — a line that is not equal to the one above it, or a final line that has not been checked against the definition.

🎨 Creativity — Pose your own, then write the chain

What to do: compose your own calculation from the tiles below, then write its full chain yourself — on the Scratchpad or on paper. When your chain is written, reveal a model chain and compare, line by line.

Your challenge: pose one calculation whose chain needs the extra rewrite line, and one that does not. Can you tell which will be which before you write?

Carry this idea forward: your best chain — especially one with the rewrite line — belongs in the Final Task fact file. A rewrite is evidence of understanding, not a mistake.
🌙 Rest — What is the calm four-step route?

Look away from the screen, then retrieve this route in your own words:

regroup → simplify each part → check against the definition → rewrite if needed

Every chain ends resting in standard form. The rewrite line is part of good working, not a detour.

🥣 Nutrition — What is this lesson nourishing?

This lesson uses intellectual nourishment. It nourishes mathematical self-reliance: the knowledge that a calculation at any scale yields to a short chain of justified lines — and that you can write one.

🌱 Structure
I regroup one large calculation into small familiar pieces.
🌱 Reasoning
I can give the reason for every line I write.
🌱 Care
I check my final line against the definition.
🌱 Self-reliance
When a problem is unfamiliar, I look for the next true line.

Carry one idea forward: which of these four feels stronger than it did at the start of the lesson?

🛠 Final Task — Publish your Cosmic Fact File

Three comparisons the universe is proud of

What to do: create a fact file (Scratchpad export, paper, poster or slide) containing three calculations at astronomical or microscopic scale. It must include:

  • three calculations in standard form, each set out as a full written chain of equalities with a reason for every line (a distance, a comparison, and one of your own choosing);
  • at least one chain that needs the rewrite line — shown proudly, with the factorisation visible (for example 15 = 1.5 × 10);
  • each answer translated into an ordinary number and one sentence of meaning; and
  • one sentence explaining why a chain of justified lines can handle any calculation, however unfamiliar.
