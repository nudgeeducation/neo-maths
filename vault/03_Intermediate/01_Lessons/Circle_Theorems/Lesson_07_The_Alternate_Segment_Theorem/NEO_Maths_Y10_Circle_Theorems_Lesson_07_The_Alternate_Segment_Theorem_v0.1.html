NEO Mathematics: Circle Theorems

The alternate segment theorem

Driving Question: Why does the tangent angle match the angle in the far segment?

Lesson type: Core — the subtlest theorem of the unit, linking a tangent to an inscribed angle across a chord. Take it slowly.

Stepping Stone: discover, and then justify, that the angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle in the alternate segment — and use it (with the reason) to find missing angles.

You will need: that a tangent is perpendicular to the radius (Lesson 6), the angle in a semicircle (Lesson 3), angles in the same segment (Lesson 4), and that angles in a triangle sum to 180°.

🌿 Connection — The tangent's hidden twin angle

🔤 The idea for this lesson

Here is the subtlest fact in the whole unit. A tangent touches the circle at T, and a chord TB is drawn from that point. The angle between the tangent and the chord has a surprising twin: it is exactly equal to the angle in the alternate segment — the inscribed angle ∠TPB you get by looking at the same chord TB from a point P on the arc on the other side of the chord. "Alternate" just means the segment on the far side. Two angles in quite different places, always equal. This one does not fall straight out of the keystone — it takes a short proof — so go slowly and watch which angle matches which.

What to do: the tangent touches at T (the horizontal line), with chord TB. Point P sits in the alternate segment. Both matching angles are drawn in red. Drag B (to change the chord) or P (around its arc) and watch the two red angles.

What you are trying to notice: the tangent–chord angle at T and the angle ∠TPB at P are always the same size — and ∠TPB does not change as P slides along its arc (that is the same-segment fact from Lesson 4).

🪞 Reflection — Two angles that refuse to differ the hard one

What to do: drag P all the way around its arc, and drag B to reshape the chord. Keep both red angles in view. Then press Why equal?.

What you are trying to notice: however you move things, the tangent–chord angle and the alternate-segment angle stay equal. Sliding P shows the same-segment fact (its angle is constant); changing the chord changes both together. Two independent freedoms, one stubborn equality.

🎨 Creativity — Why are they equal? stretch

What to do: press the steps. The proof strings together three facts you already have — tangent ⟂ radius, the angle in a semicircle, and angles in the same segment. Call the tangent–chord angle x.

What you are trying to notice: the trick is a single extra line — the diameter from T. It turns the tangent–chord angle into an angle inside a right-angled triangle, and the semicircle right angle finishes the job.

🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

That was the hard one. Notice it did not arrive as a fifth separate rule to memorise — it was built from three facts you already own. If it still feels slippery, that is completely normal; the alternate segment theorem takes most people a few encounters. Be kind to yourself and take a breath.

Notice one thing: which of the three ingredient facts (tangent ⟂ radius, semicircle right angle, same segment) did you most rely on to feel convinced? There is no right answer.

You can jot it on the Scratchpad, say it aloud, or simply hold it in mind. You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🛠️ Final Task — Evidence for today

Your task: show you can state, use and justify the alternate segment theorem.

  • Draw a circle with a tangent touching at T and a chord TB. Mark the tangent–chord angle, and a point P on the alternate arc, and show ∠TPB equals the tangent–chord angle, giving the reason.
