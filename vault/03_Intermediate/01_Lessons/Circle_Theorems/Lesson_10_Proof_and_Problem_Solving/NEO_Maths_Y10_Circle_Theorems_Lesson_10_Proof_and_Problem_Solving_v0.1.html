🌿 Connection — What makes a proof a proof

🔤 The idea for this lesson

An angle chase finds values; a proof goes further — it shows a result is always true, for reasons that leave no room for doubt. A proof is a chain of statements, each one following from the givens or from statements already made, and each carrying a reason. The order matters: you cannot use a fact before you have established it. This final lesson is about building that chain — laying the statements in a valid order, justifying every one — and then presenting a full, clean solution. Here we start by getting the foundations of the great keystone proof (the angle at the centre) into the right order.

What to do: the construction is drawn: P on the circle, the line PO extended to Q, and radii OA, OB. Build the first three steps of the proof — click the statements in a valid order. Each correct card is placed, and its angles appear on the diagram.

What you are trying to notice: the very first statement can only use the givens (here, that all radii are equal). Everything after must build on what is already down.

🚶 Movement — Every statement needs a reason

What to do: each statement below is true — your job is to say why. Choose the reason that justifies it. This is the habit that turns working into proof.

What you are trying to notice: a proof is only as strong as its reasons. Being able to name the exact fact behind each line is the difference between "it looks right" and "it must be so".

🪞 Reflection — Build the keystone proof the whole argument

What to do: assemble the full six-step proof that the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference. The statements are shuffled — click them in a valid logical order. A step that relies on something not yet established is rejected; each correct step lights up on the diagram.

What you are trying to notice: there is a forced order. You must have the equal radii before the base angles, the base angles before the exterior angles, and those before you can add them. Getting the order right is the proof.

🔬 The proof builder

Carry this forward: a proof is a forced chain of justified steps — assemble it in order and it is unarguable.

🎨 Creativity — Prove another theorem yourself stretch

What to do: watch a second proof unfold — that the opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add to 180°. It leans on the very keystone you just proved. Press the steps and read the reason at each.

What you are trying to notice: once one theorem is proved, it becomes a tool for proving the next. Mathematics is built in exactly this way — each secured result carrying the weight of the ones that follow.

🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

You have reached the end of the unit — from naming the parts of a circle to proving its deepest theorems. That is a long journey, and proof is the most demanding kind of mathematical thinking there is. Notice that a proof is not about being clever; it is about being honest and complete — leaving nothing unjustified. Take a breath and feel the shape of what you have built.

Notice one thing: which proof in this unit felt most convincing to you, and why? A proof that convinces you has done its real job — not just to be correct, but to make you see why.

You can jot it on the Scratchpad, say it aloud, or simply hold it in mind. You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: intellectual. We name honestly what the learning feeds.

What it feeds: proof is the beating heart of mathematics, and the skill it builds — constructing an argument no-one can fault — reaches far beyond it. It is the same discipline a lawyer uses to build a case, a scientist to defend a claim, an engineer to certify a bridge: a chain of statements, each justified, leading inescapably to a conclusion. Today feeds intellectual honesty — the refusal to assert what you cannot justify — and the deep satisfaction of an argument that simply closes, with nothing left to doubt.

Carry one idea forward: to prove something is to show it must be true — and to know the difference between that and merely believing it.

🛠️ Final Task — Evidence for today

Your task: present a complete, justified proof.

  • Write out the full proof that the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference — every statement with its reason, in a valid order.
  • Prove one more circle theorem of your choice (the semicircle, the cyclic quadrilateral, or the equal tangents), building on results already established.
  • Solve a ‘show that’ problem: a multi-step question that ends in a statement you must justify, not just a number.
  • stretch Take one of your proofs and write it two ways — a full formal version and a short ‘proof sketch’ that captures the key idea in a sentence or two.

Submission choices: a written proof (Scratchpad or on paper), a construction with reasons in the Geometry Studio, a recorded explanation talking through the logic, or a typed reasons-included solution.
