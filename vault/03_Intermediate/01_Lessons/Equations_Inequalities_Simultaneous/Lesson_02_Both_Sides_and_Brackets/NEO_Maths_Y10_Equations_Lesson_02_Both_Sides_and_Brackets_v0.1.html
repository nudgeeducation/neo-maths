Both sides, and brackets

Driving Question: What if the unknown is on both sides, or hidden inside brackets?

Lesson type: Core — the opening lesson of the algebra unit, where solving becomes keeping the balance.

Stepping Stone: solve linear equations with the unknown on both sides (by collecting like terms) and with brackets (by expanding first), keeping the equation balanced and checking by substitution.

You will need: the four operations and their inverses, working with negative numbers, and the optional ✏️ Scratchpad. No calculator needed today.

🌿 Connection — Tidy first: collect like terms

🔤 The idea for this lesson

So far the unknown x sat on one side only. Now it can appear on both sides, or be locked inside brackets. Before solving, you tidy: collect like terms (add up the x's, add up the plain numbers) and expand any brackets. Then it is an ordinary balance problem again. Like terms are terms of the same kind — all the x's together, all the units together.

What to do: the tiles below are a jumble of x-tiles and unit tiles. Collect them into a tidy expression ax + b.

What you are trying to notice: you can only add like to like — x-tiles join x-tiles, units join units. They never merge into each other.

🧹 Collect the like terms

Carry this forward: tidy an equation before solving — collect like terms and expand brackets first, then keep the balance as usual.

🚶 Movement — The unknown on both sides

What to do: now there are x bags on both pans. The scales still balance because the two sides are equal. Take the same number of bags off both sides first (that gathers the unknown onto one side), then work as before — take blocks off both sides, then split into equal groups.

What you are trying to notice: removing the same number of x bags from both pans keeps the balance and clears the unknown from one side. It is the same rule — the same to both sides — used to collect the unknown, not just numbers. Sometimes a side carries red −1 owed tiles (a subtraction); then you add the same number to both pans to cancel them.

⚖️ Solve with x on both sides

Carry this forward: collect the unknown onto one side by removing the same bags from both, then finish by keeping the balance.

🪞 Reflection — The same moves, written down

What to do: the balancing you just did is the written method. Choose the next move — subtract the smaller number of x's from both sides to collect the unknown, then clear the number, then divide — and watch it rewrite, line by line. Then check by substituting back.

What you are trying to notice: collecting the unknown is just "subtract rx from both sides". Every line stays true because you did the same to both sides — which is why the method is trustworthy. The substitution check is the proof.

✍️ Solve both-sides equations, then check

Carry this forward: collect the unknown onto one side, then it is a two-step equation you already know how to finish — and the check confirms it.

🎨 Creativity — Opening the brackets stretch

What to do: a bracket like a(x + b) is a shorthand for a rectangle a tall and (x + b) wide. Split the rectangle to see the two pieces it hides, then use the expansion to solve.

What you are trying to notice: a(x + b) = ax + ab — the outside multiplies everything inside, not just the first term. Expanding turns a bracket equation into one you can already solve.

🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

Notice how much of this lesson is just tidying — gather the like things, open the brackets — until a tangled equation becomes a familiar one. A lot of mathematics is making something look simpler without changing what it means. Take a breath.

Notice one thing: did collecting the x's from both sides feel like the same "do it to both sides" move, just used on the unknown instead of a number? There is no right answer; just notice the connection.

You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: intellectual. The mathematics is the nourishment here, so we name honestly what the learning feeds.

What it feeds: today feeds a tidy mind — the habit of simplifying before solving, of gathering like with like and opening up what is hidden, so that a problem that looked harder turns out to be one you already know. There is real satisfaction in taming a messy-looking equation with a couple of calm, honest moves.

Carry one idea forward: when something looks complicated, tidy it first — collect, expand — and see what familiar problem is underneath.

🛠️ Final Task — Evidence for today

Your task: solve equations with the unknown on both sides and with brackets, and prove your answers.

  • Solve at least three equations: one with x on both sides (like 5x + 2 = 2x + 11), one with a bracket (like 3(x + 2) = 18), and one that has both.
  • Show the tidying step — collect like terms or expand the bracket — then solve keeping the balance.
  • Check every answer by substituting it back into the original equation.
  • stretch Make your own both-sides equation with a chosen answer, then solve it back to that answer.

Submission choices: an annotated Scratchpad image, a photographed hand-written solution showing each line, a short spoken walkthrough, or a poster of the method.
