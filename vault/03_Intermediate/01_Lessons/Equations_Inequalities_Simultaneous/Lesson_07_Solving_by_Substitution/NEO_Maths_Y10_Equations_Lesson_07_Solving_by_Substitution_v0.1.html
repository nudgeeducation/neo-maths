Solving by substitution

Driving Question: How do I find the meeting point without drawing?

🌿 Connection — If y equals it, swap it in

🔤 The idea for this lesson

Reading a crossing off a graph is quick but only as accurate as your drawing. There is an exact way. If one equation says y = 2x + 1, then y and 2x + 1 are the same number — so anywhere the other equation has a y, you may swap in (2x + 1). That leaves one equation in one unknown, which you already know how to solve. This is substitution.

What to do: one equation gives y in terms of x. Substitute that expression into the other equation and watch the y disappear.

What you are trying to notice: swapping y for what it equals does not change anything — the equation is still true — but now there is only one unknown left.

🔁 Swap y for its expression

Carry this forward: if y equals an expression, you may put that expression wherever y appears — leaving one unknown.

🚶 Movement — The substitution method, step by step

What to do: solve the pair in full. Choose each move in turn — substitute, collect, solve for x, back-substitute for y — then check in both. The wrong first move is offered too; pick the substitution.

What you are trying to notice: substitution turns two equations in two unknowns into one equation in one unknown. Solve that, put the value back to get the other unknown, and both equations come out true.

✍️ Solve by substitution

Carry this forward: substitute → collect → solve for x → back-substitute for y → check both.

🪞 Reflection — The algebra lands on the crossing

What to do: solve the pair by substitution, enter your answer, and see it land exactly where the two lines cross. The algebra and the graph give the same point.

What you are trying to notice: substitution is not a different answer from the graph — it is the exact version of it. The pair (x, y) you compute is precisely the intersection you would have read off.

🎨 Creativity — Rearrange first stretch

What to do: sometimes neither equation is ready as y = … . First rearrange one to make a variable the subject, then substitute. Watch the two steps, then find the solution.

What you are trying to notice: substitution always needs one variable made the subject. If it is not handed to you, a quick rearrangement creates it — then the method runs exactly as before.

🔧 Make the subject, then substitute

🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

Substitution is a small act of trust: because two things are equal, one can stand in for the other with nothing lost. From that single idea, two tangled equations quietly become one you can already solve. Take a breath and notice how little it really takes.

Notice one thing: did "solve two equations at once" stop feeling mysterious once it became "swap y for what it equals, then solve as usual"? Just notice the method settling into something familiar.

You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: intellectual. The mathematics is the nourishment here, so we name honestly what the learning feeds.

What it feeds: today feeds the elegance of an exact method — no drawing, no estimating, just an answer you can trust to the last digit. Substitution also feeds a deeper habit: reducing a hard problem to an easier one you have already solved, by using what you know to be equal. That move — trade the unknown for what it equals — reaches far beyond simultaneous equations.

Carry one idea forward: when two things are equal, either can stand for the other — and that swap can turn a hard problem into a familiar one.
