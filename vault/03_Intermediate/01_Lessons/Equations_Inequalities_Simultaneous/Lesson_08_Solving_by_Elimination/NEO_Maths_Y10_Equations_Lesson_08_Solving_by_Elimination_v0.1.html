NEO Mathematics: Equations, Inequalities and Simultaneous Relationships

Solving by elimination

Driving Question: How do I combine two equations to remove an unknown?

Lesson type: Core — the opening lesson of the algebra unit, where solving becomes keeping the balance.

Stepping Stone: solve a pair of simultaneous linear equations by elimination — add or subtract the equations (scaling a row first when needed) so one variable cancels, then solve, find the second variable and check in both.

You will need: the four operations and their inverses, working with negative numbers, and the optional ✏️ Scratchpad. No calculator needed today.

🌿 Connection — Add them and watch one vanish

🔤 The idea for this lesson

Two true equations can be added (or subtracted) to make another true equation. The clever part: if one variable appears as + y in one equation and − y in the other, then adding makes it cancel — gone. That leaves a single equation in one unknown. This is elimination, and the easiest case to spot is opposite signs, where adding does the work.

What to do: the two equations have y with opposite signs. Add them and watch the y disappear.

What you are trying to notice: + y and − y add to zero. Adding the whole equations keeps them true, but removes y — leaving something you can solve.

➕ Add to eliminate

Carry this forward: opposite signs on a variable mean adding the equations cancels it.

🚶 Movement — The elimination method, step by step

What to do: solve each pair in full. First decide the move that cancels a variable — add when the signs are opposite, subtract when they are the same — and when the coefficients do not match, scale a row first. Then solve, find the other variable, and check both.

What you are trying to notice: the whole method is one idea used carefully — combine the equations so a variable vanishes. Opposite signs → add; same signs → subtract; mismatched sizes → multiply a row until they match.

✍️ Solve by elimination

Carry this forward: decide add / subtract (scale first if needed) → cancel → solve → find the other → check both.

🪞 Reflection — Add or subtract?

What to do: for each pair, decide the single move that makes a variable cancel: add or subtract. The tool shows the chosen variable cancelling — or not.

What you are trying to notice: it is a quick read of the signs. If the matching variable has the same sign in both, subtracting cancels it; if it has opposite signs, adding does. Getting this decision right is the heart of the method.

⚖️ Which move cancels a variable?

Carry this forward: same sign → subtract; opposite sign → add. Read the signs first.

🎨 Creativity — When nothing matches: scale a row stretch

What to do: sometimes neither variable has matching coefficients, so nothing cancels yet. Multiply one equation through until a variable's coefficients match — then add or subtract as usual.

What you are trying to notice: multiplying a whole equation by a number keeps it true but changes the coefficients. Choose the multiplier that makes one variable line up; then the ordinary elimination move works.

🔧 Scale, then eliminate

🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

Elimination has a certain boldness to it: instead of tiptoeing around the unknowns, you combine the two equations head-on and let one simply disappear. Two lines of algebra collapse into one. There is a satisfying economy in that. Take a breath.

Notice one thing: did adding two whole equations together feel strange at first — and then obvious, once a variable vanished? Just notice the moment it clicked.

You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: intellectual. The mathematics is the nourishment here, so we name honestly what the learning feeds.

What it feeds: today feeds a sense of leverage — the realisation that you can act on two equations at once, combining them so a difficulty cancels itself out. Choosing the move that makes a problem simpler, rather than grinding through it, is a habit of elegant thinking that pays off far beyond algebra.

Carry one idea forward: sometimes the cleverest step is to combine two things so that part of the problem cancels.

🛠️ Final Task — Evidence for today

Your task: solve pairs of simultaneous equations by elimination, and check them.

  • Solve at least three pairs: one where you add (opposite signs), one where you subtract (same signs), and one where you must scale a row first.
  • For each, show the elimination, solve for one variable, then find the other.
  • Check each solution in both original equations.
  • stretch Explain how you decide, just from the signs, whether to add or subtract.
