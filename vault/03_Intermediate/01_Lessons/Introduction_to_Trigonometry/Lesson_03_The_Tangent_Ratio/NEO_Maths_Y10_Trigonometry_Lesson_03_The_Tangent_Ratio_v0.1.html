The tangent ratio

Driving Question: How does opposite ÷ adjacent capture an angle?

Lesson type: Defining — meeting the first named trigonometric ratio, the tangent, and seeing it measure an angle.

Stepping Stone: define and use tan θ = opposite ÷ adjacent, and recognise that a bigger tangent means a steeper angle.

🌿 Connection — The ratio with a name

🔤 New words for this lesson

Tangent — for a chosen angle θ in a right-angled triangle, the tangent is the ratio oppositeadjacent. We write it tan θ.
Opposite — the side across from θ (from Lesson 2).
Adjacent — the side next to θ, not the hypotenuse (from Lesson 2). The tangent does not use the hypotenuse at all.

What to do: here are three right-angled triangles that are all the same shape (same angle θ) but different sizes. For each, work out opposite ÷ adjacent. Press Reveal the ratio to check.

What you are trying to notice: just as in Lesson 1, the ratio is the same for every size — because the angle is the same. That one shared number has a name: it is tan θ, the tangent of the angle.

🏷️ The ratio with a name

Carry this forward: tan θ = opposite ÷ adjacent. It is the same for every triangle with that angle — a single number that belongs to the angle.

🚶 Movement — Tangent Explorer

What to do: drag the angle slider to change θ. The adjacent side stays the same length; watch the opposite side and the value of tan θ = opposite ÷ adjacent change with it.

What you are trying to notice: as the angle gets bigger, the triangle gets steeper, the opposite side grows, and tan θ grows too. The tangent is a fingerprint of the angle: each angle has its own value, and a bigger angle means a bigger tangent.

📐 Tangent Explorer

Carry this forward: tan θ increases as θ increases. Knowing the angle fixes the tangent; knowing the tangent fixes the angle.

🪞 Reflection — Is this tan θ?

What to do: each card shows a right-angled triangle with the opposite and adjacent marked, and a claimed value for tan θ. Decide whether the claim is right or a slip, then check.

What you are trying to notice: the two classic slips are using the hypotenuse instead of the adjacent, and turning the ratio upside down (adjacent ÷ opposite). Tan θ is always opposite ÷ adjacent — nothing else.

🔎 Is this tan θ?

Carry this forward: to test any tangent, ask: is it opposite ÷ adjacent? Not the hypotenuse, and not upside down.

🎨 Creativity — Rank by steepness

What to do: here are three ramps (or roof slopes), each given by how far it rises (opposite) over how far it runs (adjacent). Work out tan θ = rise ÷ run for each, then put them in order from gentlest to steepest. Drag or use the buttons to order them, then check.

What you are trying to notice: you do not need to measure the angles to compare them — the tangent alone tells you which is steeper. A bigger tan θ is a steeper slope.

🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

A single number that captures a whole angle — how steep, how tilted, how much rise for each step along — is a quietly powerful idea. Take a breath before moving on.

Notice one thing: when the angle grew and the tangent grew with it, did the connection feel natural, or surprising? There is no right answer — just what your own attention noticed.

You can jot it on the Scratchpad, say it aloud, or simply hold it in mind. You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: intellectual. This mathematics does not live in food or the body, so we name honestly what the learning nourishes.

What it feeds: today feeds the powerful mathematical move of letting one number stand in for something larger — a whole angle captured by a single ratio. This is the seed of measurement itself: temperature standing for warmth, a grade standing for a slope, a tangent standing for a tilt. Learning that a carefully chosen number can carry all the information you need — and can be compared, ordered and reasoned about — trains a mind to look for the right measure of a thing. It also feeds honesty about method: tan θ is opposite ÷ adjacent, in that order, every time — and precision like that is what makes the number trustworthy.

Carry one idea forward: a good measure lets you compare things you could never line up side by side. The tangent lets you compare angles by a single number.

🛠️ Final Task — Evidence for today

Your task: show that you can define and use the tangent ratio.

  • Draw a right-angled triangle, mark an angle θ, label the opposite and adjacent, and work out tan θ = opposite ÷ adjacent. Show your working.
  • Draw a bigger triangle of the same shape and show that tan θ is the same — explain why.
  • Take three slopes given as rise over run, work out each tangent, and order them from gentlest to steepest, explaining how the tangent tells you the order.

Submission choices: an annotated Scratchpad image, a written solution, a labelled poster, or a spoken/video walkthrough.
