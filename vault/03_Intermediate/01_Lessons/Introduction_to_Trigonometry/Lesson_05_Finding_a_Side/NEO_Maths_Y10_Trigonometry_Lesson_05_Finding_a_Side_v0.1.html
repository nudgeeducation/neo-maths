NEO Mathematics: Introduction to Trigonometry

Finding a side

Driving Question: How do I find a length from one side and one angle?

Lesson type: Applying — using a trigonometric ratio to find an unknown side that sits in the numerator (the top of the fraction), so a single multiplication solves it.

Stepping Stone: given one angle and one side, write the ratio as an equation, substitute the numbers, and use algebra to solve for the unknown (e.g. sin θ = x ÷ hypotenuse, so x = hypotenuse × sin θ).

You will need: a calculator in degrees — use the 🧮 Calculator button in the bottom-right of the screen (it is already set to degrees), or your own. A √ Symbols palette sits under each answer box too.

🌿 Connection — Set it up

🔤 The idea for this lesson

There is no new rule to memorise. When you know one angle and one side, you write the ratio as an equation, put in the numbers, and use algebra to solve for the unknown — call it x. It is always the same four lines:

1. Write the ratio:  sin θ = oppositehypotenuse
2. Put in what you know (unknown = x):  sin θ = xhyp
3. Rearrange to get x on its own — this is the algebra
4. Work it out on a calculator (set to degrees).

Line 3 is the important one. Think about the equation, not a formula: if x is divided by a number, multiply both sides by it.  For line 4, open the 🧮 Calculator (bottom-right) — it works in degrees.

What to do: the triangle shows the marked angle, one known side, and the side you want (marked ?). Press sin, cos or tan to set up the ratio, then reveal the working one line at a time and watch the algebra unfold.

What you are trying to notice: every solution is the same four lines — ratio, substitute, rearrange, evaluate. Once you can do the algebra in line 3, you never need to remember a special rule.

🧩 Set it up

Carry this forward: ratio → substitute → rearrange → evaluate. The algebra does the work; there is no rule to memorise.

🚶 Movement — Side Finder

What to do: build the solution line by line. First choose the ratio (that writes lines 1 and 2 for you). Then type the rearrangement yourself — the line that gets x on its own. Finally work out the answer with your calculator (to 1 d.p.). Each line stays on screen below the one before.

What you are trying to notice: you are not remembering a rule — you are solving an equation. Line 2 is ratio = x ÷ known; to get x alone you multiply both sides by the known number. That is the whole of line 3.

📐 Side Finder


Carry this forward: write the ratio, substitute, then do the algebra to make x the subject. Understand the equation and you never need a rule.

🪞 Reflection — Set up or slip?

What to do: each card shows two lines of someone's working — their ratio equation and their rearrangement. Decide whether the working is right or a slip, then check.

What you are trying to notice: two things can go wrong — the wrong ratio (a side that isn't in the problem), and the wrong algebra (dividing when the equation says multiply). When line 2 is ratio = x ÷ known, line 3 must multiply both sides by the known number.

🔎 Set up or slip?

Carry this forward: check two things — is it the right ratio, and (with the unknown in the numerator) is it known × ratio, not known ÷ ratio?

🎨 Creativity — Reach the unmeasurable

What to do: each scenario is a real length you could never reach with a tape measure — the height of a tree, a cliff, a flagpole. You are given a distance and an angle. Set it up and find the height with your calculator.

What you are trying to notice: this is what trigonometry is for. One side you can measure, plus one angle, gives you a length you could never reach — the whole point of the ratios.

🌲 Reach the unmeasurable

Make it your own: on the Scratchpad, invent your own "reach the unmeasurable" problem — a tall thing, a distance and an angle — solve it, and challenge a friend.

🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

Finding a length you could never reach — the top of a tree, the far side of a river — from a single angle and one measurement is a genuinely powerful thing. Take a breath before moving on.

Notice one thing: you did not follow a rule — you wrote an equation and solved it. Did understanding the algebra feel steadier than trying to remember what to do? There is no right answer — just what your own attention noticed.

You can jot it on the Scratchpad, say it aloud, or simply hold it in mind. You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: intellectual. The tree and the cliff are honest problem settings, not the nourishment itself — so we name honestly what the learning feeds.

What it feeds: today feeds the power of indirect measurement — reaching something you cannot touch by reasoning from something you can. This is one of the oldest and most useful moves in all of science: the height of a mountain, the distance to a star, the depth of the sea have all been found this way, without ever going there. Learning that careful reasoning can measure the unreachable builds a quiet confidence that problems which look impossible often have a clever, measurable way in. It also feeds method discipline: a reliable three-step routine you can trust turns a daunting task into a calm one.

Carry one idea forward: when something cannot be measured directly, ask what you can measure — and what links it to what you want.

🛠️ Final Task — Evidence for today

Your task: show that you can find an unknown side from one side and one angle.

  • Take a right-angled triangle with a marked angle and one known side. Write the ratio as an equation, substitute the numbers (unknown = x), rearrange to make x the subject, and evaluate with a calculator. Show all four lines.
  • Do this for all three cases: finding the opposite from the hypotenuse (sine), the adjacent from the hypotenuse (cosine), and the opposite from the adjacent (tangent), explaining the algebra step each time.
  • Solve one reach the unmeasurable problem — a height you could not measure directly, from a distance and an angle of elevation — and explain your method.
