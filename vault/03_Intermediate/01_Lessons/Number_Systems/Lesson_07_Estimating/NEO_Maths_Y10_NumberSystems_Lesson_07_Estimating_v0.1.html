NEO Mathematics: Number Systems and Calculator Judgement

Estimating and judging reasonableness

Driving Question: Is my answer about right?

Lesson type: Core — quick, reliable estimates with powers, roots and decimals, and using them to judge an answer.

Stepping Stone: round to 1 significant figure to estimate calculations with powers, roots, decimals less than 1 and fractions; estimate a square root to 1 decimal place; decide whether an estimate is an overestimate or an underestimate; and judge whether an answer is sensible, giving a reason.

You will need: rounding, square numbers, and estimating before you calculate — and any calculator, if you have one, to check answers at the end. The optional ✏️ Scratchpad is there too.

🌿 Connection — One significant figure

🔤 The idea for this lesson

To estimate, round each number to 1 significant figure — its first non-zero digit — and work out the easier calculation in your head. A good estimate tells you whether an answer is about right. A better one also tells you which way it is out: an overestimate is bigger than the true answer; an underestimate is smaller.

What to do: round each number to 1 significant figure.

What you are trying to notice: the first significant figure is the first digit that is not 0 — wherever it is. 0.0372 rounds to 0.04, and 9.81 rounds to 10.

🎯 Round it

To 1 significant figure:

Carry this forward: find the first non-zero digit, look at the digit after it, and round.

🚶 Movement — The root locator

What to do: find the two whole numbers the square root lies between, using square numbers. Then home in to 1 decimal place by squaring your guesses.

What you are trying to notice: √50 lies between √49 = 7 and √64 = 8, and 50 is much nearer 49 — so √50 is just over 7.

📍 Locate the root

To 1 decimal place:

Carry this forward: squares on either side first; then square a decimal guess to see if it is too big or too small.

🪞 Reflection — The sense detector

What to do: each case shows a calculation and someone's answer. Round each number, build your estimate, and decide: is their answer sensible? Then decide whether your estimate is an overestimate, an underestimate — or whether you cannot be sure. Your decisions build up into a decision trail.

What you are trying to notice: rounding a number up pushes a product up, but rounding a number you divide by up pulls the answer down. Under a √, round to a nearby square number.

    🎨 Creativity — Trap the answer

    What to do: for each number, choose whether to round it down or up. Build one estimate that is definitely too big, and one that is definitely too small. Together they trap the true answer between them.

    What you are trying to notice: to make an answer as big as possible, make the numbers you multiply bigger — and the numbers you divide by smaller.

    🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

    You do not need the exact answer to know a lot about it. Take a slow breath.

    Notice one thing: a quick estimate is like a glance at a map before a journey. You may not know every turn, but you will know if you end up somewhere completely wrong.

    You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

    🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

    Mode: intellectual. The mathematics is the nourishment, so we name honestly what the learning feeds.

    What it feeds: today feeds number sense — a feel for how big things are. It is what lets engineers, nurses and shoppers notice in a moment that a figure is ten times too big. This whole unit has been about the same thing: knowing your numbers well enough to trust them, and to catch them when they go wrong.

    Carry one idea forward: before you trust an answer, ask “about how big should it be?”

    🛠️ Final Task — About right, and why

    Your task: show that you can estimate, locate roots, and judge answers with reasons.

    • Estimate 49.6 × 0.3120.0187, showing each number rounded to 1 significant figure.
    • Estimate √85 to 1 decimal place. Explain how you know it is between 9 and 10.
    • Someone says 7.92² × 0.204 = 127.96. Use an estimate to decide whether this is sensible, and explain.
    • Is 30 × 8 an overestimate or an underestimate of 29.4 × 7.83? Explain using the direction of each rounding.
    • stretch Find an overestimate and an underestimate that trap the value of 47.3 × 6.82.3. Explain why each must be too big or too small.
