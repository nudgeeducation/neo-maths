Union, intersection and complement

What you are trying to notice: every number lands in exactly one of four places: only A, both, only B, or neither.

📍 Place it

🔤 The idea for this lesson

A ∩ B, read “A intersect B”, is the intersection: everything in A and in B. A ∪ B, read “A union B”, is the union: everything in A or B, including what is in both. A′, read “A prime” or “not A”, is the complement: everything in ℰ that is not in A. On a Venn diagram, each one is a choice of regions.

Today's main activity, the Region Lighter, is in Movement.

🚶 Movement — The Region Lighter (today's main activity)

What to do: tap regions of the Venn diagram to light them up, and watch the notation and the listing change. Say each one aloud as you tap: “in A and in B”. Then switch to Shade the card: read the notation and light exactly the right regions.

What you are trying to notice: ∩ lights one region, ∪ lights three, and ′ lights everything outside.

💡 The Region Lighter

Carry this forward: ∩ means and (in both); ∪ means or (in at least one); ′ means not (outside). Light the regions, then list what is in them.

🪞 Reflection — And, or, not

What to do: match each description in words to its notation.

What you are trying to notice: “or” in mathematics includes “both”; “but not” is an intersection with a complement.

🎨 Creativity — Count it right

What to do: set how many elements are only in A, in both, and only in B. Meet each brief, then check.

What you are trying to notice: adding n(A) and n(B) counts the intersection twice.

🍎 Nutrition — What this feeds

Mode: practical. Today the mathematics feeds how you search.

What it feeds: today feeds searching precisely. Search engines, library catalogues and shopping filters use AND, OR and NOT: “AND” narrows a search to the intersection, “OR” widens it to the union, and “NOT” removes a set. Knowing which you need finds what you want faster.

🛠️ Final Task — My two-set diagram

Your task: choose a universal set and two sets inside it (numbers, or something from your life, such as subjects or places).

  • Draw the Venn diagram with every element in its region.
  • List A ∩ B, A ∪ B, A′ and (A ∪ B)′, and shade one of them on a copy of the diagram.
  • Write each one in words as well as in notation.
  • stretch Show that n(A ∪ B) = n(A) + n(B) − n(A ∩ B) for your sets, and explain why it must be true.
