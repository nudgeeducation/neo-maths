NEO Mathematics: Straight Lines

A table can predict a line

Driving Question: How can a few coordinate pairs reveal a whole linear pattern?

Stepping Stone: generate coordinates from a linear rule and recognise the constant rate — the steady step in the y values.

🌿 Connection — Fill the table, then predict the line

🔤 New words for this lesson

Generate coordinates — work out the y value for each x using the rule, giving points (x, y).
First difference — the amount y changes between one row of the table and the next.
Constant rate — when that first difference is the same every time. A constant rate is exactly what makes a line straight.

What to do: use the rule to fill in each y, and check. Notice the constant step in the y column, then predict the line — before you reveal the graph.

What you are trying to notice: a few rows of a table already contain the whole line. The steady step in y is m, and the first value is c — enough to predict everything.

🚶 Movement — Step-to-build the table

What to do: begin at x = 0, where the value is c, then press + step to add one row at a time. Each press adds the same amount — watch the table grow and the point climb the line.

What you are trying to notice: the table is built by adding the same step over and over. That repeated equal step is the constant rate m.

Carry this forward: a straight-line table is just "start, then add the same step, again and again". Miss out the middle and you can still jump straight to any row.

🪞 Reflection — First-difference detective

What to do: look at each table's y values. Decide whether the step is constant (a straight line) or not, then reveal the reasoning.

What you are trying to notice: only a constant first difference makes a straight line. If the step changes, the pattern is not linear — not every rule is a straight line.

🔍 Constant step — or not?

Carry this forward: before trusting a line, check the first differences are equal. Constant step = straight line; changing step = something else.

🎨 Creativity — Predict a mystery line

What to do: you are shown only the first two rows of a mystery table. Work out the step, predict y when x = 4, then reveal the whole line.

What you are trying to notice: two rows are enough. The step between them is m, and the first value is c — so you can predict any row, and the graph.

🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

Generating values and spotting a pattern takes real concentration. Take a breath before moving on.

Notice one thing: did you reach a point where you could jump straight to the answer without filling every row in between? There is no right answer — just what your own attention noticed.

You can jot it on the Scratchpad, say it aloud, or simply hold it in mind. You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — Where does this show up in the real world?

Mode: contextual. Predicting from a steady rate is how people plan ahead.

Why it matters: a phone bill that rises by the same amount each gigabyte, a car's distance at a steady speed, savings growing by a fixed sum each week, a recipe scaled up — all let you predict far ahead from just a couple of values, because the step is constant. Spotting whether a rate really is constant (or is speeding up, like compound interest) is a genuinely useful life skill: it tells you when a simple straight-line prediction can be trusted, and when it cannot.

Next time something grows "by the same amount each time", you already know its whole future — that is a straight line.

🛠️ Final Task — Evidence for today

Your task: generate, predict and check.

  • Before drawing, predict: does the line go up or down, and where does it cross the y-axis? Then plot the points and check your prediction.
