🌿 Connection — The gradient is the rate

🔤 New words for this lesson

Gradient (m) — how steep a line is: how much y changes each time x goes up by 1.
Rate of change — another name for the gradient: the steady rate at which y changes as x increases.
Positive / negative / zero gradient — a rising line (m > 0), a falling line (m < 0), or a flat line (m = 0).

What to do: change m and read the table beside the line. Notice how much y jumps each time x goes up by 1.

What you are trying to notice: the gradient m is the constant step you met in the table — the amount y changes for every 1 across. Gradient and "rate of change" are the same thing.

🚶 Movement — Pivot the gradient

What to do: the intercept c is locked. Drag m and watch the line pivot about its crossing point. Notice the little step: 1 across, then m up.

What you are trying to notice: changing m tilts the line without moving where it crosses. A bigger m is steeper; a negative m falls; m = 0 is flat.

🪞 Reflection — Read and compare gradients

What to do: each card shows a line or two. Decide whether it rises, falls or is flat — or which of two lines is steeper — then check.

What you are trying to notice: the sign of m tells you the direction, and the size of m tells you the steepness — whatever the intercept.

🔎 Rising, falling, or steeper?

Carry this forward: read a gradient in two parts — its sign (up, down or flat) and its size (how steep). Both come straight from m.

🎨 Creativity — Model a real rate

What to do: pick a real situation with a steady rate. Set the m slider so the line changes at the right rate, then check.

What you are trying to notice: a real rate — growing, spending, draining — is exactly a gradient. A rate that goes down is a negative gradient.

🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

Feeling how one number controls a line's whole steepness and direction is a real shift in thinking. Take a breath before moving on.

Notice one thing: what did a negative gradient feel like when you first saw the line fall? There is no right answer — just what your own attention noticed.

You can jot it on the Scratchpad, say it aloud, or simply hold it in mind. You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — Where does this show up in the real world?

Mode: contextual. A gradient is a rate — and rates run the world.

Why it matters: speed is a gradient (distance per hour), a wage is a gradient (pay per hour), a phone tariff's cost per gigabyte, the price per litre at a fuel pump, a medicine dose per kilogram of body weight — all gradients. A steeper line means a faster rate; a negative gradient means something falling — a battery draining, a balance dropping, a temperature cooling. Reading how steep a line is, and which way it leans, tells you how fast something is changing and whether it is growing or shrinking.

Next time you see the word "per" — miles per hour, cost per item — you are looking at a gradient.

🛠️ Final Task — Evidence for today

Your task: pin down the gradient.

  • Draw three lines through the same point but with different gradients — one steep, one shallow, and one with a negative gradient (falling). Label each with its m.
  • For one line, show the "1 across, m up" step, and explain how it shows the rate of change.
  • Describe a real situation with a negative rate, and explain why its gradient is negative.
