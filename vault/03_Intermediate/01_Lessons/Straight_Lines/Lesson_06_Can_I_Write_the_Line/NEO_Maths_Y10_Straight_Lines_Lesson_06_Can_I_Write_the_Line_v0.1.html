NEO Mathematics: Straight Lines

Can I write the line?

Driving Question: How do the gradient and the intercept become an equation?

Lesson type: Synthesising — combining the intercept c and the gradient m into the equation y = mx + c.

Stepping Stone: write the equation of a line from clues — read c (the y-intercept) and m (the gradient), then assemble y = mx + c.

NEO Cornerstones: Connection · Movement · Reflection · Creativity · Rest · Nutrition

🌿 Connection — Line Detective

🔤 New words for this lesson

y = mx + c — the equation of a straight line. Just two numbers control it: m and c.
Gradient (m) — the steepness and direction: how much y changes for every 1 across.
y-intercept (c) — where the line crosses the y-axis: the value of y when x = 0.

What to do: you are the detective. Read the line's two clues — where it crosses the y-axis (that is c) and its gradient (that is m) — then write them into the equation. Press Reveal to check, and Next case for a new line.

What you are trying to notice: every straight line is pinned down by just those two numbers. Find c, find m, and the equation y = mx + c writes itself.

🕵️ Line Detective

Carry this forward: to write a line, you need exactly two things — its y-intercept c and its gradient m. Together they are the equation.

🚶 Movement — Equation Builder

What to do: a faint target line is waiting. Move the c slider to set where your line crosses, and the m slider to set its tilt, until your line lands exactly on the target. Watch the equation build itself as you go.

What you are trying to notice: c slides the line up and down; m tilts it. You need both to pin a line down — and once you do, the two numbers are the equation y = mx + c.

🎛️ Equation Builder

Carry this forward: setting c and m is the same as writing y = mx + c. The equation is just the two dials, recorded.

🪞 Reflection — Right or slip?

What to do: each card shows a line and the equation someone wrote for it. Decide whether the equation is right or a slip, then check.

What you are trying to notice: the two classic slips are m and c swapped (putting the intercept where the gradient goes) and a lost sign on a negative gradient or a negative intercept. Gradient multiplies x; the intercept stands alone.

🔎 Right or slip?

Carry this forward: in y = mx + c, m is the number multiplying x (the gradient) and c stands alone (the intercept) — and both keep their signs.

🎨 Creativity — Write a real line

What to do: each situation has a starting value and a steady rate. Turn the start into c, the rate into m, and write the equation y = mx + c. Type it in and check.

What you are trying to notice: the intercept is where the story begins (the amount when x = 0), and the gradient is the rate it changes by. A cost that goes down each step gives a negative gradient.

🌍 Write a Real Line

Make it your own: on the Scratchpad, invent a situation with a starting amount and a steady rate, and write its equation — is the gradient positive or negative?

🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

Bringing the two dials together into a single equation — realising that m and c are all a line needs — is a real shift in thinking. Take a breath before moving on.

Notice one thing: when you matched the target in the Equation Builder, which dial did you reach for first — the tilt or the crossing point? There is no right answer — just what your own attention noticed.

You can jot it on the Scratchpad, say it aloud, or simply hold it in mind. You can pause here and return whenever you are ready.

🍎 Nutrition — Where does this show up in the real world?

Mode: contextual. Most everyday prices and plans are a line you can write down.

Why it matters: a taxi with a flag fee then a rate per mile, a gym with a joining fee then a monthly charge, a phone plan with a fixed line rental plus a cost per gigabyte, a tradesperson's call-out charge plus an hourly rate — every one of these is y = mx + c. The c is the fixed part you pay just to start; the m is the rate that stacks up with use. Writing the equation lets you predict any total, compare two deals, and see exactly when one becomes cheaper than another — which is the whole point of the next lessons on parallel lines and where two lines meet.

Next time you see "a fixed fee plus so much per…", you are looking at c and m — a line waiting to be written.

🛠️ Final Task — Evidence for today

Your task: write the line.

  • Take a line from a graph — read its y-intercept (c) and its gradient (m), and write its equation y = mx + c. Show how you found each number.
  • Write the equation for a line with a negative gradient and a negative intercept, and draw it to check it matches.
  • Turn a real situation — a starting value plus a steady rate — into an equation, and say what m and c mean in that story.

Submission choices: an annotated Scratchpad image, a written solution, a labelled poster, or a spoken/video walkthrough.
