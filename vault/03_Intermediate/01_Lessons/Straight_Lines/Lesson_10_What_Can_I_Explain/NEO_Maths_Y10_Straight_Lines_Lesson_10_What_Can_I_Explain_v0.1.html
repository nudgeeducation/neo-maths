🌿 Connection — The whole story of a line

🔑 The big ideas of this unit

Two numbers — every straight line is fixed by its gradient m and its intercept c: y = mx + c.
Four faces — the same line can be shown as an equation, a graph, a table, and a story in words.
Lines together — parallel lines share a gradient and never meet; lines with different gradients cross; and a line can act as a converter.

What to do: look at one line told four ways at once. Check that the equation, the graph, the table and the story all describe the very same line. Press Another line to see a new one.

What you are trying to notice: these are not four topics — they are four views of one thing. Read m and c in each, and you can move between them at will.

🪞 Reflection — Where does the model break?

What to do: each card shows a real situation modelled by a straight line. The maths of the line is perfect — but the real thing has limits. Decide where the model stops making sense, then check.

What you are trying to notice: a line can be exactly right as maths and still be a poor model of reality. Real quantities can't go below zero, can't grow forever, or only follow the line within a certain range. A good modeller knows where their line stops being trustworthy.

⚠️ Where does the model break?

Carry this forward: a straight line is a model, not the real thing. Always ask: over what range does it make sense — and where does reality stop following the line?

🎨 Creativity — A line that tells a human story

What to do: pick a real situation. First write its equation (turn the starting value into c and the rate into m). Then say where the model runs out — the value of x at which the real thing hits a limit.

What you are trying to notice: a good model is two things — the right line, and an honest sense of where it stops. Writing the equation shows the maths; naming the limit shows you understand the reality.

🌙 Rest — A pause to let it settle

You began this unit seeing a line as a mysterious rule. You end it able to read, write, compare and question one. Take a breath, and notice how far that is.

Notice one thing: of everything in this unit — the gradient, the intercept, writing the equation, parallel lines, crossings, conversions, and the limits of a model — which idea feels most solid to you now, and which would you still like to revisit? There is no right answer.

🍎 Nutrition — Where does this show up in the real world?

Mode: contextual. Straight-line thinking is everywhere — and so is knowing its limits.

Why it matters: across this unit you have modelled taxis, gyms, phone plans, savings, temperatures and currencies with a single idea — a rate and a starting value. That same linear thinking underpins budgeting, pricing, wages, dosages and speeds. But the deepest skill you have gained is judgement: knowing that a straight line is a model, powerful but never the whole truth. Real batteries stop at 0%, real plants stop growing, real deals only apply within a range. Scientists, engineers and economists all use straight-line models precisely because they know where those models break. Being able to write the line and say where it stops being trustworthy is what turns mathematics into good decisions.

Next time you see a straight-line prediction, ask the modeller's question: over what range does this actually hold?

🛠️ Final Task — Evidence for today

Your task (portfolio): tell the whole story of a line — and be honest about its limits.

  • Choose a real situation with a starting value and a steady rate. Show it four ways: its equation y = mx + c, its graph, a small table, and a sentence of story. Say what m and c mean in your situation.
  • Bring in the relationships: write a line parallel to yours (and say what that would mean), or a second option that crosses yours (and read the break-even).
  • Finish with the modeller's honesty: state where your line stops making sense — the range over which it is trustworthy, and what happens in reality beyond it.

Submission choices: an annotated Scratchpad image, a written solution, a labelled poster, or a spoken/video walkthrough.
