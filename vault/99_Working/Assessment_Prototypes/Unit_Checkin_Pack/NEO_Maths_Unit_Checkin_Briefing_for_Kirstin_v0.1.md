# End-of-Unit Assessment: a Proposal for Discussion

**To:** Kirstin Stevens, Director of NEO · **From:** Gerry Docherty, curriculum lead · **Date:** 3 November 2026
**For:** the November milestone, "Spring term lessons … with end-of-unit assessments"

## The proposal in one paragraph
Each unit already opens with a diagnostic expedition that records where every learner starts. We propose that each unit closes with a **Unit Check-in**: the learner returns to the same expedition world, now answering questions on each lesson's own idea. The learner says how each idea feels first, then tries it. The result is a simple picture of **where they started and where they are now**, in their own words. Their supporting adult adds one overall label using our four Proficiency descriptors. The same page goes to the learner, the adult and the family. Commissioners receive a one-page termly summary. There are no scores, timers or rankings anywhere.

## How it serves each stakeholder

| Stakeholder | What they need | How the Unit Check-in meets it |
|---|---|---|
| Learners | To see their growth, know what's next, and feel safe | A familiar world, no clock, no marks; a side passage on every floor; "not yet" leads straight to next steps; they can come back and try again |
| Supporting adults | A clear picture with no paperwork | Outcomes recorded automatically; a short note on any common slip; one label to choose; optional conversation cards |
| Families | Plain language, and how to help | A one-page family summary with the learner's own words and ideas for home |
| Commissioners | Credible progress from a baseline, linked to the iGCSE | Start-of-unit and end-of-unit evidence for every learner, in the same format across the curriculum, summarised termly with iGCSE coverage |

## The five parts
1. **The Return Expedition:** the core evidence. Untimed, can be paused, taken in a window the learner chooses.
2. **The learner's reflection:** how each idea feels, asked before each question, plus an optional "looking back" comment.
3. **The adult's confirmation:** one of Approaching Proficiency, Developing Proficiency, Proficiency or Refining Proficiency, informed by the Final Tasks, with an optional maths conversation.
4. **The Unit Summary:** one page for learner, adult and family alike.
5. **The Exam Corner (Intermediate):** optional exam-style practice at the end, never recorded, to build exam familiarity gradually.

## What is in this pack
- **Prototype:** Return to the Lighthouse, the check-in for Algebraic Expressions. It works end to end on any computer and saves only on that device. Open it, step inside, and try a few floors, including a wrong answer and the side passage. Then see the floor plan, the adult view, the confirmation and the Exam Corner.
- **Exemplars** (using Alex, a fictional learner): the Unit Summary, the family summary, the maths conversation cards and the termly commissioner summary.
- **Draft GreenPrint entry v2.5**, which would record the decision once agreed.

## Decisions we need
1. Is the blended **Unit Check-in** the right approach?
2. **Commissioners:** is a termly one-page summary right, or do they expect a set format or frequency?
3. **Families:** an online summary, a printed one, or both?
4. **Exam Corner:** Intermediate only? From when?
5. **Recording:** the check-in would save to the results service, like the diagnostics, and appear in the learner passport and educator dashboard. Are you happy for the adult's label and one-line note to be stored there too?

## If agreed: next steps and effort
- Connect the check-in to the results service (passport and dashboard), as we did for the diagnostics.
- Build a check-in for each Spring HT1 unit: Return to the Walled Garden, the Workshop, the Observatory and the Lighthouse. Each is quicker to build than a diagnostic, because it reuses that unit's world, field kit and results page.
- From then on, every new unit is planned with both its diagnostic and its check-in.
- Add a "Finish here" post to each Classroom unit, alongside the "Start here" post.
