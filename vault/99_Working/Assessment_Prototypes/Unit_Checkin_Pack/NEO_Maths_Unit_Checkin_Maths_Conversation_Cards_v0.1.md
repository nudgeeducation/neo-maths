# Maths Conversation Cards: Algebraic Expressions and Identities

**NEO Mathematics · Intermediate · Unit Check-in · Exemplar v0.1 · 3 November 2026 · for discussion**

## How to use these cards
A maths conversation is a short, relaxed chat between a learner and their supporting adult. Use it when the Return expedition doesn't show what a learner really knows: for example, when they felt sure but the answer didn't work out ("Worth a second look"), when reading or typing is a barrier, or when a learner would simply rather talk.

- **Five to ten minutes, one or two cards.** Let the learner choose the card, or start with a floor from their next steps.
- **No writing is needed from the learner.** They can talk, point, sketch on the Scratchpad or use their field kit.
- **Listen more than you ask.** The prompts are there to open the conversation, not to be read in order.
- **Never give the answer to "check" them.** If they get stuck, say "Let's look at that together in the lesson" and move on.
- **Record one line only**, in the learner's own words where possible. It goes on the Unit Summary, which the learner sees.

## Card 1 · Expressions, equations, formulae and identities
- **Show me:** "Sort these: 2*x* + 3 = 11, 2(*x* + 3) = 2*x* + 6, *A* = *lw*, 3*x* + 2. Which is which?"
- **Why:** "How can you tell an identity from an equation?"
- **What if:** "Can you make up an identity of your own?"
- **Listen for:** an identity is true for every value; an equation only for some.

## Card 2 · Expanding and factorising
- **Show me:** "Factorise 6*x*² + 9*x*. How do you know you've finished?"
- **Why:** "Why does expanding your answer check it?"
- **What if:** "What if it were 6*x*² + 9? What changes?"
- **Listen for:** the highest common factor, letters as well as numbers.

## Card 3 · Expanding three brackets
- **Show me:** "Talk me through (*x* + 1)(*x* + 2)(*x* − 3). Where would you start?"
- **Why:** "Why do two of the terms cancel?"
- **What if:** "How many terms could you get before collecting?"
- **Listen for:** two brackets first, then a grid for the third; careful signs.

## Card 4 · Simplifying algebraic fractions
- **Show me:** "Simplify (*x*² + 5*x* + 6)/(*x* + 3)."
- **Why:** "Why can't you cancel the 6 with the 3?"
- **What if:** "Is there any value of *x* this doesn't work for?"
- **Listen for:** only factors cancel, never separate terms.

## Card 5 · Adding and subtracting algebraic fractions
- **Show me:** "Add 1/*x* and 2/(*x* + 1). What do they need first?"
- **Why:** "How is this like adding 1/2 and 1/3?"
- **What if:** "What happens if you just add the numerators and the denominators? Try it with numbers."
- **Listen for:** a common denominator, and scaling each numerator.

## Card 6 · Changing the subject, harder cases
- **Show me:** "Make *x* the subject of *y* = (*x* + 2)/(*x* − 1)."
- **Why:** "Why do you need to get all the *x* terms on one side?"
- **What if:** "How could you check your new formula with a number?"
- **Listen for:** clearing the fraction, collecting *x* terms, factorising.

## Card 7 · Algebraic proof
- **Show me:** "Convince me the sum of three consecutive whole numbers is always a multiple of 3."
- **Why:** "Why isn't one example enough?"
- **What if:** "Is the sum of four consecutive numbers always a multiple of 4? How would you find out?"
- **Listen for:** general algebra (*n*, *n* + 1, *n* + 2) and a concluding sentence.

## Card 8 · Function notation, domain and range
- **Show me:** "f(*x*) = 1/(*x* − 4). What can't you feed this machine, and why?"
- **Why:** "What does f(3) mean, in your own words?"
- **What if:** "Could you design a machine that can't take the number 7?"
- **Listen for:** you cannot divide by zero; f(□) means put □ where *x* was.

## Recording the conversation
One line on the Unit Summary, for example:

**Example (Alex, a fictional learner):** Card 5, about 7 minutes. Alex explained the common denominator clearly using 1/2 + 1/3, then applied it to 1/*x* + 2/(*x* + 1) with one prompt. In Alex's words: "It's the same as normal fractions, just with letters."

No marks, no ticks, no timings beyond a rough length.
