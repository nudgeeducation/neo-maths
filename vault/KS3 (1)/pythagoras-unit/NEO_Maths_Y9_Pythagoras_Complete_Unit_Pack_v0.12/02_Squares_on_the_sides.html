Session 2 · Squares on the sides

Driving Question: What might the areas of three squares tell us about one triangle?

Primary Cornerstone: Creativity · Secondary: Connection, Movement

🌱 Open Learner Guide 📘 Open Supporting Adult Guidance 🧭 Unit Route
🌿 Connection — What mathematical structure matters today?

The theorem begins as an area relationship. The two smaller squares combine to match the area of the square on the hypotenuse.

Stepping Stone focus: notice and describe the area relationship between the squares constructed on the three sides of a right-angled triangle.

🚶 Movement — How does the mathematical idea change, move or unfold?

📐 Dynamic Squares on a Right Triangle

Drag the blue or green point to change the right-angled triangle. Drag anywhere else on the completed construction to move the whole triangle-and-squares shape around the workspace. Watch what happens to the areas of the three squares.

Inquiry route: Observe → Compare → Drag → Repeat → Write a statement.

Area measurements

What do you notice?

First, compare the three areas without revealing a calculation.

Change the triangle

Try at least four different triangles. Does the same area relationship survive?

Build your conjecture

Complete the statement in your own words before any formal theorem is named.

🎨 Creativity — How can I make or represent the idea in my own way?

Use colour to show the two smaller square areas as a pair. Add a Sticky Note with your conjecture before revealing the total.

You may draw, annotate, type, speak to a Supporting Adult or use a Sticky Note.

🪞 Reflection — What changed in my mathematical thinking?

Pause and notice what your thinking is doing, not only whether you reached an answer.

Reflection prompt: What changed in your understanding of notice and describe the area relationship between the squares constructed on the three sides of a right-angled triangle during this session?

Mastery reflection: Can you now explain how the two smaller square areas compare with the square area on the hypotenuse?

You may respond with a sentence, a sketch, a Sticky Note, a spoken explanation or an annotated Scratchpad image.

🌙 Rest — What helps me pause, regulate and return?

Do not memorise the formula yet. Look at the three areas and ask what combines.

A calm visual route can reduce the pressure to remember symbols before they have meaning.

Regulation choice: close other panels, use one colour, use paper, pause, or return later.

🥣 Nutrition — What sustains the learning?

Many mathematical ideas became powerful because people found ways to represent invisible relationships visually. Here, areas make a hidden length relationship visible.
