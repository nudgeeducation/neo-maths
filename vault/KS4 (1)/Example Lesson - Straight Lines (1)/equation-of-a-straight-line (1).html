🌿 Connection — How are the equation, table and graph connected?

The structure of a straight line

Many straight-line graphs can be written in the form:

y = mx + c

  • m is the gradient. It tells us how much y changes when x increases by 1.
  • c is the y-intercept. It tells us where the line crosses the y-axis.

A line is a relationship. It connects every x-value to a y-value. A table shows some of the pairs. A graph shows the whole relationship visually.

Quick connection check

For the equation y = 2x + 3:

  • The gradient is 2.
  • The y-intercept is 3.
  • The graph crosses the y-axis at (0, 3).
  • Each time x increases by 1, y increases by 2.

🌙 Rest — Where is the pause?

Calm graph-reading routine

When a graph or equation feels crowded, pause and use this routine:

  1. Find the equation form: y = mx + c.
  2. Identify c: where the line starts on the y-axis.
  3. Identify m: how much y changes for each 1 step in x.
  4. Make a small table of values.
  5. Plot carefully or interpret the story.
  6. Check whether your answer makes sense.

Helpful self-talk: “I do not need to understand the whole graph at once. I can find the start, then find the change.”

🥣 Nutrition — How can linear models support real choices?

Lines and life decisions

Straight-line graphs can model real situations: saving money, food costs, travel, energy use, phone contracts and event planning. The gradient often shows the ongoing rate. The intercept often shows a starting value or fixed cost.

Mini inquiry

A community lunch has a fixed room cost and then a cost per person for food.

Total cost = cost per person × number of people + room cost

In this model:

  • The gradient is the cost per person.
  • The intercept is the room cost.
  • The graph helps organisers predict and plan.

Reflection sentence: “A straight-line model helps with planning because…”
